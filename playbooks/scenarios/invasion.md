## Right now

**Missile strikes come first, and they hit infrastructure.** The register's planning case is "a combination of conventional missiles and cyber operations" against infrastructure, with civilian deaths even though population centres are not the target ([NRR 2025, p. 184](doc:nrr-2025#page=184)). Stay away from power stations, substations, fuel depots, ports, airfields, barracks, bridges and communication masts; they are on the map ([military and airfield overlays](map:?overlay=military&overlay=airports&overlay=rail)).

**On a warning or an explosion:** get inside, to a ground-floor or basement room with no windows, or a corridor with two walls between you and the outside, and lie down; the Protect and Survive fall-out room is also the best blast room in an ordinary house ([Protect and Survive, p. 5](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=5)). Glass kills: stay away from windows and draw curtains ([The Blitz](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/The_Blitz)). Do not go out to look, do not film, do not drive towards it.

**Casualties:** pressure, packing and a tourniquet for limbs ([Severe bleeding card](card:severe-bleeding); [FM 4-25.11, p. 41](doc:fm-4-25-11-first-aid#page=41)); do not touch unexploded ordnance or debris that may hide it ([Unexploded ordnance](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Unexploded_ordnance)).

**Listen.** Emergency Alerts, BBC radio and the police tell you whether to stay or move ([Prepare, alerts](kiwix:prepare_uk/prepare.campaign.gov.uk/get-prepared-for-emergencies/alerts/)); the register's standing instruction is not to delay an evacuation you are told to make ([NRR 2025, p. 22](doc:nrr-2025#page=22)).

## First 72 hours

- **Stay or go.** The state's plan for this scenario is to fight, not to move the population; staying put in a prepared house is the default unless you are next to a target or told to leave ([Evacuation module](module:evacuation)). In 1939 the government evacuated children from cities by organised trains, not by families driving ([Evacuations of civilians in the United Kingdom during the Second World War](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Evacuations_of_civilians_in_the_United_Kingdom_during_the_Second_World_War)); roads jam and become targets.
- **Grab bags and documents.** Passports, birth certificates, prescriptions, insurance, cash, photographs of the house and its contents; a copy of this box's address card ([Household plan](page:household-plan)).
- **Grid, water, telecoms** fail early because they are the targets ([NRR 2025, p. 184](doc:nrr-2025#page=184)); run the [grid collapse playbook](playbook:grid-collapse) alongside this one.
- **Communications.** Mobile networks may be jammed or shut down; PMR446 and FM radio work locally ([PMR446 channels](page:pmr446)). Licensed amateurs may pass emergency traffic ([Ofcom licence, p. 3](doc:ofcom-amateur-licence-2024#page=3)), but Ofcom can order amateur stations off the air by notice once a state of emergency is declared ([Ofcom licence, p. 8](doc:ofcom-amateur-licence-2024#page=8)), so listen for that notice and obey it.
- **Drones.** Small armed drones are flown against vehicles and people in the open, a bus included ([Loitering munition](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Loitering_munition)); during strikes stay inside, do not gather in the street or on roofs, and treat a downed drone as unexploded ordnance: mark it from a distance and report it.
- **Fuel:** half a tank as a rule, and petrol stored only within the lawful limit and outside the house (see [vehicles and fuel](module:vehicles-fuel)).
- **Mines and cluster munitions.** Anything on the ground that looks like a tin, a ball or a small parachute is left alone, marked from a distance and reported by any route ([Unexploded ordnance](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Unexploded_ordnance)).

## First month

- **Blast and ballistic injuries** are surgical problems; the field manual in this box is the reference for what can and cannot be done outside a hospital, and its triage rules decide who is treated first when there are more casualties than hands ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 38](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=38)).
- **Occupation.** If your area comes under the control of a hostile force, the Fourth Geneva Convention still binds it: civilians may not be deported, taken hostage or collectively punished, and must be allowed food and medical care ([Fourth Geneva Convention](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Fourth_Geneva_Convention)). Civilians who take up arms lose that protection. This box carries no tactical material and this playbook gives no advice on resistance: it is about keeping the household alive and lawful.
- **Checkpoints, papers and curfews.** An occupying power may register the population, demand identity papers and impose curfews, and the law of occupation obliges it to protect civilians who comply ([Military occupation](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Military_occupation); [Fourth Geneva Convention](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Fourth_Geneva_Convention)). Whoever holds the road, approach a checkpoint slowly with hands visible and interior lights on at night, have documents ready, answer questions, do not film, do not argue, and be home before a curfew starts: a curfew is enforced at the minute, not at the intent.
- **The Channel Islands 1940 to 1945** are the British precedent: registration, curfews, requisitioned houses, deportations, and by the winter of 1944 near-starvation until the Red Cross ship Vega arrived ([German occupation of the Channel Islands](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/German_occupation_of_the_Channel_Islands)). The lesson is food: a garden, hens, stored staples and a community that shares.
- **Displacement and separated families.** Register with the council or the rest centre wherever you end up; keep the family together; a person who has fled inside their own country is an internally displaced person with the same rights as anyone else ([Internally displaced person](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Internally_displaced_person)). If you lose someone, the Red Cross's Restoring Family Links service traces missing people, carries short family messages and registers separated children, free of charge and across front lines, through the [British Red Cross](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/British_Red_Cross) ([Restoring Family Links](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Restoring_Family_Links)); register yourself as safe with it as well as asking after others.
- **Mental health:** children and adults under shelling need routine, honest answers and sleep; the Prepare trauma advice is written for exactly this ([Coping with trauma](kiwix:prepare_uk/prepare.campaign.gov.uk/coping-with-trauma/)).

## Long term

The register expects a war involving a NATO ally to produce "impacts lasting several years", severed economic ties and severe disruption to gas supplies ([NRR 2025, p. 183](doc:nrr-2025#page=183)), and a wave of British nationals returning from abroad over three to four weeks ([NRR 2025, p. 177](doc:nrr-2025#page=177)). The Blitz lasted eight months and killed about 43,000 civilians; life continued around it ([The Blitz](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/The_Blitz)). Plan for years of the [supply chain playbook](playbook:supply-chain) and, if the state's capacity is lost, the [long rebuild playbook](playbook:long-rebuild).

## UK specifics

- **Emergency powers** are those of the Civil Contingencies Act 2004, summarised in the [security and the law module](module:security-law): there is no "martial law" in UK law.
- **Weapons.** The knife, firearm and offensive-weapon laws do not change with a war; carrying a weapon "for defence" remains an offence ([Knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law)). Possessing material useful to terrorism is an offence under the Terrorism Act 2000 s58, which is why this library carries medical and survival manuals and nothing tactical ([Terrorism Act 2000 s58](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/11/section/58)).
- **Targets in Britain** (the naval bases, the submarine yards, the airfields, the fuel depots and grid supply points) are listed in the [nuclear war playbook](playbook:nuclear-war) and shown on the map ([military and airfield overlays](map:?overlay=military&overlay=airports&overlay=fuel)).
- **Routes:** footpaths, bridleways and byways off the main roads ([Footpaths overlay](map:?overlay=footpaths)), and Scotland's right of responsible access on foot ([Scottish Outdoor Access Code](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Scottish_Outdoor_Access_Code); [Rights of way in England and Wales](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rights_of_way_in_England_and_Wales)).
- {{#if phones}}**Numbers**: [[call 999]] while it answers; the LRF list gives who runs the local response ([LRF contacts](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/guidance/local-resilience-forums-contact-details)).{{else}}**Numbers**: nothing answers with the network down, so an emergency is reported in person at a fire or police station, a rest centre or to the council's wardens ([getting help without phones](page:no-phones); [LRF contacts](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/guidance/local-resilience-forums-contact-details)).{{/if}}

## Checklist

- [ ] Everyone into the blast room and lying down: no windows, two walls from outside, ground floor or basement {#blast-room now}
- [ ] Away from substations, depots, ports, airfields, barracks, bridges and masts; the route round them found on the map {#targets-mapped now}
- [ ] Radio and Emergency Alerts on: they say stay or go, and an evacuation you are told to make is not delayed {#evacuate-when-told now}
- [ ] Bleeding kit out: pressure dressings, packing gauze, a tourniquet, gloves {#bleeding-kit now}
- [ ] Grab bag by the door: documents, medicines, cash, photos of the house, the box's address card {#grab-bag-documents hour}
- [ ] Ordnance rule taught to children: do not touch, mark it, tell an adult {#uxo-rule hour}
- [ ] PMR446 channel and check-in times agreed with the street {#street-radio hour}
- [ ] Routine, sleep and honest answers for children every day {#children-routine today}
- [ ] Household registered with the council or rest centre if displaced {#register-if-displaced today}
- [ ] Two weeks of food, a garden plan and seed; hens if you can keep them {#food-for-the-long-haul week}
