## Right now

**If you see a flash, do not look at it.** Drop flat behind anything solid, face down, eyes shut, mouth open, hands under your body. The light arrives first; the blast wave follows seconds to a minute later, and it is the blast and flying glass that injure most people outside the fireball ([FEMA, p. 31](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=31)). Flash blindness is temporary but can last up to 30 minutes at night and happen 24 km from the burst ([FEMA, p. 31](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=31)); do not drive. How far the blast and heat reach is under UK specifics.

**Then get inside and stay inside.** Fallout starts to come down where the wind carries it within minutes to hours of the burst ([Nuclear fallout](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Nuclear_fallout)). Go into the nearest solid building, to a basement or the middle of the ground floor, away from windows and outside walls, and stay there ([FEMA, p. 74](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=74)).

**Caught outdoors, or in a car far from home:** if you can reach home in a couple of minutes, go; if not, take cover where you are or in the nearest building; if there is no building within a couple of minutes, use any cover or lie flat in a ditch with the skin of head and hands covered, and if no blast wave has come after ten minutes, move to the nearest building ([Protect and Survive, p. 11](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=11)). A car or a tent is not a shelter: leave it for a building as soon as you safely can, and do not drive home through fallout to shelter there ([UKHSA, what to do](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/radiation-emergencies-information-for-the-public/what-to-do-in-a-radiation-emergency); [FEMA, p. 74](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=74)). While fallout is still falling on you, do not strip: dust yourself off without breathing the dust, and take the outer layer off only once you are under cover ([FEMA, p. 116](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=116)).

**If you were outside when fallout fell:** outer clothes off and bagged at the door, then wash; the steps are in the [radiation module](module:radiation) below. **Do not take iodine tablets unless told to** ([NRPB, p. 8](doc:nrpb-stable-iodine#page=8)); the module explains why.

There is no national siren network; a few licensed nuclear and chemical sites have local sirens and loudhailers under REPPIR and COMAH, and the Emergency Alerts phone system is the national warning, backed by radio and television ([Prepare, alerts](kiwix:prepare_uk/prepare.campaign.gov.uk/get-prepared-for-emergencies/alerts/); [REPPIR 2019](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2019/703/contents)).

## First 72 hours

**Stay in for at least 48 hours, then reassess with the radio, not by looking outside.** "The danger from fall-out is greatest in the first forty-eight hours. During that time you must stay in the fall-out room and as far as possible within your inner refuge" ([Protect and Survive, p. 15](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=15)). The [radiation module](module:radiation) explains why the dose rate falls so fast (the 7:10 rule). FEMA's floor is no self-evacuation before 24 hours unless told, and 48 hours is the sensible household minimum because you will not know how heavy your local fallout is ([FEMA, p. 74](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=74)).

**Build the inner refuge in the first hours** if you did not before: a table or door-and-earth lean-to in the corner of the fall-out room furthest from outside walls, packed round with boxes of earth, books, furniture and full water containers; block the windows ([Protect and Survive, p. 5](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=5)). The halving thicknesses for earth, concrete, water and wood are in the [radiation module](module:radiation). In a block of flats of five storeys or more, do not use the top two floors; in a bungalow, shelter with a neighbour if you can ([Protect and Survive, p. 5](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=5)).

**Water and food.** Use only water that was covered or in pipes and tanks before the fallout came; you cannot boil radioactivity out of water ([Protect and Survive, p. 7](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=7)). Sealed and tinned food is safe; wipe the tins before opening. Eat perishables first.

**A lidded bucket is the toilet**, with plastic bag liners and a second bin for refuse outside the room ([Protect and Survive, p. 14](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=14)).

**Fire.** Turn off the gas at the meter, damp down open fires, keep a bucket of water and sand by the door; the thermal flash sets curtains and rubbish alight through windows ([Protect and Survive, p. 10](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=10)).

**Pets in the fall-out room.** Bring them in before the fallout arrives; do not go out to look for one that is missing ([UKHSA, what to do](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/radiation-emergencies-information-for-the-public/what-to-do-in-a-radiation-emergency)). An animal that was out in the fallout is washed with soap or shampoo and water before anyone handles it, with gloves and a mask if you have them, or kept apart from people if it cannot be washed; once in, it stays in ([UKHSA, what to do](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/radiation-emergencies-information-for-the-public/what-to-do-in-a-radiation-emergency)). Long-haired animals are harder to clean than people because there is no outer layer to take off ([FEMA, p. 117](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=117)). Feed and water them from the store, and give them a lined box or tray in the room.

**Essential trips out** in the first days should be minutes long and done by adults over 40, whose thyroid risk from radioiodine is negligible ([NRPB, p. 16](doc:nrpb-stable-iodine#page=16)); keep separate shoes and outer clothes for outside and take them off at the door ([Protect and Survive, p. 13](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=13)).

**Radiation sickness.** Vomiting within an hour of exposure means a severe dose; write down the times; rest, fluids and cleanliness are the treatment you can give ([Radiation sickness card](card:radiation-sickness)).

## First month

Expect up to two weeks confined in the worst-affected areas ([Protect and Survive, p. 4](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=4)). Listen for instructions on when and where evacuation is organised; leave only by the route given, because the fallout pattern is uneven and a wrong turn can take you into a worse area ([FEMA, p. 33](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=33)).

No fresh milk, leafy vegetables or anything grown in the open until it is declared safe, and wash and peel root vegetables; milk is the main food route for radioiodine, as the [radiation module](module:radiation) explains.

Deaths: place the body in another room, wrapped and labelled; if no instructions have come within five days, bury it temporarily as soon as it is safe to go out and mark the spot ([Protect and Survive, p. 13](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=13)); the practicalities and the grief are on [death and grief](page:death-and-grief). Infection is the main killer of people who received a moderate dose, in the weeks when their blood counts fall; keep every wound clean and keep them away from crowds ([Acute radiation syndrome](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Acute_radiation_syndrome)).

## Long term

The government's own summary is blunt: "in the case of a nuclear event, the impacts would be catastrophic for the UK", with long-term exclusion of contaminated areas ([NRR 2025, p. 40](doc:nrr-2025#page=40)). Caesium-137 has a 30-year half-life and is what keeps land closed for decades ([Caesium-137](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Caesium-137)); iodine-131 is gone in a few months ([Iodine-131](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Iodine-131)). Grow food in soil that was covered or dug over, not on the surface layer.

Whether a large exchange cools the climate for years is disputed: the 1987 survival manual called "nuclear winter" a myth ([NWSS, p. 23](doc:nwss#page=23)); later climate models disagree ([Nuclear winter](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Nuclear_winter)). Plan for the worse case with the [impact winter playbook](playbook:impact-winter) and the [long rebuild playbook](playbook:long-rebuild).

## UK specifics

- **Targets and wind.** The UK's nuclear weapons infrastructure is HMNB Clyde at Faslane and the warhead store at Coulport, AWE Aldermaston and Burghfield, the submarine yards at Barrow, Devonport and Rosyth, and the US-used air bases ([Nuclear weapons of the United Kingdom](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Nuclear_weapons_of_the_United_Kingdom)); they are on the map with the airfields ([UK target overlay](map:?overlay=nuclear-sites&overlay=airports-military)). Britain's prevailing wind is south-westerly ([Climate of the United Kingdom](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Climate_of_the_United_Kingdom)), so fallout from a strike usually travels north-east of it; the wind on the day decides, and the map's compass tells you which way you are from the nearest site.
- **How far the blast and heat reach.** A Trident warhead is about 100 kilotons ([Trident](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Trident_(UK_nuclear_programme))), and warheads of 100 to 475 kilotons are the commonest in the big arsenals ([Effects of nuclear explosions](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Effects_of_nuclear_explosions)). Under clear skies a 20 kiloton air burst (Hiroshima-class) destroys most buildings out to about 1.7 km, damages them out to about 4.7 km and gives third-degree burns to exposed skin out to about 2.5 km; a 1 megaton burst destroys most buildings to about 6.2 km, damages them to about 17 km and burns to about 12 km ([Effects of nuclear explosions](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Effects_of_nuclear_explosions)); for 100 kilotons, severe blast reaches about 1.9 km ([Effects of nuclear explosions](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Effects_of_nuclear_explosions)). FEMA's planning table for a 10 kiloton ground burst is severe damage to 0.8 km, moderate to 1.6 km and light damage (mostly broken glass) to 4.8 km ([FEMA, p. 27](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=27)). A megaton burst breaks some windows 64 km away, and its blast wave takes about 22 seconds to reach a point 10 km out ([NWSS, p. 30](doc:nwss#page=30)): that gap is why "drop and cover" at the flash works.
- **No public shelters.** The Cold War shelter and warning organisations were wound up in 1992, and the rising-and-falling siren and the four-minute warning belong to the past ([Four-minute warning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Four-minute_warning)); Protect and Survive (1980) remains the only public shelter guidance ever printed in the UK ([Protect and Survive, p. 3](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=3)), and its instruction on the warning was the same as today's: children to the fall-out room, gas and electricity off at the mains, stoves closed, windows shut, curtains drawn ([Protect and Survive, p. 11](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=11)). The register's nuclear-attack scenario is held at a higher classification ([NRR 2025, p. 184](doc:nrr-2025#page=184)).
- **Stable iodine.** UK stock is potassium iodate, pre-distributed only inside the planning zones round civil nuclear sites under REPPIR 2019; the doses by age and who has priority are in the [radiation module](module:radiation). The tablets do nothing for radiation sickness and nothing against fallout gamma ([FEMA, p. 97](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=97)).
- **Official advice** for a radiation release is "go in, stay in, tune in" ([UKHSA nuclear emergencies](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/nuclear-emergencies-information-for-the-public)); the numbers that may still answer are on [UK numbers](page:uk-numbers).
- **Water.** Protect and Survive asked for fourteen days of drinking water at two pints a person a day and twice that for washing ([Protect and Survive, p. 7](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=7)); this box plans for 14 days at 3 litres a person a day, food for two weeks, and cash for two weeks of essentials in small notes, on top of the government's three-day baseline ([Prepare](kiwix:prepare_uk/prepare.campaign.gov.uk/get-prepared-for-emergencies/)).

## Checklist

- [ ] Choose the fall-out room now: basement or ground-floor middle room, fewest outside walls {#choose-fallout-room}
- [ ] Store 14 days of covered drinking water (3 litres a person a day) and 14 days of tinned food {#fourteen-days-water-food}
- [ ] Battery or wind-up radio, spare batteries, torch, candles, matches in the room {#radio-and-light}
- [ ] Lidded bucket, bin liners, disinfectant, toilet paper, a second bin for refuse {#bucket-toilet}
- [ ] Materials to block windows and pack round the inner refuge: earth, sand, books, boxes {#refuge-materials}
- [ ] Turn off gas and electricity at the mains, damp fires, shut windows, draw curtains {#mains-off-windows-shut}
- [ ] Everyone in the room; stay in for 48 hours minimum, then reassess by radio {#stay-48-hours}
- [ ] Outer clothes off and bagged at the door; wash hair and skin if you were outside {#decontaminate-at-door}
- [ ] No iodine tablets unless the authorities say so; note who took what and when {#iodine-only-if-told}
- [ ] Write down the time of any vomiting after exposure, per person {#log-vomiting-times}
- [ ] Wipe tins before opening; no fresh milk, leafy greens or open-grown food until cleared {#no-milk-or-greens}
- [ ] Mark the nearest nuclear site and the wind direction on the map {#map-sites-and-wind}
