## Right now

**If you see a flash, do not look at it.** Drop flat behind anything solid, face down, eyes shut, mouth open, hands under your body. The light arrives first; the blast wave follows seconds to a minute later, and it is the blast and flying glass that injure most people outside the fireball ([FEMA, p. 31](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=31)). Flash blindness is temporary; do not drive.

**Then get inside and stay inside.** Within ten minutes fallout begins to come down where the wind carries it. Go into the nearest solid building, to a basement or the middle of the ground floor, away from windows and outside walls, and stay there. Cars are not shelters ([FEMA, p. 74](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=74)). The 1980 British instruction was the same: send the children to the fall-out room, turn off the gas and electricity at the mains, close stoves, shut windows, draw curtains, go to the room ([Protect and Survive, p. 11](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=11)).

**If you were outside when fallout fell:** take off your outer clothes at the door, bag them, wash hair and skin with soap and plenty of water, no conditioner, no scrubbing; this removes most of the contamination ([UKHSA decontamination sheet, p. 1](doc:ukhsa-radiation-decontamination#page=1)).

**Do not take iodine tablets unless told to.** They protect the thyroid only, against radioactive iodine only, and only if taken at the right time ([NRPB, p. 8](doc:nrpb-stable-iodine#page=8)); see UK specifics.

There is no siren network in the UK today. Any warning will come as an Emergency Alert on 4G and 5G phones, on the radio and on television ([Prepare, alerts](kiwix:prepare_uk/prepare.campaign.gov.uk/get-prepared-for-emergencies/alerts/)). The rising-and-falling siren note and the four-minute warning belong to the past ([Four-minute warning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Four-minute_warning)).

## First 72 hours

**Stay in for at least 48 hours, then reassess with the radio, not by looking outside.** "The danger from fall-out is greatest in the first forty-eight hours. During that time you must stay in the fall-out room and as far as possible within your inner refuge" ([Protect and Survive, p. 15](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=15)). The physics is the 7:10 rule: every sevenfold increase in time cuts the dose rate tenfold, so what is left at 7 hours is a tenth of the rate at 1 hour, at 49 hours a hundredth, and at two weeks a thousandth ([FEMA, p. 35](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=35)). FEMA's planning floor is 12 to 24 hours in a good shelter and no self-evacuation before 24 hours unless told; 48 hours is the sensible household minimum because you will not know how heavy your local fallout is ([FEMA, p. 74](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=74)).

**Build the inner refuge in the first hours** if you did not before: a table or door-and-earth lean-to in the corner of the fall-out room furthest from outside walls, packed round with boxes of earth, books, furniture and full water containers; block the windows ([Protect and Survive, p. 5](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=5)). Each 9 cm of packed earth halves the gamma dose, as does 6 cm of concrete, 18 cm of water or 22 cm of wood ([NWSS, p. 19](doc:nwss#page=19)). In a block of flats of five storeys or more, do not use the top two floors; in a bungalow, shelter with a neighbour if you can ([Protect and Survive, p. 5](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=5)).

**Water and food.** Use only water that was covered or in pipes and tanks before the fallout came; you cannot boil radioactivity out of water ([Protect and Survive, p. 7](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=7)). Sealed and tinned food is safe; wipe the tins before opening. Eat perishables first.

**A lidded bucket is the toilet**, with plastic bag liners and a second bin for refuse outside the room ([Protect and Survive, p. 14](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=14)).

**Fire.** Turn off the gas at the meter, damp down open fires, keep a bucket of water and sand by the door; the thermal flash sets curtains and rubbish alight through windows ([Protect and Survive, p. 10](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=10)).

**Essential trips out** in the first days should be minutes long and done by adults over 40, whose thyroid risk from radioiodine is negligible ([NRPB, p. 16](doc:nrpb-stable-iodine#page=16)); keep separate shoes and outer clothes for outside and take them off at the door ([Protect and Survive, p. 13](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=13)).

**Radiation sickness.** Vomiting within an hour of exposure means a severe dose; write down the times; rest, fluids and cleanliness are the treatment you can give ([Radiation sickness card](card:radiation-sickness)).

## First month

Expect up to two weeks confined in the worst-affected areas ([Protect and Survive, p. 4](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=4)); most shelters can be left for longer periods after two weeks ([NWSS, p. 19](doc:nwss#page=19)). Listen for instructions on when and where evacuation is organised; leave only by the route given, because the fallout pattern is uneven and a wrong turn can take you into a worse area ([FEMA, p. 33](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=33)).

Milk from grazing animals is the main food route for radioiodine, as the 1957 Windscale fire showed when milk over 500 km² was destroyed ([Windscale fire](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Windscale_fire)); avoid fresh milk, leafy vegetables and anything grown in the open until it is declared safe ([NWSS, p. 90](doc:nwss#page=90)). Wash and peel root vegetables.

Deaths: place the body in another room, wrapped and labelled; if no instructions have come within five days, bury it temporarily as soon as it is safe to go out and mark the spot ([Protect and Survive, p. 13](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=13)). Infection is the main killer of people who received a moderate dose, in the weeks when their blood counts fall; keep every wound clean and keep them away from crowds ([Acute radiation syndrome](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Acute_radiation_syndrome)).

A Kearny fallout meter, built from a tin, foil and thread, tells you the dose rate when no instrument exists ([NWSS, p. 221](doc:nwss#page=221)).

## Long term

The government's own summary is blunt: "in the case of a nuclear event, the impacts would be catastrophic for the UK", with long-term exclusion of contaminated areas ([NRR 2025, p. 40](doc:nrr-2025#page=40)). Caesium-137 has a 30-year half-life and is what keeps land closed for decades ([Caesium-137](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Caesium-137)); iodine-131 is gone in a few months ([Iodine-131](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Iodine-131)). Grow food in soil that was covered or dug over, not on the surface layer.

Whether a large exchange cools the climate for years is disputed: the 1987 survival manual called "nuclear winter" a myth ([NWSS, p. 23](doc:nwss#page=23)); later climate models disagree ([Nuclear winter](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Nuclear_winter)). Plan for the worse case with the [impact winter playbook](playbook:impact-winter) and the [long rebuild playbook](playbook:long-rebuild).

## UK specifics

- **Targets and wind.** The UK's nuclear weapons infrastructure is HMNB Clyde at Faslane and the warhead store at Coulport, AWE Aldermaston and Burghfield, the submarine yards at Barrow, Devonport and Rosyth, and the US-used air bases ([Nuclear weapons of the United Kingdom](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Nuclear_weapons_of_the_United_Kingdom)); they are on the map with the airfields ([UK target overlay](map:?overlay=nuclear-sites&overlay=airports-military)). Britain's prevailing wind is south-westerly ([Climate of the United Kingdom](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Climate_of_the_United_Kingdom)), so fallout from a strike usually travels north-east of it; the wind on the day decides, and the map's compass tells you which way you are from the nearest site.
- **No public shelters, no sirens.** The Cold War shelter and warning organisations were wound up in 1992 ([Four-minute warning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Four-minute_warning)); Protect and Survive (1980) remains the only public shelter guidance ever printed in the UK ([Protect and Survive, p. 3](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=3)). The register's nuclear-attack scenario is held at a higher classification ([NRR 2025, p. 184](doc:nrr-2025#page=184)).
- **Stable iodine.** UK stock is potassium iodate, pre-distributed only inside the planning zones round civil nuclear sites under REPPIR 2019 ([REPPIR 2019](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2019/703/contents)). The NRPB doses are adults 100 mg iodine (169 mg potassium iodate or 130 mg potassium iodide), children 3 to 12 half that, 1 month to 3 years a quarter, newborns an eighth ([NRPB, p. 9](doc:nrpb-stable-iodine#page=9)); priority is newborns, children under ten and pregnant or breastfeeding women ([NRPB, p. 8](doc:nrpb-stable-iodine#page=8)). The tablets do nothing for radiation sickness and nothing against fallout gamma ([FEMA, p. 97](doc:fema-nuclear-detonation-2022#page=97)).
- **Official advice** for a radiation release is "go in, stay in, tune in" ([UKHSA nuclear emergencies](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/nuclear-emergencies-information-for-the-public)); the numbers that may still answer are on [UK numbers](page:uk-numbers).
- **Water.** Protect and Survive asked for fourteen days of drinking water at two pints a person a day and twice that for washing ([Protect and Survive, p. 7](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=7)); this box asks for 2.5 to 3 litres a day per [Prepare](kiwix:prepare_uk/prepare.campaign.gov.uk/get-prepared-for-emergencies/).

## Checklist

- [ ] Choose the fall-out room now: basement or ground-floor middle room, fewest outside walls {#choose-fallout-room}
- [ ] Store 14 days of covered drinking water (2.5 to 3 litres a person a day) and 14 days of tinned food {#fourteen-days-water-food}
- [ ] Battery or wind-up radio, spare batteries, torch, candles, matches in the room {#radio-and-light}
- [ ] Lidded bucket, bin liners, disinfectant, toilet paper, a second bin for refuse {#bucket-toilet}
- [ ] Materials to block windows and pack round the inner refuge: earth, sand, books, boxes {#refuge-materials}
- [ ] Turn off gas and electricity at the mains, damp fires, shut windows, draw curtains {#mains-off-windows-shut}
- [ ] Everyone in the room; stay in for 48 hours minimum, then reassess by radio {#stay-48-hours}
- [ ] Outer clothes off and bagged at the door; wash hair and skin if you were outside {#decontaminate-at-door}
- [ ] No iodine tablets unless the authorities say so; note who took what and when {#iodine-only-if-told}
- [ ] Write down the time of any vomiting after exposure, per person {#log-vomiting-times}
- [ ] Wipe tins before opening; no fresh milk, leafy greens or open-grown food until cleared {#no-milk-or-greens}
- [ ] Mark the nearest nuclear site and the wind direction on the map {#map-sites-and-wind}
