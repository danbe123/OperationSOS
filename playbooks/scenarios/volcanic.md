## Right now

**Iceland is the source.** The register names Bárðarbunga and Eyjafjallajökull; its scenario is an ash-rich eruption lasting three months with up to 15 days of severe flight disruption and 10 more of moderate ([NRR 2025, p. 129](doc:nrr-2025#page=129)). In 2010 Eyjafjallajökull closed British airspace for six days ([2010 eruptions of Eyjafjallajökull](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/2010_eruptions_of_Eyjafjallajökull)). Nobody is flying: plan accordingly.

**A gas-rich eruption is the dangerous one.** Laki in 1783 sent a sulphurous haze over Britain for months; the summer of 1783 killed an estimated 23,000 people in England, mostly from breathing it ([Laki](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Laki)). The register's air-quality scenario, 30 days of elevated ozone or fine particulates with deaths from respiratory and heart disease, is the planning frame ([NRR 2025, p. 153](doc:nrr-2025#page=153)).

**On a bad-air day:** stay in, windows shut, exertion off, inhalers close; anyone with asthma or COPD who cannot speak in sentences needs an ambulance — [[call 999]] ([Asthma (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/asthma/); [Asthma attack card](card:asthma-attack)). Sulphur dioxide stings eyes and throat and is worse for children and the old ([Sulfur dioxide](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Sulfur_dioxide)).

**Cover the water and disconnect the downpipes.** Ash and the fluoride it carries settle on butts, troughs and open tanks: lids on now, and the downpipe pulled out of every water butt before the ash arrives so the roof does not wash it into the store ([Volcanic ash](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Volcanic_ash)).

## First 72 hours

- **Masks.** An FFP2 or FFP3 respirator stops ash; a cloth over the face stops the largest grains and nothing else; nothing stops sulphur dioxide except staying in ([European respirator standards](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/European_respirator_standards); [Surgical mask](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Surgical_mask)). Goggles for anyone who must work outside; contact lenses out.
- **The house.** Doors and windows shut, gaps taped, damp cloths at the sills, extractor fans off; the boiler's air intake and the car's cabin filter clog with ash ([Volcanic ash](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Volcanic_ash)). Sweep ash wet, never dry; do not wash it into drains.
- **Roofs.** Wet ash is heavy, and rain can add half to its weight again; flat roofs and conservatories fail first; clear it from the ground or not at all ([Volcanic ash](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Volcanic_ash)).
- **Driving.** Ash cuts visibility and scours engines and brakes; stay off the road in ashfall; if you must, slow, lights on, windows up, air on recirculate.
- **Animals.** Grazing animals in Iceland died of fluorosis from ash on the grass in 1783, and in New Zealand in 1995 two thousand sheep died on land with only 1 to 3 mm of ash ([Fluoride toxicity](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Fluoride_toxicity); [Volcanic ash](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Volcanic_ash)); bring livestock and poultry under cover, feed stored hay and covered water, keep pets in ([Livestock module](module:livestock)).
- **Warnings.** Bad-air days are announced through Defra's Daily Air Quality Index, a ten-point scale in four bands from low to very high with health advice for each, carried in the Met Office forecast, and through Emergency Alerts where needed ([Air quality index](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Air_quality_index); [How alerts work](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/alerts/how-alerts-work)).

## First month

- **Weeks of it.** A three-month eruption means episodes of ashfall and haze whenever the wind is from the north-west; keep the mask routine and the covered-water routine for the duration, and watch the forecast for the wind swinging back ([NRR 2025, p. 129](doc:nrr-2025#page=129)).
- **Water.** {{#if water}}Tap water from treated mains is safe{{else}}The mains has stopped, so covered stored water comes first{{/if}}. A butt, trough or private supply that caught ash is a different matter: the grains settle and filter out, but the fluoride and acid they leach are dissolved, and neither boiling nor a filter removes a dissolved chemical, so drink stored or mains water and keep ash-touched rainwater for the garden and the toilet until the butt has been emptied, rinsed and refilled from a clean roof ([Volcanic ash](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Volcanic_ash); [Water module](module:water); [DWI private supplies](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.dwi.gov.uk/private-water-supplies/)). Acid rain from sulphur dioxide is a garden and pond problem, not a drinking one ([Acid rain](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Acid_rain)).
- **Cleaning up.** Wet the ash before moving it, shovel it into bags or a heap away from drains and the vegetable patch, and clear gutters before the next rain because ash sets like cement and blocks them; do not hose it into the sewer, which it blocks and abrades ([Volcanic ash](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Volcanic_ash)). Masks and goggles on for the whole job.
- **Machinery and electrics.** Ash is abrasive and, when wet, conductive: change the car's air and cabin filters after each fall, keep this box and anything with a fan in a sealed room, and expect outdoor electrics, pumps and a generator's air intake to need cleaning ([Volcanic ash](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Volcanic_ash)).
- **Supplies.** No flights means no air freight: some medicines, some fresh produce and mail order stop; the [supply chain playbook](playbook:supply-chain) applies to those things.
- **Animals for the duration.** Stored hay, covered water and housing for as long as ash lies on the grass; a thin fall puts animals off grazing and a heavy one poisons them, so the flock that cannot be housed and fed is sold or killed early rather than left to it ([Volcanic ash](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Volcanic_ash); [Butchery](page:butchery); [Livestock module](module:livestock)).
- **Growing.** A dusting of ash is fertiliser once washed in; a thick fall smothers; rinse leafy crops before eating and cover cold frames ([Growing food module](module:growing-food)).
- **Health.** Chest infections follow weeks of bad air; pneumonia signs and the sepsis rule are on the NHS pages ([Pneumonia (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/pneumonia/); [Sepsis (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/sepsis/)); anyone with a heart or lung condition keeps two weeks of medicine in hand ([Medical module](module:medical)).

## Long term

A large enough eruption cools the world for a year or two: Tambora in 1815 gave Europe the "Year Without a Summer" in 1816, with frosts in July, failed harvests and famine ([Year Without a Summer](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Year_Without_a_Summer); [1815 eruption of Mount Tambora](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/1815_eruption_of_Mount_Tambora)). If the summer after the eruption is cold and dark, the [impact winter playbook](playbook:impact-winter) and the [famine playbook](playbook:famine) are the next chapters: store this year's harvest, plan next year's for cold, and keep the animals fed.

## UK specifics

- **The register** lists volcanic eruption among natural hazards with Iceland as the main source and aviation as the main impact ([NRR 2025, p. 129](doc:nrr-2025#page=129)); the GOV.UK hazards guidance covers volcanic ash ([Extreme weather and natural hazards](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/guidance/extreme-weather-and-natural-hazards)).
- **Which volcanoes:** Bárðarbunga (whose Holuhraun fissure poured sulphur dioxide in 2014 to 2015), Eyjafjallajökull, Grímsvötn, Katla and Hekla are the frequent ones; Laki and Eldgjá are the historic gas eruptions ([Bárðarbunga](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Bárðarbunga); [Grímsvötn](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Grímsvötn); [Katla (volcano)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Katla_(volcano)); [Eldgjá](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Eldgjá)).
- **Airports** on the map close first and open last ([airports overlay](map:?overlay=airports)); ferries and the tunnel keep running.
- **Health warnings** come through UKHSA's Weather-Health Alert system and Defra's Daily Air Quality Index ([Weather-health alerting system](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/guidance/weather-health-alerting-system); [Air quality index](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Air_quality_index)); {{#if phones}}[[call 111]] for breathing problems that are not an emergency, and [[call 999]] when they are ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)).{{else}}with the phones down, work from the [asthma card](card:asthma-attack) and take anyone who cannot speak in sentences to hospital yourself ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)).{{/if}}
- **Scotland and the north** are downwind first and most; the Northern Isles and the Hebrides got the worst of Laki's haze ([Laki](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Laki)).
