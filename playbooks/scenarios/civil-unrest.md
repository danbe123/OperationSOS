## Right now

**Go in, stay in, tune in.** The register's disorder scenario is criminal damage, arson, rioting and looting at several flashpoints in urban areas at once, with injuries to the public and responders and health services under pressure ([NRR 2025, p. 173](doc:nrr-2025#page=173)). Nobody was killed by going home early. Lock doors, close curtains, move the car off the street if you can do it quietly, and keep away from windows on the street side.

**Do not go to watch, and do not go to help unless asked.** The register's own words: turning up at the scene "could be dangerous and overwhelm the emergency services" ([NRR 2025, p. 23](doc:nrr-2025#page=23)). Five people died in the 2011 riots, three of them protecting shops ([2011 England riots](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/2011_England_riots)).

**Fire is the killer in a riot.** Test the smoke alarms, keep the stairs clear, know the way out of the back, and keep buckets of water where an arson attack on the front door would need them ([Shelter and heat module](module:shelter-heat)). Call 999 for fire; fires in riots get answered even when burglaries do not.

**Rumours.** Every riot runs on messages that are half true; check with the SHARE checklist before believing or forwarding ([NRR 2025, p. 22](doc:nrr-2025#page=22)). Emergency Alerts and BBC local radio are the official sources ([How alerts work](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/alerts/how-alerts-work)).

## First 72 hours

- **Curfews and dispersal orders** are lawful under the Public Order Act 1986 and its successors; obey them ([Public Order Act 1986](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1986/64/contents); [Public Order Act 2023](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/15/contents)). Under the Civil Contingencies Act 2004 ministers can restrict movement and assembly by regulation ([Civil Contingencies Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/contents)).
- **Force.** You may use reasonable force to protect yourself, others and property; a householder facing an intruder is judged only on whether the force was grossly disproportionate ([CJIA 2008 s76](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2008/4/section/76)). Carrying anything as a weapon in the street, or lying in wait, is an offence, and the courts after 2011 gave riot-related offences heavier sentences than normal ([2011 England riots](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/2011_England_riots)).
- **Looting is theft and burglary** whatever the crowd is doing ([Theft Act 1968 s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/60/section/1)); over 1,000 people were charged within weeks of the 2024 riots ([2024 United Kingdom riots](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/2024_United_Kingdom_riots)).
- **Supplies out of sight.** Nothing visible from the street: no generator noise, no full shelves in the window, bins put out late ([Security and the law module](module:security-law)).
- **The street.** Neighbours who know each other, share numbers and a PMR446 channel and check on the vulnerable are the only security that scales ([Community module](module:community)); patrols and "defence groups" are how residents end up arrested or hurt.
- **Medical:** A&E will be full; treat cuts, burns and smoke inhalation at home with the cards, and call 999 only for life-threatening cases ([Burns card](card:burns); [Severe bleeding card](card:severe-bleeding)).

## First month

- **Strikes and shortages.** Disorder and industrial action travel together; the register plans for prolonged strikes in critical roles with services "at a reduced capacity", and the Civil Contingencies Act obliges key organisations to plan for mass absence ([NRR 2025, p. 175](doc:nrr-2025#page=175)). Shops close early, deliveries stop in affected areas, cash machines are looted or empty; two weeks of food and cash carry you past the worst ([Food module](module:food)).
- **Movement.** Stations and fuel stations near trouble close; plan journeys away from the map's marked stations and forecourts and travel in daylight ([stations and fuel overlay](map:?overlay=rail&overlay=fuel)).
- **Evacuation** is rarely needed, but a house that has been attacked or is next to a fire is left with the grab bag by the back way, to family or the council's rest centre ([Evacuation module](module:evacuation)).
- **Insurance and compensation:** photograph damage, report it to the police for a crime number, and claim; keep politics out of every conversation with neighbours, insurers and the police ([Community module](module:community)).
- **Children:** keep them in, keep routines, answer honestly; distress that lasts weeks needs help ([Coping with trauma](kiwix:prepare_uk/prepare.campaign.gov.uk/coping-with-trauma/)).

## Long term

Order in Britain has broken locally and briefly, never nationally for long: the 1926 general strike lasted nine days ([1926 United Kingdom general strike](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/1926_United_Kingdom_general_strike)), the 2011 riots five ([2011 England riots](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/2011_England_riots)). If policing fails for months, the armed forces act under police control as military aid to the civil power, not as a replacement for law ([Military aid to the civil power](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Military_aid_to_the_civil_power)). Disorder that comes with war is the [invasion playbook](playbook:invasion). A street that has organised itself for a fortnight has the beginnings of the community plan in the [long rebuild playbook](playbook:long-rebuild). The Prepare advice on trauma applies to everyone who saw the worst of it ([Coping with trauma](kiwix:prepare_uk/prepare.campaign.gov.uk/coping-with-trauma/)).

## UK specifics

- **Numbers:** 999 for danger to life and fire; 101 for everything else, which will not answer; Samaritans 116 123 ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)).
- **Offences you can commit by accident:** riot, violent disorder and affray under the Public Order Act 1986 apply to anyone who joins in, including to "have a look" ([Public Order Act 1986](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1986/64/contents)); aggravated trespass and obstruction of the highway are the protest offences ([CJPOA 1994 s68](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/33/section/68)); assaulting a police officer is a separate offence under the Police Act 1996 ([Police Act 1996](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/16/contents)).
- **Weapons:** carrying a knife, a bat or a pepper spray "for protection" is an offence; the penknife rule is 3 inches, non-locking ([CJA 1988 s139](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/33/section/139); [Knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law)).
- **Prevention of Crime Act 1953** makes carrying an offensive weapon in public an offence in its own right ([Prevention of Crime Act 1953](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Eliz2/1-2/14)).
- **Who runs the response:** the police lead; Local Resilience Forums coordinate councils, fire and health; councils open rest centres ([LRF contacts](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/guidance/local-resilience-forums-contact-details)).
- **Scotland and Northern Ireland** have their own public order law and, in Northern Ireland, their own history; the numbers and the go-in-stay-in rule are the same ([Security and the law module](module:security-law)).

## Checklist

- [ ] Everyone home; doors locked, curtains closed, car moved quietly off the street {#everyone-home}
- [ ] Smoke alarms tested; stairs clear; back way out known; buckets of water by the front door {#fire-ready}
- [ ] Radio on BBC local; Emergency Alerts on; SHARE checklist before forwarding anything {#official-sources}
- [ ] Nothing valuable or edible visible from the street {#nothing-visible}
- [ ] Street numbers list and PMR446 channel agreed; vulnerable neighbours named {#street-network}
- [ ] First-aid kit out: dressings, burns kit, gloves {#first-aid-out}
- [ ] Two weeks of food and cash in the house {#two-weeks-food-cash}
- [ ] Grab bag by the back door in case of fire or attack {#grab-bag-back-door}
- [ ] Curfew times and any emergency regulations written down and obeyed {#know-the-orders}
- [ ] Damage photographed and reported for a crime number {#photograph-report}
