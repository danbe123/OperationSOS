## Right now

**Count the calories, not the tins.** An adult needs about 2,000 kcal a day for women and 2,500 for men ([The Eatwell Guide (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/)); add up what the store holds in days at that rate and ration from today, because a store spent in the first month is gone before the first harvest ([Food module](module:food)). What 2,000 kcal looks like on a plate, and how far a sack of grain goes, is on [Food storage](page:food-storage). Children, pregnant women and the sick are fed first.

**Sow what the month allows ([Growing food module](module:growing-food)):** nothing tender outdoors before mid-May, hardy crops and under-cover sowing in every other month, and every bed, tub and lawn edge counted as ground. The 1940 Dig for Victory campaign turned lawns and parks into 1.4 million allotments ([Victory garden](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Victory_garden)).

**Do not eat what you cannot name.** The [food module](module:food) below names the plants and fungi that kill in Britain; nothing on the strength of a photograph, and nothing from roadsides, dog areas or sprayed fields ([FSA safe foraging](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-for-safe-foraging/guidance-for-safe-foraging)).

## First 72 hours

- **Ration.** The wartime ration was small and the population was healthier for it: registration with one shop, points for tins, and fair shares ([Rationing in the United Kingdom](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rationing_in_the_United_Kingdom)). Run the household the same way: a weekly allowance per person, written down.
- **Buy and store staples in bulk** while anything is still for sale: grain and flour keep for years if dry, cool and sealed against mice; a hand mill turns wheat into flour for as long as the wheat lasts ([Food storage](page:food-storage); [Wheat flour](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Wheat_flour)).
- **Preserve every glut** by drying, salting, pickling and jam; the canning rule that stops botulism is in the [food module](module:food) above ([USDA canning](kiwix:usda-2015_en/home)).
- **Water** for people, animals and crops in that order: {{#if water}}fill the butts from every downpipe while the mains still runs{{else}}it all comes from the store, the butts and the stream while the mains is off{{/if}}, and a drought-year garden is watered at the roots in the evening ([Water module](module:water)).
- **Animals.** Feed animals only what people cannot eat: a hen or a rabbit on bought feed is a net loss in a famine, and a herd that cannot be fed through winter is culled in autumn while it still has flesh on it. Killing and dressing at home is on [Butchery](page:butchery); the registration, housing-order and stunning rules are in the [livestock module](module:livestock) below.

## First month

- **Foraging.** Nettles, blackberries, rosehips (the wartime vitamin C), hazelnuts, sweet chestnuts, acorns leached of their tannin, seaweed and shellfish from clean water are the calories and vitamins that grow wild in Britain ([Urtica dioica](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Urtica_dioica); [Rose hip](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rose_hip); [Acorn](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Acorn); [Edible seaweed](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Edible_seaweed)); shellfish carry poisoning and norovirus ([Paralytic shellfish poisoning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Paralytic_shellfish_poisoning)). What you may pick, where, and the rule on uprooting and selling: [Foraging law](page:foraging-law).
- **Fishing and shooting** need a licence, permission and the season ([Foraging, fishing and hunting law](page:foraging-law)). Rabbits, wood pigeons and grey squirrels are the abundant wild meat ([European rabbit](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/European_rabbit); [Common wood pigeon](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Common_wood_pigeon)); the Hunting Act 2004 bans hunting mammals with dogs, but its Schedule 1 exempts rabbits and rats, so a terrier or a lurcher on rabbits with the landowner's permission is lawful ([Hunting Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/37/contents)). Paunching, skinning and jointing what you catch is on [Butchery](page:butchery).
- **Nutrition.** Signs of malnutrition are weight loss, tiredness, poor healing and infections; children stop growing ([Malnutrition (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/malnutrition/)). Scurvy from no fresh food in three months (bleeding gums, bruising) is cured by any vitamin C ([Scurvy (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/scurvy/)); rickets in children from no vitamin D is prevented by sun and oily fish ([Rickets (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/rickets-and-osteomalacia/)). Fat and protein run out before starch: eggs, milk, pulses, nuts and fish are the priority ([Medical module](module:medical)). Skipping meals under strict rationing brings on low blood sugar — shaking, sweating and confusion — treated with fast sugar then a proper meal ([Low blood sugar card](card:low-blood-sugar)).
- **Refeeding.** Feeding a starving person is itself dangerous: small frequent meals for the first days, because a sudden full plate shifts potassium, phosphate and magnesium out of the blood and can stop the heart ([Refeeding syndrome (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Refeeding_syndrome)).
- **Community kitchens.** The wartime British Restaurants fed a hot meal for a set price to anyone; a village hall with one cooker does the same ([British Restaurant](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/British_Restaurant); [Community module](module:community)).

## Long term

Ireland lost a million people to hunger and a million to emigration between 1845 and 1852 when blight took the one crop most people ate ([Great Famine (Ireland)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Great_Famine_(Ireland)); [Phytophthora infestans](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Phytophthora_infestans)); the Dutch Hunger Winter of 1944 to 1945 killed 18,000 in a modern country cut off from food ([Dutch famine of 1944–1945](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Dutch_famine_of_1944–1945)). The lesson of both is many crops, not one: a grain as well as potatoes, roots that stand in the ground through winter, and animals that turn what people cannot eat into eggs, milk and meat; the rotation, fertility and seed-saving that make it last are in the [growing food module](module:growing-food) above. Store the harvest in bulk and mill as you go ([Food storage](page:food-storage)); the [long rebuild playbook](playbook:long-rebuild) is the decade after.

## UK specifics

- **Why it happens here.** The register's food and farming risks are contamination of the food chain ([NRR 2025, p. 122](doc:nrr-2025#page=122)), foot and mouth, with 1.9 million animals culled on 2,900 premises in the planning case and six million in 2001 ([NRR 2025, p. 160](doc:nrr-2025#page=160)), avian influenza with eight million birds lost over six to eight months ([NRR 2025, p. 162](doc:nrr-2025#page=162)), African swine fever ([NRR 2025, p. 166](doc:nrr-2025#page=166)), plant pests ([NRR 2025, p. 168](doc:nrr-2025#page=168)) and three-dry-winter drought ([NRR 2025, p. 151](doc:nrr-2025#page=151); [heat and drought playbook](playbook:heat-drought)). Nitrogen fertiliser is made from natural gas by the Haber process, so a gas crisis is a harvest crisis a year later ([Haber process](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Haber_process); [NRR 2025, p. 62](doc:nrr-2025#page=62)). Foot and mouth closed the countryside in 2001 and housing orders during bird flu are law ([livestock module](module:livestock)).
- **The law of the land.** Foraging, uprooting, selling and the protected plants: [Foraging law](page:foraging-law). Open access land and Scotland's right of responsible access let you walk, not pick crops or take game ([Moving across country](page:fieldcraft-moving); [access land overlay](map:?overlay=access-land&overlay=footpaths)). Taking crops from a field is theft ([Theft Act 1968 s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/60/section/1)).
- **Allotments.** Under the Small Holdings and Allotments Act 1908 a parish or district council in England and Wales has a duty to provide allotments to meet demand, and section 23 lets six residents on the electoral register require it in writing; the council's duty and the 10-rod plot are on [Allotment (gardening)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Allotment_(gardening)). Ask in writing, in a group, early.
- **Precedent.** Britain rationed from 1940 to 1954 and imported less than it does now; the Ministry of Food, points and the Woolton pie are the working model ([Rationing in the United Kingdom](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rationing_in_the_United_Kingdom); [Minister of Food](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Minister_of_Food)).
- **Water** taken from a river, lake or borehole for irrigation is regulated by the Environment Agency under the Water Resources Act 1991, which needs an abstraction licence beyond small daily amounts ([Water Resources Act 1991](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Water_Resources_Act_1991)); rainwater and grey water are yours ([Water module](module:water)).

## Checklist

- [ ] Store counted in days at 2,000 to 2,500 kcal a person, and the weekly ration written down today {#count-calories now}
- [ ] Children, pregnant women, nursing mothers and the sick fed first, at every meal {#feed-the-weakest-first now}
- [ ] Every bed, tub and lawn edge sown with what the month allows; seed potatoes chitting {#plant-everything now}
- [ ] Nothing eaten that cannot be named: the two deadly plants learned by every adult before anyone forages {#foraging-rules now}
- [ ] Refeeding rule known: small meals first for anyone who has gone hungry {#refeeding-rule today}
- [ ] Vitamin C and vitamin D sources for children planned: rosehips, eggs, sun {#vitamins-for-children today}
- [ ] Rod licence, permission and seasons checked before fishing or shooting {#fishing-and-shooting-law today}
- [ ] A year's fertility found without bought fertiliser: manure source agreed with someone who keeps animals {#feed-the-soil week}
- [ ] Hens registered; housing-order rules known; rabbits or goats considered {#animals-lawful week}
- [ ] Pressure canner or drying racks ready for the glut; botulism rule learned {#preserve-safely week}
- [ ] Community kitchen or shared cooker organised in the hall {#community-kitchen week}
- [ ] Grain or flour in sealed bulk store; hand mill found; allotment request drafted {#bulk-grain-and-mill week}
