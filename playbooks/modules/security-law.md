## Key facts

- The Criminal Justice Act 1988 s139 makes it an offence to carry a bladed or pointed article in public without good reason; the only exemption is a folding pocket knife whose cutting edge does not exceed 3 inches (7.62 cm) and which does not lock ([s139](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/33/section/139); [GOV.UK knives](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/buying-carrying-knives)).
- There is no "martial law" in UK law: the armed forces may assist the civil authorities on request under Military Aid to the Civil Authorities, but they act **in support of** the police and civil authorities and stay under military command throughout, and service personnel have only a citizen's powers of arrest, not police powers. The Civil Contingencies Act 2004 is the emergency law. Part 1 makes police, fire, ambulance, councils, the NHS and the Environment Agency "Category 1 responders" with a duty to plan; Part 2 lets ministers make emergency regulations by Order in Council, or a minister alone if delay would be serious, that can restrict movement and assembly, requisition property and create offences, but each set lapses after 30 days unless Parliament renews it, Parliament must be told at once, and the regulations cannot touch the Human Rights Act or the Habeas Corpus Act ([Civil Contingencies Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/contents); [Civil Contingencies Act 2004 (Wikipedia)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Civil_Contingencies_Act_2004)).
- Terrorism Act 2000 s58 is the reason this library carries only public-domain survival and medical manuals and no tactical material ([s58](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/11/section/58)).

## What to do

1. Make the house hard to enter the way the Building Regulations now require of new homes: doors and ground-floor windows to the PAS 24 security standard, with the letter plate, hinges and glazing that go with it ([Approved Document Q, p. 11](doc:ad-q#page=11)); add lighting on the approaches, keep supplies out of sight of the street, and join or start a Neighbourhood Watch, whose members report and do not intervene ([Neighbourhood watch](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Neighborhood_watch)). Walk away from confrontation rather than escalate it.
2. Know what force the law allows: reasonable force is lawful under the Criminal Law Act 1967 s3, and for a householder confronting an intruder in their own home in England and Wales, force is unlawful only if it is grossly disproportionate ([s76](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2008/4/section/76); [self-defence in English law](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Self-defence_in_English_law)).
3. Taking goods from a shop or house during a disaster is theft or burglary, not an exception to the law; the courts treated looting harshly after the 2011 riots ([2011 England riots](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/2011_England_riots)).
4. Do not keep or acquire a prohibited weapon "just in case": s141 of the 1988 Act and the Offensive Weapons Act 2019 ban even home possession of zombie knives, knuckledusters and the rest of the prohibited list, and restrict corrosive substances ([Offensive Weapons Act 2019](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2019/17/contents)).

{{#if scenario:civil-unrest}}
**Go in and stay in.** The register's own advice to the public in disorder is that, unless the building itself is at risk, going inside and seeking information is usually the safest thing to do ([NRR 2025, p. 22](doc:nrr-2025#page=22)), and that turning up at the scene "could be dangerous and overwhelm the emergency services" ([NRR 2025, p. 23](doc:nrr-2025#page=23)). Lock up, close the curtains, move the car off the street if you can do it quietly, and keep supplies, generator noise and full shelves out of sight. Do not stand guard over a shop and do not form a patrol. Fire is what still gets answered — [[call 999]].
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:invasion}}
**Emergency powers, not martial law.** Regulations under the Civil Contingencies Act 2004 can restrict movement and assembly, requisition property and create offences, and they lapse after 30 days unless Parliament renews them ([Civil Contingencies Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/contents)). Obey checkpoints and curfews, carry identification, and keep a written, dated record of anything requisitioned, with the name and unit of whoever took it. Do not photograph or approach military positions, and leave unexploded ordnance where it lies. A licensed amateur may be ordered off the air by Ofcom notice once a state of emergency is declared ([Ofcom licence, p. 8](doc:ofcom-amateur-licence-2024#page=8)).
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:terrorism}}
**Run, hide, tell.** Run if there is a route away from the danger, leaving your belongings; hide if there is not, out of sight, phone silent, door barricaded; tell the police what you saw, where and when, once you are safe — [[call 999]] ([Run, hide, tell](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Run,_hide,_tell)). Do not stop to film and do not gather to watch: after an explosion, move well away and out of sight of the scene, because bombers plan for the crowd that gathers and for the responders. Armed police go in first and ambulances wait for the scene to be declared safe, so the first aid in between is yours ([Severe bleeding card](card:severe-bleeding)).
{{/if}}

## UK specifics

- Weapons in three lines: a folding non-locking knife under 3 inches is the only blade you may carry without good reason, and self-defence is never a good reason; pepper spray and stun guns are prohibited weapons under the Firearms Act 1968 s5 everywhere in the UK; crossbows are 18 and over, air weapons need no certificate in England and Wales below 12 foot-pounds but need one in Scotland and Northern Ireland, and shotguns and rifles always need a certificate. Every rule, with its citations and the Scotland and Northern Ireland differences, is on [Knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law) ([GOV.UK knives](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/buying-carrying-knives)).
- Trespass is a civil wrong in England and Wales, but not everywhere: on the railway and on military land where byelaws are posted it is a criminal offence, and water undertakers' byelaws made under the Water Industry Act 1991 s157 can make entering a waterworks a summary offence punishable by a fine up to level 5, on top of the offences of polluting or interfering with a water supply ([Water Industry Act 1991](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/56/contents)).
- Citizen's arrest: in England and Wales anyone may arrest a person who is committing, or has just committed, an indictable offence, but only if it is not reasonably practicable for a constable to do it and the arrest is needed to stop injury, damage, loss or escape (PACE 1984 s24A); Northern Ireland has the same power under its 1989 Order; Scotland has a narrower common-law power for serious crime seen in the act ([Citizen's arrest](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Citizen's_arrest)). Use it rarely: hand over to the police at once, and use no more force than the arrest needs ([Criminal Law Act 1967 s3](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1967/58/section/3)).
- Trespass is a civil matter in England and Wales, but aggravated trespass and residing on land with vehicles are criminal offences ([CJPOA 1994 s68](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/33/section/68); [PCSCA 2022 s83](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/32/section/83)); Scotland instead has a right of responsible access, covered in the [Navigation module](module:navigation).
