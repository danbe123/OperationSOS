## Key facts

- The Criminal Justice Act 1988 s139 makes it an offence to carry a bladed or pointed article in public without good reason; the only exemption is a folding pocket knife whose cutting edge does not exceed 3 inches (7.62 cm) and which does not lock ([s139](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/33/section/139); [GOV.UK knives](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/buying-carrying-knives)).
- There is no "martial law" in UK law: the armed forces may assist the civil authorities, but always under police control. The Civil Contingencies Act 2004 instead lets ministers make emergency regulations that can restrict movement and assembly, and it defines Category 1 responders ([Civil Contingencies Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/contents)).
- Terrorism Act 2000 s58 is the reason this library carries only public-domain survival and medical manuals and no tactical material ([s58](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/11/section/58)).

## What to do

1. Practical security is locks, lighting, a dog, and neighbours who know each other; do not display supplies, and walk away from confrontation rather than escalate it.
2. Know what force the law allows: reasonable force is lawful under the Criminal Law Act 1967 s3, and for a householder confronting an intruder in their own home, force is unlawful only if it is grossly disproportionate ([s76](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2008/4/section/76); [self-defence in English law](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Self-defence_in_English_law)).
3. Taking goods from a shop or house during a disaster is theft or burglary, not an exception to the law; the courts treated looting harshly after the 2011 riots ([2011 England riots](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/2011_England_riots)).
4. Do not keep or acquire a prohibited weapon "just in case": s141 of the 1988 Act and the Offensive Weapons Act 2019 ban even home possession of zombie knives, knuckledusters and the rest of the prohibited list, and restrict corrosive substances ([Offensive Weapons Act 2019](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2019/17/contents)).

## UK specifics

- Zombie-style knives and machetes were added to the ban in September 2024 and ninja swords in August 2025; the full current list is on [Knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law).
- Scotland's equivalent knife offence sits in the Criminal Law (Consolidation) (Scotland) Act 1995 s49 ([s49](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1995/39/section/49)). Pepper spray and stun guns are prohibited weapons everywhere in the UK under the Firearms Act 1968 s5 ([s5](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/27/section/5)). Crossbows may be owned from age 18 under the Crossbows Act 1987; air weapons need no licence in England and Wales under 12 ft·lb but do need one in Scotland ([Air Weapons and Licensing (Scotland) Act 2015](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2015/10/contents)); shotguns and rifles need certificates ([firearms licensing](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/guidance/firearms-licensing-police-guidance)).
- Trespass is a civil matter in England and Wales, but aggravated trespass and residing on land with vehicles are criminal offences ([CJPOA 1994 s68](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/33/section/68); [PCSCA 2022 s83](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/32/section/83)); Scotland instead has a right of responsible access, covered in the [Navigation module](module:navigation).
