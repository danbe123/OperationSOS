## Key facts

- A grid reference is two letters for the 100 km square, then eastings before northings: six figures place you within 100 m, eight within 10 m. For example, Ordnance Survey headquarters is SU 38726 14830 ([OS National Grid](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Ordnance_Survey_National_Grid)).
- Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland use the Irish Grid instead ([Irish grid reference system](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Irish_grid_reference_system)); the Channel Islands are referenced by latitude and longitude.
- A phone works out its own GPS position without any mobile network, but a browser only lets a web page read that position over a secure (https) connection, and this box is usually served over plain http, so the map page cannot show where the phone is. Use the map's own grid reference instead (it shows one for the centre of the view and for any point you tap), or read it off a paper map, and carry a compass ([OS National Grid](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Ordnance_Survey_National_Grid)).

## What to do

1. Estimate walking time with Naismith's rule: 5 km an hour, plus one extra minute for every 10 m of climb ([Naismith's rule](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Naismith's_rule)).
2. Check the map's margin for the current magnetic variation before relying on a compass bearing; in Britain it is now within a degree or two of zero, so it can be ignored for rough work ([Magnetic declination](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Magnetic_declination)).
3. Learn to take and walk a bearing, use a back bearing, count paces, aim off and use handrails and attack points, and to find north from the sun, a watch or the Pole Star: all of it, with the tide rules for the coast, is on [Map, compass and tides](page:fieldcraft-navigation) and in the map-reading manual ([FM 3-25.26](doc:fm-3-25-26-map-reading)).
4. In an emergency ask for the police, who call out mountain rescue, or the coastguard on the coast — [[call 999]]; the distress signal is six blasts or flashes a minute, and the whole procedure is on [Getting found](page:fieldcraft-rescue).
5. Check the weather and remaining daylight before setting out, and treat every beach and estuary as a tidal trap: this box carries no tide tables, since the UK Hydrographic Office's tidal data is not openly licensed, so work the tide out from the moon and the strand line as [Map, compass and tides](page:fieldcraft-navigation) describes.

## UK specifics

- England and Wales have public rights of way and CRoW open access land ([CRoW s2](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/37/section/2)); Scotland has a right of responsible access to almost all land ([Land Reform (Scotland) Act s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2003/2/section/1)); Northern Ireland has very few rights of way. Where you may walk and camp, in full, is on [Moving across country](page:fieldcraft-moving).
- The footpaths and access-land overlays on the box's map show what is open to you locally ([map](map:?overlay=footpaths&overlay=access-land)).
