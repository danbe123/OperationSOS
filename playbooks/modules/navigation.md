## Key facts

- A grid reference is two letters for the 100 km square, then eastings before northings: six figures place you within 100 m, eight within 10 m. For example, Ordnance Survey headquarters is SU 38726 14830 ([OS National Grid](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Ordnance_Survey_National_Grid)).
- Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland use the Irish Grid instead ([Irish grid reference system](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Irish_grid_reference_system)); the Channel Islands are referenced by latitude and longitude.
- Phones find a GPS position without any mobile network, but they cannot pass that position to this box over a plain connection, so use the offline map packs or read the grid reference straight off a paper map.

## What to do

1. Estimate walking time with Naismith's rule: 5 km an hour, plus one extra minute for every 10 m of climb ([Naismith's rule](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Naismith's_rule)).
2. Check the map's margin for the current magnetic variation before relying on a compass bearing; in the UK in 2026 it is small, roughly 0 to 2 degrees east, so it can otherwise be ignored for rough work ([magnetic declination](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Magnetic_declination)).
3. In an emergency call 999 and ask for police, then mountain rescue, or the coastguard if you are near the coast.
4. Signal distress with six whistle blasts or torch flashes a minute, then a minute's pause, and repeat ([FM 21-76, p. 134](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=134)).
5. Check the weather and remaining daylight before setting out, and treat every beach and estuary as a tidal trap: this box carries no tide tables, since the UK Hydrographic Office's tidal data is not openly licensed.

## UK specifics

- In England and Wales, public rights of way include footpaths, bridleways, restricted byways and byways open to all traffic, and CRoW open access land covers mountain, moor, heath, down and registered common ([CRoW s2](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/37/section/2)).
- Scotland instead has a right of responsible access to almost all land under the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 and the Scottish Outdoor Access Code ([s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2003/2/section/1)); Northern Ireland has very few public rights of way by comparison.
- The footpaths and access-land overlays on the box's map show what is open to you locally ([map](map:?overlay=footpaths&overlay=access-land)).
