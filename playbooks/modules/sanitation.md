## Key facts

- If the sewer still works, flush with a bucket of grey water rather than mains water. If it does not, use a bucket lined with a bag (keep urine separate) or dig a pit latrine at least 30 metres from any well, stream or spring, downhill of it, and cover each use with soil or ash ([Pit latrine](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Pit_latrine)).
- The 1980 government advice was a bucket with a lid for waste and a second bucket for other refuse ([Protect and Survive, p. 8](doc:protect-and-survive-1980#page=8)).
- Floodwater is contaminated with sewage: wash everything it has touched and throw away food it reached ([UKHSA flooding advice](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/flooding-and-health-public-advice/flooding-and-health-advice-for-the-public)).

## What to do

1. {{#if water}}Wash hands with soap for 20 seconds after the toilet, before eating and after handling waste, even when water is scarce.{{else}}Wash hands with soap for 20 seconds after the toilet, before eating and after handling waste: with the mains off, have someone pour a thin stream from a jug over a bowl, and keep that water for flushing.{{/if}}
2. Clean surfaces contaminated by vomit or faeces with a 0.5% chlorine solution (one part 5% thin bleach to nine parts water); use a weaker 0.05% solution on hands and skin ([Sodium hypochlorite](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Sodium_hypochlorite)).
3. Keep food waste sealed, and bury or burn paper and food scraps; never burn plastics. Rats spread leptospirosis, so do not let rubbish accumulate ([Leptospirosis](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Leptospirosis)).
4. Stock nappies and menstrual products in advance; they are easy to forget until they run out.
5. For a death in the household, confirm death, wear gloves, keep the body cool, and wrap and label it ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 12, p. 1](doc:scmg-ch12#page=1)); the full steps, registration, temporary burial and grief are on [Death and grief](page:death-and-grief).

{{#unless sewage}}
**Stop flushing.** With the sewer or its pumping station down, what you flush comes back, and the ground-floor toilet and the shower tray fill first. Use a lidded bucket lined with a bag, keeping urine separate so the solids stay dry and light, or dig a pit latrine at least 30 metres from any well, stream or spring and downhill of it, covering each use with soil or ash ([Pit latrine](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Pit_latrine)). Seal the bags, store them cool and away from food, and bury the contents when you safely can. Septic tanks and cesspools are the permanent answer ([Approved Document H, p. 31](doc:ad-h#page=31)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
**Hygiene is the first thing rationing kills, so protect it.** Keep one container for hand washing and another for everything else, and pour a thin stream from a jug over a bowl: it uses a fraction of what a running tap does, and the used water still flushes a toilet. Wash hands before food and after the toilet even on the days you skip the dishes; alcohol gel is a stopgap and does not work on visibly dirty hands. Grey water from the bath or the washing-up flushes a pan, a bucket at a time, poured fast ([Water module](module:water)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:storms-flooding}}
**Floodwater is sewage.** Keep children and pets out of it, wear wellingtons and gloves for anything that has been in it, and throw away food and drink it reached, including tins whose seals went under ([UKHSA flooding advice](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/flooding-and-health-public-advice/flooding-and-health-advice-for-the-public)). Once the water has gone, wash and disinfect every hard surface with the 0.5% chlorine solution above, and throw out carpet and soft furnishings that soaked. Cuts that met floodwater are cleaned and watched ([Wound cleaning card](card:wound-cleaning)); fever with muscle pain a week later can be leptospirosis, which rats spread ([Leptospirosis](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Leptospirosis)).
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:pandemic}}
**The sickroom's waste is the household's biggest hazard.** The sick person gets their own towel, cup, plate, bedding and lidded bin ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 6](doc:scmg-ch06)). Wear gloves for anything soiled, double-bag it, keep it away from the ordinary rubbish and wash your hands after touching the bag. Use the 0.5% chlorine solution above on anything vomit or faeces reach, and the weaker skin solution on hands when there is no soap and water. Wash bedding and clothes as hot as they will take and dry them outdoors or by the fire.
{{/if}}

## UK specifics

- Septic tanks, cesspools and drainage fields are the long-term answer once mains sewerage is unavailable for good ([Approved Document H, p. 31](doc:ad-h#page=31)).
- Burial when no undertaker can come, the distances to keep from wells and watercourses, and registering the death first are on [Death and grief](page:death-and-grief).
