## When to use

A twisted or overstretched joint or muscle: pain, swelling, bruising, hard to move, but not bent out of shape.

## Steps

1. Rest it. Stop what you were doing and take the weight off it.
2. Cold: ice or frozen peas in a damp cloth, never straight on the skin.
3. Compress with a crepe bandage, firm but not tight.
4. Ice for 10 to 15 minutes at a time, 3 or 4 times a day, until the swelling settles; longer than 20 minutes slows healing ([Sprain (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Sprain); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 16](doc:scmg-ch04#page=16)).
5. Elevate above the heart on cushions; rest and elevation for 2 to 3 days, then start gentle movement, because sprains do better with early movement than with long rest ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 16](doc:scmg-ch04#page=16); [Sprain (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Sprain)).
6. Painkillers: paracetamol, two 500 mg tablets up to 4 times in 24 hours ([NHS paracetamol](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/paracetamol-for-adults/how-and-when-to-take-paracetamol-for-adults/)); ibuprofen 200 to 400 mg up to 3 times a day with food ([NHS ibuprofen](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/ibuprofen-for-adults/)). Children: [fever in a child](card:fever-child) doses.
7. From the second or third day warmth and gentle movement help; cold on the first day, warm soaks afterwards ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 97](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=97)).
8. Cannot put weight on it, bone tender when pressed, numbness, or a limb bent the wrong way: treat as a [broken bone](card:broken-bones). When in doubt, assume a fracture ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 16](doc:scmg-ch04#page=16)).

{{#unless power}}
No freezer, so no frozen peas. A cloth wrung out in cold water and changed as it warms does the same job, and so does a bag of cold water, or a stream or a water butt in winter. Keep to the same 10 to 15 minutes at a time and never more than 20 ([Sprain (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Sprain)). Improvise the crepe bandage from a torn sheet if you must, firm but not tight, and check the fingers or toes beyond it for colour and feeling.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
Cooling a sprain is worth stored water only if you have plenty, because drinking comes first. A cloth wrung out in a cupful and re-wet as it warms is enough, and the same water can be used again for it. If water is short, leave the cooling out and rely on rest, a firm bandage and elevation, and on paracetamol for the pain ([NHS paracetamol](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/paracetamol-for-adults/)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Fingers or toes going white, blue or numb below the bandage: loosen it at once.
**Warning:** Ibuprofen is not for anyone with asthma, kidney, stomach or heart problems without advice ([NHS ibuprofen](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/ibuprofen-for-adults/)).

## Stop or escalate

A limb bent out of shape, bone through the skin, a white cold limb with no pulse below the injury — [[call 999]]. Cannot put weight on it, bone tender to press, or not improving after a few days — [[call 111]] or the urgent treatment centre for an X-ray. If nobody can come: splint it as for a fracture, rest and elevate for 2 to 3 days, then move it gently and support it with a bandage; a sprain heals in weeks and, even if a bone is cracked, the treatment at home is the same ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 16](doc:scmg-ch04#page=16); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 151](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=151)).

{{#unless phones}}
There is no X-ray, so treat it as a break whenever you cannot rule one out: splint it, rest it, elevate it and give paracetamol ([broken bones](card:broken-bones)). Nothing is lost by splinting a sprain and a great deal is lost by walking on a fracture. A limb bent out of shape, bone through the skin, or a limb below the injury that is white, cold or numb is a circulation problem where hours matter: carry them to whatever hospital is crewed ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)). Otherwise look at it every day, and take a leg that is no better in a week seriously.
{{/unless}}
