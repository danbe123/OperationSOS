## When to use

Splashed, doused or caught in a cloud of an unknown chemical; stinging eyes, coughing, burning skin.

## Steps

1. Get away from the source, upwind and uphill, into fresh air.
2. Remove their clothing; cut it off rather than pull it over the head.
3. Remove the chemical: blot and rub with any dry absorbent material.
4. If it is a powder, brush it off first; if it is caustic or in the eyes, rinse with lots of water.
5. {{#if water}}Then wash skin and hair with plenty of water, and soap if you have it, rinsing on while the skin itches or burns.{{else}}Then wash skin and hair with every litre you can pour, stored bottles, the bath, a water butt, rinsing on while the skin itches or burns ([Water module](module:water)).{{/if}}
6. Flush eyes with clean water for 15 minutes, eyelids held open ([eye injury](card:eye-injury)).
7. Bag the clothing and keep it away from people. Do not induce vomiting if the chemical was swallowed ([poisoning](card:poisoning)).

## Warnings

**Warning:** Do not enter a cellar, tank or confined space to rescue someone; the gas that dropped them will drop you.
**Warning:** Keep the wash water away from you and from drinking water.

## Stop or escalate

Breathing difficulty, burns, confusion, or any exposure to an industrial release — [[call 999]]. Name the substance if you know it. If nobody can come: keep washing until the stinging stops and then some; never make someone vomit after swallowing petrol, paraffin, acids or caustic soda, but if they are awake give water or milk to dilute it; nurse breathing trouble sitting up in the freshest air you have, and treat chemical burns as [burns](card:burns) ([WTIND, p. 152](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=152); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 2](doc:scmg-ch02)).
