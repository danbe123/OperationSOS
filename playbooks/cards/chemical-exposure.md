## When to use

Splashed, doused or caught in a cloud of an unknown chemical; stinging eyes, coughing, burning skin.

## Steps

1. Get away from the source, upwind and uphill, into fresh air.
2. Remove their clothing; cut it off rather than pull it over the head.
3. Remove the chemical: blot and rub with any dry absorbent material.
4. If it is a powder, brush it off first; if it is caustic or in the eyes, rinse with lots of water.
5. {{#if water}}Then wash skin and hair with plenty of water, and soap if you have it, rinsing on while the skin itches or burns.{{else}}Then wash skin and hair with every litre you can pour, stored bottles, the bath, a water butt, rinsing on while the skin itches or burns ([Water module](module:water)).{{/if}}
6. Flush eyes with clean water for 15 minutes, eyelids held open ([eye injury](card:eye-injury)).
7. Bag the clothing and keep it away from people. Do not induce vomiting if the chemical was swallowed ([poisoning](card:poisoning)).

{{#unless power}}
No electric shower, no extractor fan and no lights, so do this outdoors in daylight if you safely can, or by head torch, upwind of the house. Water does not have to be warm to work, but a wet, stripped person in a cold house gets hypothermic fast, so have dry clothes and blankets ready before you start and dry them straight afterwards ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)). Do not light a stove or a candle near anyone soaked in an unknown chemical; many are flammable ([Power module](module:power)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:chemical}}
Remove, remove, remove, and do it where you are rather than driving anywhere: get away from the cloud, upwind and uphill, clothing off and cut away, skin blotted and then rinsed ([What to do in a chemical emergency (UKHSA)](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/chemical-emergencies-information-for-the-public/what-to-do-in-a-chemical-emergency)). If the advice is to go in, stay in and tune in, shelter first, close windows and doors and turn off ventilation. Do not walk into the cloud to help somebody, and do not take contaminated clothing indoors with you ([chemical release](playbook:chemical)).
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Do not enter a cellar, tank or confined space to rescue someone; the gas that dropped them will drop you.
**Warning:** Keep the wash water away from you and from drinking water.

## Stop or escalate

Breathing difficulty, burns, confusion, or any exposure to an industrial release — [[call 999]]. Name the substance if you know it. If nobody can come: keep rinsing a chemical burn for about an hour rather than the twenty minutes a heat burn takes; never make someone vomit after swallowing petrol, paraffin, acids or caustic soda, and give them nothing at all to eat or drink, not even water or milk: rinse the mouth and spit it out ([Poisoning (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/poisoning/)); nurse breathing trouble sitting up in the freshest air you have, and treat chemical burns as [burns](card:burns) ([WTIND, p. 152](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=152); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 2](doc:scmg-ch02)).

{{#unless phones}}
No fire service is coming to decontaminate anyone, so nothing waits for them. Strip, blot and rinse the person outside the house, bag the clothes and leave the bag outside, then dry and warm them. Only after they are clean do you move them: carry or walk them to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health), with a written note of the chemical, the time and what you did, and take the container or a photograph of the label if you have one. Watch breathing for the rest of the day, because chest symptoms can come on hours later.
{{/unless}}
