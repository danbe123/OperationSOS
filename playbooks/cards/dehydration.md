## When to use

Dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness, headache, tiredness; in babies a sunken soft spot, few wet nappies.

## Steps

1. Sit them in the shade or a cool room and give water in small sips.
2. Make rehydration drink: 1 litre clean water, 6 level teaspoons sugar.
3. Add half a level teaspoon of salt. Stir until clear.
4. After each loose stool, a child under 2 gets 50 to 100 ml (a quarter to half a cup), a teaspoon at a time; a child of 2 to 10 gets 100 to 200 ml (half to a full cup) ([oral rehydration therapy](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Oral_rehydration_therapy)).
5. Older children and adults drink as much as they want, and at least 200 to 400 ml after each loose stool ([oral rehydration therapy](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Oral_rehydration_therapy)). Keep breastfeeding or milk feeds going, before other drinks.
6. {{#if water}}Keep going in small sips, day and night, until urine is pale and plentiful; an adult with diarrhoea needs three litres or more a day ([WTIND, p. 201](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=201)).{{else}}Keep going in small sips, day and night, until urine is pale and plentiful; an adult with diarrhoea needs three litres or more a day ([WTIND, p. 201](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=201)), boiled or treated first because the mains is off ([Water module](module:water)).{{/if}}
7. Bought sachets (Dioralyte and similar) work the same way; follow the packet.
8. Avoid alcohol, strong coffee and very sugary drinks; they make it worse.

{{#unless power}}
No kettle: boil on the gas hob lit with a match, or on a camping stove used in a ventilated place, and let it cool covered before mixing the drink. Measure the sugar and salt with a spoon, not by eye, and make it up fresh each day rather than keeping it in a warm fridge that has stopped. Keep the drink covered and out of the sun, work by torch, and mark the jug so you can see how much has actually gone in ([Water module](module:water); [Power module](module:power)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Too much salt is dangerous; measure the half teaspoon, and taste it: it should be no saltier than tears.
**Warning:** A baby under a year with diarrhoea can become dangerously dry within hours.

## Stop or escalate

Drowsy or confused, passing little or no urine, unable to keep fluids down, a fast weak pulse, sunken eyes or skin that stays pinched up, or a baby with a sunken soft spot or dry nappies ([WTIND, p. 200](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=200)) — [[call 999]]. {{#if phones}}For a milder case where they are still alert and drinking, [[call 111]].{{else}}A milder case still needs someone who can treat them: the [medical module](module:medical), and a runner to the nearest help.{{/if}} If nobody can come: keep giving the drink in sips every few minutes, day and night, even if they vomit, because not all of it comes back; someone who cannot swallow can be given the drink very slowly as an enema ([WTIND, p. 201](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=201); [WTIND, p. 65](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=65)).

{{#unless phones}}
Nobody is coming, so somebody sits with them and counts. Write down every drink given and every wet nappy or trip to the toilet, and check every hour: pale plentiful urine is winning, no urine for many hours with sunken eyes and a fast weak pulse is losing. If they are losing after a full day of patient sipping, or cannot be roused, carry them to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health) with the drink and the notes, and keep giving sips on the way.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:pandemic}}
Most people who die at home in an outbreak die dry, not of the illness itself, so fluids are the treatment you can actually give. One named carer, apron, gloves and a mask if you hold them, hands washed before and after every contact, and the sick person's cup, jug and bowl kept apart and washed separately ([Sanitation module](module:sanitation)). Keep the drink going through fever and vomiting, a spoonful at a time. Fever with confusion, a fast breathing rate or mottled skin is [sepsis](card:sepsis), not dehydration.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:heat-drought}}
In heat the water goes out through sweat before anyone feels thirsty, so drink to a timetable rather than to thirst, and add the salt: water alone in heavy sweating brings on cramps. Rest in the coolest, shaded part of the house through the middle of the day and work early or late ([heat stroke](card:heat-stroke)). When water is rationed, the sick, the very young and the very old get theirs first, and washing water is reused for flushing rather than drinking ([Water module](module:water)).
{{/if}}
