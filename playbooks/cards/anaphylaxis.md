## When to use

Swollen face, lips or throat, wheeze or trouble breathing, hives or faintness after a sting, food or medicine.

## Steps

1. Use their adrenaline pen now: outer thigh, through clothing.
2. Hold in place: Jext 10 seconds, EpiPen 3 seconds. Then [[call 999]].
3. Lie them flat with legs raised; sit them up only if breathing is hard.
4. A pregnant woman lies on her left side. Do not let them stand or walk.
5. If no better after 5 minutes, give a second pen if there is one.
6. Loosen tight clothing. Note the time of each dose for the paramedics.
7. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing, start [CPR](card:cpr-adult).

{{#unless power}}
Work by head torch, not a candle: you have to see the outer thigh and the pen's window. Adrenaline pens live at room temperature in a pocket or a bag, never in the fridge and never where they freeze or sit against a stove, and a pen whose liquid has gone cloudy or brown is past use if you hold another. Write the time of each dose on paper, because there is no phone clock to check it against later ([Anaphylaxis (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Antihistamines are not a treatment for anaphylaxis; adrenaline is.
**Warning:** Standing up suddenly during anaphylaxis can cause collapse and death.

## Stop or escalate

This is always an emergency, and the message is "anaphylaxis" — [[call 999]]. Hospital observation is needed even after recovery because a second reaction can follow hours later. If nobody can come: give the second pen after 5 minutes if there is no improvement (a second dose is needed in up to a third of cases), keep them lying down with the legs raised and give fluids to drink once they can swallow, and watch them for a full 24 hours, because symptoms return in some people between 1 and 72 hours later ([Anaphylaxis (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Anaphylaxis); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 96](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=96)). Stings and bites that cause it are on the [bites and stings card](card:bites-stings).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming, so the plan is yours and it is a long one. Give the second pen after five minutes if there is no improvement, keep them flat with the legs raised, and do not let them stand, walk to the toilet or be left alone. Do not set off anywhere while they are still reacting. Once they are steady, carry them rather than walk them to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health), with a written note of the trigger and the time of every dose. Watch them for a full 24 hours ([Anaphylaxis (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Anaphylaxis)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:supply-chain}}
Pens are the hardest thing in the house to replace, so keep two together on the person at all times, write the expiry dates on the [household plan](page:household-plan) and check them monthly, and show everyone in the house how to fire one before it is needed. An antihistamine has never been a substitute. An out-of-date pen may have lost strength, so keep it behind an in-date one rather than instead of it, and if a reaction starts, avoiding the known trigger completely matters more than usual, because there may be no second chance ([Anaphylaxis (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/)).
{{/if}}
