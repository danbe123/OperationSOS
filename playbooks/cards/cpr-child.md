## When to use

A child or baby does not respond and is not breathing normally.

## Steps

1. Shout for help. Alone? One minute of CPR, then [[call 999]].
2. Tilt the head back a little, lift the chin, and give 5 rescue breaths.
3. Baby: seal your mouth over mouth and nose. Child: pinch the nose.
4. Press the centre of the chest 30 times at 100 to 120 a minute: a baby with two fingers about 4 cm deep, or, if there are two of you, with both thumbs and your hands wrapped round the chest; a child with the heel of one hand about 5 cm deep (a third of the chest) ([infant CPR, MedlinePlus](kiwix:medlineplus.gov_en_all/medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000011.htm)).
5. Give 2 breaths, then 30 more presses. Keep going 30 to 2.
6. After the first minute, if nobody else has, {{#if phones}}[[call 999]]{{else}}send someone to a landline, a neighbour or a payphone: 999 will not connect from here{{/if}}. Use a defibrillator if one arrives (paediatric pads or setting if available; adult pads if not).
7. Do not stop until the child breathes normally or help takes over.

{{#unless power}}
Nothing here needs the mains: a defibrillator has its own battery and works in a blackout, with paediatric pads or setting if there are any and adult pads if not. Put a torch on the floor pointing at the chest rather than holding it, clear the space round the child first, and let someone else deal with the light while you keep going. Keep the box's power bank for the torch ([Power module](module:power)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
A small child on a cold floor loses heat very fast, and cold makes everything that follows worse. Work on a firm surface but put a coat, a mat or cardboard under them if you can do it without stopping the compressions, and once they are breathing again, dry them, wrap them and hold them against a warm adult under blankets ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Do not tilt a baby's head far back; a neutral position keeps the airway open.
**Warning:** Never shake a child to wake them.

## Stop or escalate

Someone else raises the alarm straight away, otherwise you go after one minute of CPR — [[call 999]]. Stop only when the child breathes normally (then the [recovery position](card:recovery-position)) or a paramedic takes over. If nobody can come: children most often arrest because they stopped breathing, from drowning, choking, smoke or suffocation, and those are the cases where long CPR is worth it because the heart can restart once oxygen returns; without a defibrillator and with no response at all after 20 minutes it is reasonable to stop ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 96](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=96)). A child who is drowsy or fitting with a fever is on the [fever in a child card](card:fever-child).

{{#unless phones}}
Alone with a child and no phone, the minute of CPR still comes first, then you shout, bang on walls and doors, or carry the child to the nearest person while doing what you can, rather than leaving them to run for help that cannot be phoned for. With two of you, one presses and one goes. Send whoever runs to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health) or a fire station with the child's age and what happened written down ([getting help without phones](page:no-phones)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:storms-flooding}}
Children drown in floodwater in gardens, ditches and flooded cellars, often within sight of the house, and this is the commonest reason a child needs CPR in a flood. Give the 5 rescue breaths first, before any compressions, because the problem is oxygen ([drowning](card:drowning)). Keep going a long time: a cold child pulled from cold water has the best chance of the long resuscitations. Strip the wet clothes and wrap them the moment they breathe ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)).
{{/if}}
