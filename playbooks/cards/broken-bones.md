## When to use

Pain, swelling, a limb bent the wrong way, grating, or unable to move or bear weight after an injury.

## Steps

1. Keep the injured part still in the position you found it.
2. Do not try to straighten it or push bone back in.
3. Support it with padding: cushions, rolled clothes, a sling for an arm.
4. Splint a leg to the other leg or to a board with padding between, tying above and below the break, not over it.
5. Cover any open wound with a clean dressing and control bleeding with pressure around, not on, the bone.
6. {{#if power}}Wrapped ice or a cold pack for 20 minutes eases swelling.{{else}}With the freezer off, a cloth wrung out in cold water and changed often eases swelling.{{/if}}
7. Check colour, warmth and feeling below the injury every few minutes; loosen ties if fingers or toes go white or numb. If it may only be a sprain, see [sprains and strains](card:sprains-strains).

{{#unless power}}
Splint by head torch, propped or held by someone else so that both of your hands are free, and judge the fingers or toes by touch as much as by sight: warm and soft is good, cold, hard or numb means the ties are too tight. Lay everything out before you start, because you cannot search a dark house one-handed with someone in pain in front of you ([WTIND, p. 147](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=147)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
Someone lying still with a broken bone goes cold quickly, and cold makes shock worse. Insulate them from the floor with a mat, cardboard or spare clothes, cover them and their head, and nurse them in the warmest room in the house rather than where they fell ([shock](card:shock); [hypothermia](card:hypothermia)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Do not move someone with a suspected neck, back or pelvis injury unless they are in danger ([spinal injury](card:spinal-injury)).
**Warning:** Do not give food or drink; they may need an anaesthetic.

## Stop or escalate

An open fracture, a thigh, hip, pelvis, spine or skull injury, an obvious deformity, numbness or a white cold limb below the injury, or heavy bleeding — [[call 999]]. If nobody can come: a well-padded splint stays on for about a month for an arm, with no strain on it for another month, and about two months for a leg with no weight on it; a broken thigh or hip is splinted to the whole body; an open fracture is washed gently with cool boiled water, the bone is never pushed back until wound and bone are clean, and an antibiotic is started at once if you hold one ([WTIND, p. 147](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=147); [WTIND, p. 148](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=148)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming, so splint first and move second. Pad and tie the limb where it lies, check the fingers or toes, give paracetamol, then decide: an arm can walk out, a leg, hip or pelvis cannot and needs carrying. Take them to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health) with a written note of the time, how it happened and what you have given. If nothing is reachable, the splint and the month of rest above are the treatment, and you watch for a white, cold or numb limb, which cannot wait.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless roads}}
With the roads blocked you are carrying them. Make a stretcher from two poles and three coats, or use a door, a wheelbarrow or a garden trolley, and pad under the splint; four carriers if you have them, feet first downhill, swapping often: that is mountain-rescue practice rather than published guidance, but it is what keeps a stretcher level and a carry going. Tie the person on so a stumble does not throw them off. It is slow, so send a runner ahead to say who is coming and with what ([getting help without phones](page:no-phones); [Rope, knots and tools](page:fieldcraft-rope-tools)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:storms-flooding}}
Most bones broken in a flood are broken by slipping on wet ground, on stairs and on debris in the dark, so this is the injury you will actually meet. Never walk or carry anyone through moving water, which hides holes and open drains. Get them out of the wet before you splint, strip the soaked clothes and cover them, because a wet casualty who is lying still is on the way to hypothermia ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia); [storms and flooding](playbook:storms-flooding)).
{{/if}}
