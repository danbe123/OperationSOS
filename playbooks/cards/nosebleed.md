## When to use

Blood from one or both nostrils after a knock, nose-picking, a cold or dry air, or for no reason at all.

## Steps

1. Sit up and lean forward. Blow the nose gently once to clear clots.
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose firmly, just below the bone.
3. Keep pinching without letting go; breathe through the mouth.
4. Time it: hold for 10 to 15 minutes by the clock, and spit out any blood rather than swallowing it ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 132](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=132); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 185](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=185)).
5. Still bleeding: pinch for another 15 minutes ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 185](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=185)). Bleeding that continues after 30 minutes of pressure needs help ([Nosebleed (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Nosebleed)).
6. Once it stops: no blowing, picking, hot drinks, bending or lifting for the rest of the day. Petroleum jelly inside the nostrils twice a day if it bleeds often ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 132](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=132)).
7. Do not tip the head back or lie down: blood runs down the throat and is swallowed or breathed in ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 132](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=132)).

{{#unless power}}
Work by torch light, held by somebody else or worn on your head, because packing a nostril takes both your hands. Ice went with the freezer, and it was never the treatment anyway: the pinch is. Time the 10 to 15 minutes by a wind-up clock or a watch rather than a phone you are saving, and call the minutes out loud, because holding on without letting go feels far longer than it is.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
You will have blood on your hands. Wash them before and after with soap and stored or boiled water poured over them by somebody else, rather than dipping them in a bowl, or use hand sanitiser if you hold it ([hygiene without a tap](page:fieldcraft-hygiene)). Do not rinse the mouth or the nose with untreated water; spitting into a bowl is enough, and the bowl goes outside afterwards.
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** After a head injury, thin watery bloodstained fluid from the nose is a skull fracture, not a nosebleed: do not pinch or pack, see [head injury](card:head-injury) ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 7](doc:scmg-ch04#page=7)).
**Warning:** Anyone on blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban) bleeds longer and needs checking if it does not stop.

## Stop or escalate

Bleeding still heavy after two full 15-minute pinches, which is the point the NHS says to seek help ([Nosebleed (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Nosebleed)), a large amount lost, feeling faint or pale, difficulty breathing, or a nosebleed after a head injury — [[call 999]] or get to A&E ([severe bleeding](card:severe-bleeding)). Frequent nosebleeds, or one in someone on blood thinners — [[call 111]], or in Northern Ireland the GP out-of-hours service ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)). If nobody can come: pack the nostril with ribbon gauze or a strip of clean cloth well smeared with petroleum jelly, fed in flat along the floor of the nose (not upwards) until the nostril is full, then take it out gently at 24 hours if the bleeding has stopped and never leave it in beyond 48, because a pack left longer needs antibiotic cover and risks toxic shock ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 13](doc:scmg-ch04#page=13)); an older person bleeding from the back of the nose sits leaning forward and tries not to swallow until it clots ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 132](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=132)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming. A nosebleed still going after two full 15-minute pinches is packed as above; take the pack out at 24 hours if the bleeding has stopped, and never leave it in beyond 48 hours. Watch the person rather than the towels: pale, clammy, faint on sitting up, or a fast weak pulse means real blood loss ([shock](card:shock)). Somebody swallowing blood will vomit it, so keep them leaning forward. If they are still bleeding heavily after packing, or going pale, carry them to the nearest crewed hospital ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)) with a note of when it started.
{{/unless}}
