## When to use

A blow or fall on the head: dazed, knocked out, vomiting, headache, confused, or a cut or dent on the scalp.

## Steps

1. Unresponsive? Check breathing. Not breathing: [CPR](card:cpr-adult).
2. Keep the head and neck still after a fall, dive or hard blow.
3. Sit them down. Wrapped cold pack on the bump; press a pad on a cut.
4. Ask what happened, the day and where they are. Not knowing, slurred speech, drowsiness or unusual behaviour means a brain injury until proved otherwise ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 6](doc:scmg-ch04#page=6)).
5. If the skull feels dented or something is in the wound, do not press on it: put a dressing over it and pad and bandage around it ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 1, p. 33](doc:scmg-ch01#page=33)).
6. Compare the pupils with a torch. One wide pupil that does not shrink, or weakness on one side of the body, means a bleed pressing on the brain — [[call 999]] ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 7](doc:scmg-ch04#page=7)).
7. If they were unconscious and are breathing, put them in the [recovery position](card:recovery-position) keeping the neck in line with the body, and watch for vomiting or fits; treat a neck injury as present until proved otherwise ([spinal injury](card:spinal-injury); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 7](doc:scmg-ch04#page=7)).
8. Someone stays with them through the first night and checks they can be roused and answer sensibly; a person can seem fine and then deteriorate ([Head injury (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Head_injury)). Paracetamol is fine for the headache; no alcohol, no driving and no sport until fully recovered ([Concussion (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Concussion)).

{{#unless power}}
Checking the pupils needs light. Use a head torch or the box's torch rather than a phone you are saving, keep the beam off the eye until the moment you check, and check only when a check is due, to save the batteries. A cloth wrung out in cold water does the work of the ice pack you no longer have. Keep the torch and a spare set of batteries beside whoever is on watch.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
A head injury in a cold house is two problems at once. Someone lying still in an unheated room cools quickly, and the drowsiness, clumsiness and confusion of being cold look exactly like the brain injury you are watching for ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)). Keep them in the warmest room, insulated from the floor, in dry clothes with a hat, blankets over and under, and give a warm sweet drink once they are alert and swallowing. Take the temperature of the room into account before you decide they are deteriorating.
{{/unless}}

{{#if dark}}
Keep one torch by the bed all night and the spare batteries beside it. Shade the light so the room stays dim enough for them to sleep between checks, and bring the torch close only to look at the pupils and their colour. Clear the route to the door and to the bucket, because a confused person with a head injury falls in the dark and a second knock on the head is the thing you are trying to avoid.
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Clear or bloodstained fluid from the nose or ear, bruising around both eyes or behind the ears, or a dent in the skull means a skull fracture — [[call 999]] ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 7](doc:scmg-ch04#page=7)).
**Warning:** Anyone on blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban), anyone drunk, a baby or an older person needs checking even after a knock that seems minor.

## Stop or escalate

Knocked out even briefly, drowsy or hard to wake, confused, vomiting more than once, a fit, unequal pupils, weakness or numbness, a worsening headache, fluid from the ear or nose, a dented skull, or a fall from height or road crash — [[call 999]]. A headache, sickness or dizziness that is not settling — [[call 111]]. If nobody can come: rest them half-sitting, nothing by mouth while drowsy, and check response, pupils and breathing regularly, day and night, until they have been themselves for a full day; a bleed inside the skull cannot be treated at home and time will show whether they recover ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 298](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=298); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 126](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=126)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming, so the watch is the treatment. Two people take turns: rouse them every hour for the first six hours and every two hours for the rest of the day and night, enough to give their name and squeeze both your hands, and look at the pupils each time. Write down every check with the time. If they cannot be roused, vomit repeatedly, have a fit, or one pupil stays wide, carry them lying flat with the head slightly raised to the nearest crewed hospital ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)), and send someone ahead with the time of the injury and what you have seen.
{{/unless}}
