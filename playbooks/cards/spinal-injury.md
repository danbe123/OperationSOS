## When to use

A fall, dive, crash or heavy blow to the head or back; neck or back pain, tingling, numbness or weakness.

## Steps

1. Tell them not to move. Do not move them unless they are in danger.
2. Kneel behind the head and hold it still, a hand each side.
3. Get help coming — [[call 999]]. Keep them warm and talking.
4. Keep the head, neck and back in a straight line. If you have a collar fit it; otherwise rolled towels or clothes each side of the head keep the neck still while you wait ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 7](doc:scmg-ch04#page=7); [Emergency War Surgery, p. 334](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=334)).
5. If they must be moved, one person holds the head and the others roll the body as one block, spine straight, onto a board, door or blanket; never bend the neck or back ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 148](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=148); [Spinal precautions (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Spinal_precautions)).
6. Unresponsive but breathing: open the airway with a jaw thrust (fingers behind the angles of the jaw, lift it forward) rather than tilting the head ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 89](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=89)). If they vomit or you must leave them, roll them into the [recovery position](card:recovery-position) with the head held in line.
7. Not breathing: start [CPR](card:cpr-adult). The airway comes before the spine.
8. Note whether they can move fingers and toes and feel your touch, and tell the crew if it changes. Cover wounds and control [bleeding](card:severe-bleeding); electric shock and falls from height injure the spine too ([electric shock](card:electric-shock); [Emergency War Surgery, p. 410](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=410)).

{{#unless power}}
You will be working outdoors or by torch. Put a light on the casualty and another on the route out before anybody moves, and brief everyone on what the count will be. With no ambulance trolley, hoist or lift, plan the carry to the last step: doorways, stairs, gates, and exactly where they will lie at the end. Rolled towels or clothing on each side of the head do the work of a collar, and a door off its hinges does the work of a board.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
Somebody lying still on a cold floor for hours will get cold, and a high spinal injury can take away both the shivering and the body's control of its own temperature, so they cool without shivering at all ([Spinal cord injury (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Spinal_cord_injury); [hypothermia](card:hypothermia)). Insulate underneath, blankets over, a hat, and feel whether they are warm on the chest every time you check them. Never put a heater or a hot water bottle against skin they cannot feel, because they will not feel it burn.
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Never sit them up, lift them by the arms and legs, or turn the head to look at a wound.
**Warning:** Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness below the injury, means the cord may be damaged: every movement matters.

## Stop or escalate

Any suspected spinal injury — [[call 999]]; ask for the police and mountain rescue if you are on a hill or coast ([calling for rescue](page:fieldcraft-rescue)). If nobody can come: keep them flat on a firm surface with the neck supported, roll them as one block every two hours to prevent pressure sores, keep the skin clean and dry, help them empty the bladder, and watch the breathing, which weakens with high neck injuries; a stable fracture heals with weeks of rest, but nothing done at home repairs a cut cord ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 434](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=434); [Emergency War Surgery, p. 333](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=333); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 148](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=148)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance and no spinal board, so decide early whether to move them: a person on a cold road or in a wrecked car has to be moved, and a person indoors and warm is left where they are until you have enough hands. Moving takes at least four people, one at the head giving the orders, rolling the body as one block onto a door, a ladder or a rigid board, then padding and strapping the head, chest, hips and legs. After that it is weeks of nursing: rolled as one block every two hours, skin kept clean and dry, the bladder emptied, and the breathing watched ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 434](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=434)).
{{/unless}}
