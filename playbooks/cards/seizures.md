## When to use

Someone is shaking or jerking uncontrollably, has gone stiff, or is unresponsive with twitching or staring.

## Steps

1. Note the time it started. Move hard objects away.
2. Cushion the head with something soft. Do not hold them down.
3. Do not put anything in their mouth. Loosen clothing at the neck.
4. Stay with them and let the seizure run its course.
5. When the jerking stops, put them in the [recovery position](card:recovery-position) and check breathing.
6. Stay until they are fully awake; they will be confused and tired. Tell them what happened.
7. If they have a known epilepsy plan or rescue medicine, follow it. A fit in a hot, feverish child is on the [fever in a child card](card:fever-child).

{{#unless power}}
Epilepsy medicines are never stopped suddenly, and a missed dose is the commonest cause of a fit, so keep the tablets where you can find them in the dark and take them at the usual times by a wind-up clock ([Chronic conditions](page:chronic-conditions)). Check now where the rescue medicine is kept and whether it needs cool storage. Clear the floor around where they sleep and sit, because a fit in an unlit room with the furniture moved for the blackout is what causes the injuries.
{{/unless}}

{{#if dark}}
Do not shine a torch in their face during the fit. Stand it on the floor pointing at the ceiling so you can see the room without dazzling anyone, and clear the space by feel: chairs, the stove, anything hot, anything with a corner. Afterwards you do need light on them, to see their colour and their breathing, so bring the torch close then and stay until they are properly awake and know where they are.
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** A seizure lasting more than 5 minutes, or one after another without waking, is a medical emergency.
**Warning:** Do not give food, drink or tablets until they are fully awake.

## Stop or escalate

Over 5 minutes, one seizure after another, injury, in water, a first seizure, pregnancy or diabetes, or breathing that does not return to normal — [[call 999]]. If nobody can come: let them sleep it off on their side; give their rescue medicine as their plan says, and a second dose if the fit has not stopped after 15 minutes; and look for a cause you can treat, which in a child is usually fever or dehydration and in someone with epilepsy is usually missed tablets, because epilepsy medicines are never stopped suddenly ([WTIND, p. 227](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=227); [Chronic conditions](page:chronic-conditions)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming, but a fit lasting more than 5 minutes, or one fit after another without waking, is still an emergency. Give their rescue medicine if they have one, and a second dose after 15 minutes if the fit has not stopped. After that there is nothing more to give: keep them on their side, keep the airway clear, and stay with them while somebody else goes for help ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)). A first fit, a fit in pregnancy, a fit in water, or a fit with a real injury needs a hospital as soon as one can be reached.
{{/unless}}
