## When to use

Someone is shaking or jerking uncontrollably, has gone stiff, or is unresponsive with twitching or staring.

## Steps

1. Note the time it started. Move hard objects away.
2. Cushion the head with something soft. Do not hold them down.
3. Do not put anything in their mouth. Loosen clothing at the neck.
4. Stay with them and let the seizure run its course.
5. When the jerking stops, put them in the [recovery position](card:recovery-position) and check breathing.
6. Stay until they are fully awake; they will be confused and tired. Tell them what happened.
7. If they have a known epilepsy plan or rescue medicine, follow it. A fit in a hot, feverish child is on the [fever in a child card](card:fever-child).

## Warnings

**Warning:** A seizure lasting more than 5 minutes, or one after another without waking, is a medical emergency.
**Warning:** Do not give food, drink or tablets until they are fully awake.

## Stop or escalate

Over 5 minutes, one seizure after another, injury, in water, a first seizure, pregnancy or diabetes, or breathing that does not return to normal — [[call 999]]. If nobody can come: let them sleep it off on their side; give their rescue medicine as their plan says, and a second dose if the fit has not stopped after 15 minutes; and look for a cause you can treat, which in a child is usually fever or dehydration and in someone with epilepsy is usually missed tablets, because epilepsy medicines are never stopped suddenly ([WTIND, p. 227](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=227); [Chronic conditions](page:chronic-conditions)).
