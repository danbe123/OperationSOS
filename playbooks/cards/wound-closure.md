## When to use

A cut whose edges gape, once the bleeding is controlled and it is clean; deciding whether and how to close it.

## Steps

1. Stop the bleeding first with firm pressure on a clean pad.
2. Clean it thoroughly ([wound cleaning](card:wound-cleaning)).
3. Close only if it is clean, fresh, and the edges meet without pulling.
4. Do not close: bites, punctures, crush wounds, dirty wounds, wounds older than 6 hours, anything with grit or glass left inside, or a wound already red or oozing; dress these open and let them heal from the bottom ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 4](doc:scmg-ch04#page=4); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 135](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=135)).
5. Strips (Steri-Strips, or plaster tape cut into narrow strips): dry the skin, stick one end beside the wound, pull the edges together, stick the other side; repeat along the cut with gaps between strips for fluid to escape ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 3](doc:scmg-ch04#page=3)).
6. Cover with a dry dressing and keep it dry. Leave strips on for 5 days on the face, 10 on the body and 14 on a hand or foot, the same as stitches ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 135](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=135)).
7. Check daily. Redness, heat, pus, a red line or fever: take the strips off, let it open and treat as infected ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 135](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=135); [sepsis](card:sepsis)).
8. Stitches only when no help is coming, the wound is deep, clean and gapes despite strips, and you follow Where There Is No Doctor: boil a needle and nylon thread for 20 minutes, start in the middle, stitches at least 1 cm apart and 0.6 cm from the edge, never over a hollow beneath ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 135](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=135); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 4](doc:scmg-ch04#page=4)).

{{#unless power}}
Closing a wound is fine work and cannot be done badly lit. A head torch on you, a second torch held on the wound by somebody else, and everything laid out first: strips or a boiled needle and thread, dry gauze, tape, scissors. Boil the instruments for 20 minutes on gas or on a stove used outdoors and let them cool covered ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 135](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=135)). If you cannot see it properly, do not close it: dress it open and wait.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
A wound that cannot be washed out properly is not closed. Rinsing takes more clean water than people expect, so pour boiled and cooled or bottled water over it from a jug or bottle rather than dipping anything in a bowl, and keep fresh water for the last rinse ([wound cleaning](card:wound-cleaning); [water disinfection](page:water-disinfection)). If all you have is a little, spend it on the wound and clean your own hands with sanitiser.
{{/unless}}

{{#if dark}}
Wait for daylight if you safely can. A cut that has been cleaned and dressed open can be closed tomorrow, and delayed closure is a proper technique rather than a failure ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 124](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=124)). If you cannot wait, put the light on the wound and not in your own eyes, have a second person hold it steady, and stop and dress it open the moment you can no longer see what you are doing.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:long-rebuild}}
With no surgeon and no follow-up, the rule against closing dirty, old, bite, puncture and crush wounds becomes absolute: a closed infected wound becomes an abscess and then sepsis ([sepsis](card:sepsis)). Learn and practise delayed primary closure instead: clean it, pack it loosely, re-dress on alternate days, and close on day three to five if it is clean and pink ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 124](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=124)). Keep the suture materials for the wounds that really need them and use adhesive strips wherever they will hold.
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Deep cuts to the face, hands, over a joint, or with numbness or a finger that will not move need a professional: nerves and tendons may be cut.
**Warning:** When in doubt, do not close it. A wound left open heals; a dirty wound closed becomes an abscess ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 4](doc:scmg-ch04#page=4)).

## Stop or escalate

Bleeding that will not stop with pressure, or a deep wound of the neck, chest or belly — [[call 999]] ([severe bleeding](card:severe-bleeding)). A gaping cut, glass in a wound, a cut on the face or hand, or numbness or weakness beyond it — [[call 111]], or in Northern Ireland the GP out-of-hours service, or the urgent treatment centre within hours ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)), while it can still be closed. If nobody can come: pack a dirty or old wound loosely with damp clean gauze, re-dress it on alternate days, and close it by delayed primary closure on day four or five once it is clean and pink ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 124](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=124); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 4](doc:scmg-ch04#page=4)); bites stay open ([bites and stings](card:bites-stings)).

{{#unless phones}}
There is no urgent treatment centre to reach within hours, so the decision is yours, and the safe answer is usually not to close it. Clean it thoroughly, dress it open, and look again on day four or five: a wound that is clean and pink can be closed then, which is safer than closing it now ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 124](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=124)). A cut over a joint, on the face, or with numbness or a finger that will not move still needs a professional, so carry them to whatever hospital is crewed ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)).
{{/unless}}
