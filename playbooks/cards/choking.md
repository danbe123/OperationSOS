## When to use

Someone cannot breathe, cough or speak, is clutching their throat, or is turning blue.

## Steps

1. If they can cough, tell them to keep coughing. Stay with them.
2. If not: lean them forward, 5 hard blows between the shoulder blades.
3. Check the mouth after each blow and remove anything you can see.
4. If still choking: stand behind, fist above the navel, other hand over it, pull sharply inwards and upwards 5 times.
5. Keep alternating 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts.
6. Baby under one: lay face down along your forearm, head low, 5 back blows; then face up, 5 chest thrusts with two fingers on the breastbone. Never abdominal thrusts on a baby.
7. If they become unresponsive, start CPR ([adult](card:cpr-adult) or [child/baby](card:cpr-child)), and {{#if phones}}[[call 999]]{{else}}send someone to a landline, a neighbour or a payphone: 999 will not connect from here{{/if}}.

{{#unless power}}
Your hands are the treatment and they need no mains, but you do need to see. Get a torch pointed at the mouth before you look, and never sweep a finger round blindly in the dark. Do not let anyone eat alone by candlelight, feed small children sitting down where you can see them, cut food small when you are cooking on a camping stove in poor light, and keep a torch where you eat as well as where you sleep.
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Anyone who has had abdominal thrusts must be checked by a doctor afterwards.
**Warning:** Do not sweep the mouth blindly with a finger.

## Stop or escalate

If the blockage does not clear after the first cycle, or the person becomes unresponsive — [[call 999]]. If nobody can come: an unconscious person is laid on their back and the thrusts given with the heel of your hand on the belly (on the chest for a pregnant woman or a small child), then rescue breaths, and you keep cycling thrusts and breaths ([WTIND, p. 128](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=128)); once the airway clears, anyone who had abdominal thrusts, or who still coughs, wheezes or has chest or belly pain, is watched for a day for injury or for something left in the lung ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 1, p. 14](doc:scmg-ch01#page=14)).

{{#unless phones}}
Nobody can be called and nobody would arrive in time anyway: choking is over in minutes either way, so do not send anyone for help until the airway is clear. Once it is, the abdominal thrusts still have to be answered for. Take anyone who had thrusts, and anyone still coughing, wheezing or holding their chest or belly, to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health) when you can, and until then keep them in sight and sitting up, letting them eat and drink normally unless swallowing hurts, which is itself a reason to get them looked at ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 1, p. 14](doc:scmg-ch01#page=14)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:famine}}
Hunger is a choking risk in itself: people bolt food when there has not been enough, and dry, coarse or unfamiliar food goes down badly. Eat sitting up, slowly, with water to hand, cut everything small for children and older people, and never let a hungry child eat alone or run about with food in their mouth. Someone weak or exhausted may not be able to cough hard enough to clear their own throat, so stay with them while they eat. None of this is published guidance; it is plain sense about how people eat when there has not been enough ([Food module](module:food)).
{{/if}}
