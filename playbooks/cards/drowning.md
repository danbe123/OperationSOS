## When to use

Pulled from water and not breathing normally, or coughing, confused or exhausted after a near-drowning.

## Steps

1. Do not go in. Reach with a pole or throw something that floats.
2. Ask for coastguard at sea, fire service inland — [[call 999]].
3. Once out: check breathing for up to 10 seconds.
4. If not breathing normally: give 5 rescue breaths first, then carry on as on the [adult CPR](card:cpr-adult) or [child and baby CPR](card:cpr-child) card, 30 presses to 2 breaths.
5. Expect vomiting; turn them on their side to clear it, then continue.
6. If they breathe, put them in the [recovery position](card:recovery-position), strip wet clothes and cover them; treat as [hypothermia](card:hypothermia).
7. Use a defibrillator if one arrives; dry the chest first.

{{#unless power}}
A defibrillator runs on its own battery, so a blackout does not stop it. Everything else is warmth without the mains: dry clothes, blankets and sleeping bags, a hat, body heat from another person under the covers, and warm sweet drinks made on the gas hob or a camping stove once they are fully awake. Do not put them in a hot bath and do not use a hair dryer or an electric blanket even if the power comes back ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
Anyone pulled from cold water is already cooling, and a cold house finishes the job. Strip the wet clothes, dry them, wrap them including the head, insulate them from the floor, and warm the trunk first rather than the arms and legs. Handle them gently and keep them lying flat, because a very cold heart stops easily ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Anyone rescued from water must be seen by a doctor even if they seem fine; lung damage can appear hours later.
**Warning:** Cold water shock kills swimmers in minutes; do not attempt a swimming rescue.

## Stop or escalate

Anyone pulled from the water needs help coming — [[call 999]]. Continue CPR until help takes over; cold-water drowning victims have survived long resuscitations. If nobody can come: keep going, because drowning is the case where long CPR is worth it: the heart can restart once oxygen gets back in ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 96](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=96)); anyone revived is rewarmed slowly, nursed sitting up once awake, and watched for a day for breathing trouble ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 11, p. 1](doc:scmg-ch11#page=1)).

{{#unless phones}}
No coastguard, no fire service, no ambulance, so the rescue is reach or throw from the bank: the RNLI's rule is that you do not go in the water yourself, not even the shallows, and never swim to them. Once they are out, breaths first, then CPR, and keep going far longer than for a collapse on dry land. Anyone revived still needs watching for a full day: cough, breathlessness or fever hours later means water in the lungs, and that is the one to carry to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health). Send someone ahead to say what happened and how long they were under.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:storms-flooding}}
Floodwater is cold, fast, and carries sewage, fuel and debris; open drains and manholes are invisible under it. Do not walk, swim or drive into it, and never let a child near it ([Evacuation module](module:evacuation)). Anyone pulled out gets rewarmed as for [hypothermia](card:hypothermia), and any water swallowed or breathed in means washing thoroughly with clean water and soap and watching for fever, sickness or diarrhoea over the following days ([Sanitation module](module:sanitation)).
{{/if}}
