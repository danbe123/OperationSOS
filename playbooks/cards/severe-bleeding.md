## When to use

Blood is flowing or spurting, soaking through cloth, or pooling; the person is pale, faint or confused.

## Steps

1. Get help coming — [[call 999]]. Gloves on if you have them.
2. Press hard on the wound with a pad or clean cloth. Do not let go.
3. Not stopping in a couple of minutes? Escalate, do not wait it out.
4. If blood soaks through, take that pad off and press with a fresh one.
5. For a deep wound in the groin or armpit, pack the wound tightly with gauze or clean cloth and press on the packing. For the neck, press directly on the wound: do not pack it, and never put anything round the neck.
6. For a limb wound that will not stop, put a tourniquet 5 to 7 cm above the wound, not over a joint, tighten until the bleeding stops, and write the time on it. Do not loosen it. If one is not enough, put a second one above the first.
7. Lie the person down, keep them warm, and treat for [shock](card:shock).
8. Do not give food or drink.

{{#unless power}}
Wound work is done by light and needs both your hands, so a head torch beats anything held, and a second person puts a spare torch on the wound. Lay out the dressings, gloves, tape and something to write the tourniquet time with before you start. No naked flame near petrol, solvents or oxygen. A cold house makes blood loss worse, so get blankets under and over them as soon as the bleeding is controlled.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
Do not stop to wash a heavily bleeding wound: pressure first, cleaning once the bleeding has stopped ([wound cleaning](card:wound-cleaning)). When you get to it, rinse from a bottle or with boiled and cooled water, poured over the wound rather than dipping anything in a bowl, and wash your own hands the same way before you re-dress it. Untreated stream or floodwater goes nowhere near an open wound.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:terrorism}}
After a bombing or a stabbing the first job is not the wound: get behind cover and off the street, because there may be a second device or a second attacker, and nobody will be let back in until the police say so ([run, hide, tell](playbook:terrorism)). Move the casualty only if you can do it without letting go of the pressure. A public bleed control kit may be nearby in a station or a shopping centre, so send somebody to look while you keep pressing.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:invasion}}
Blast and gunshot wounds bleed from more than one place, so check the whole body, front and back, including the armpits, the groin and under clothing: a small entry wound with a bad exit wound is common. A wound in the chest, the belly or the root of a limb cannot be tourniqueted, so pack it hard, press on the packing, and get them to a surgeon if one exists ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 543](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=543)). Move them under cover, and mark the tourniquet time.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:civil-unrest}}
Treat behind a locked door, not on the street, and do not go out to a casualty while a crowd is moving. Ambulance crews will not enter an area they judge unsafe, so expect to hold pressure for a long time and to carry the person out yourself when it is quiet ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)). Write down what happened, when, and who did what: a serious injury is a police matter afterwards, whatever the state of things now.
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** A tourniquet hurts; that is expected. Leave it on for the paramedics.
**Warning:** Do not remove an object stuck in a wound; press around it.
**Warning:** A pad soaked through is not pressing on anything. Replace it rather than piling more on top, and do not hold pressure for a fixed count of minutes while the bleeding continues — pack the wound or put a tourniquet on instead.

## Stop or escalate

Bleeding like this needs help at once — [[call 999]]. If they stop breathing, start [CPR](card:cpr-adult). If nobody can come: a tourniquet is meant to be off within 2 hours whenever the bleeding can be controlled another way, so pack the wound, put on a firm wide pressure bandage, wait 30 to 60 minutes, then loosen the tourniquet and watch: if heavy bleeding restarts, tighten it again; if it does not, leave it in place but loose and manage the wound; never take off a tourniquet that has been on for more than 6 hours ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 543](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=543); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 88](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=88)). Then clean and dress it ([wound cleaning](card:wound-cleaning); [wound closure](card:wound-closure)). A nosebleed has its own card ([nosebleed](card:nosebleed)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming, so the plan is: stop the bleeding, then decide about moving. Pressure and packing first, and the tourniquet only for a limb you cannot control any other way, with the time written on it. Once the bleeding is stopped, let the clot settle: move them only when you must, and as gently as you can. Then, if they have lost a lot of blood or a tourniquet is on, carry them lying flat, feet raised and wrapped warm, to the nearest crewed hospital ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)), with somebody holding the pressure the whole way and the tourniquet time written down.
{{/unless}}
