## When to use

Someone is unresponsive but breathing normally, or is drowsy after a seizure, drink, drugs or a head injury.

## Steps

1. Kneel beside them. Take off glasses and empty bulky pockets.
2. Put the arm nearest you out at a right angle, elbow bent, palm up.
3. Far arm across the chest, back of that hand against the near cheek.
4. With your other hand, pull the far knee up so the foot is flat on the floor.
5. Keeping the hand against the cheek, pull the knee towards you to roll them onto their side.
6. Tilt the head back and lift the chin so the airway is open; adjust the top leg so hip and knee are at right angles.
7. If nobody has raised the alarm yet, {{#if phones}}[[call 999]]{{else}}send someone to a landline, a neighbour or a payphone: 999 will not connect from here{{/if}}. Check breathing every minute until help arrives; turn them onto the other side after 30 minutes.

{{#unless power}}
You need a light on them all night. Use a head torch or the box's torch on its lowest setting, with a spare set of batteries beside you, rather than draining a phone. Listen as much as you look: put your cheek near their mouth to feel the breath and a hand flat on the chest, which tells you they are breathing when watching in poor light does not.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
An unconscious person on a cold floor loses heat fast and cannot shiver themselves warm. Insulate underneath first, with a mat, cardboard or spare bedding, then blankets over, then a hat. Do not lift them onto a bed if there is any chance of a spinal injury; bring the warmth to them instead ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia); [spinal injury](card:spinal-injury)). Every time you check the breathing, feel whether they are warm on the chest, not just on the hands.
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** If you suspect a spinal injury, move them only if they are vomiting or you cannot keep the airway open ([spinal injury](card:spinal-injury)).
**Warning:** If breathing stops, roll them onto their back and start [CPR](card:cpr-adult).

## Stop or escalate

Anyone who is unconscious, however briefly, needs help coming — [[call 999]]; after a blow to the head see the [head injury card](card:head-injury). If nobody can come: an unconscious person who keeps breathing is nursed on their side with the head low and tilted back so saliva or vomit runs out, nothing by mouth until they wake, the mouth swabbed and lips greased to keep them moist, and turned regularly day and night by two people to prevent bedsores; someone checks the breathing every few minutes ([WTIND, p. 126](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=126); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 3, p. 7](doc:scmg-ch03#page=7)).

{{#unless phones}}
Nobody is coming, so this is nursing for as long as they stay unconscious. Two people take turns and somebody is awake beside them at all times. Check breathing every few minutes, turn them onto the other side every 30 minutes, swab the mouth and grease the lips, and roll them fully every two hours to save the skin. Nothing by mouth until they are properly awake. Write down when they went down, everything you know about why, and every check you make, because whoever eventually sees them will need it. If breathing stops, roll them onto their back and start [CPR](card:cpr-adult).
{{/unless}}
