## When to use

Contractions every two to three minutes, the mother says the baby is coming, or the head is visible.

## Steps

1. Get help coming, on speaker if it connects — [[call 999]].
2. Wash your hands. Clean towels, a warm room, privacy.
3. Let the mother choose her position; do not make her lie flat.
4. As the head appears, support it gently with a clean hand; do not pull. Let the mother push with contractions.
5. If the cord is around the neck, hook it loose with a finger. The shoulders and body come with the next pushes; hold the slippery baby firmly.
6. Dry the baby, put it skin to skin on the mother's chest, cover both, cover the baby's head. Rub the back if it does not cry.
7. Leave the cord until it stops pulsing, then tie it tightly in two places 10 and 15 cm from the baby and cut between with clean scissors. If nothing clean is available, leave it attached.
8. The placenta follows within an hour; keep it for the midwife. Massage the lower abdomen firmly and put the baby to the breast if bleeding is heavy.

{{#unless power}}
Set the light up before the baby comes: a head torch on the helper and a lamp or torch standing on a shelf, never a candle near bedding, oxygen or a newborn. Warm the room with what you have and close the door to keep the heat in. Boil water on the gas hob or a camping stove used in a ventilated place, and let it cool covered, for washing hands and for cleaning up afterwards ([Power module](module:power)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
You need less water than you think and it has to be clean. Set out before the birth: a covered jug of boiled and cooled water, soap, and clean towels. Wash your hands with soap and poured water before touching her, and again before handling the cord. Use bottled or boiled water for anything that touches the mother, the baby or the cord, keep a bowl for dirty water and empty it outside, and give the mother plenty to drink throughout ([Water module](module:water); [dehydration](card:dehydration)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
A newborn loses heat fast and a cold baby feeds badly and gets ill. Dry the baby at once, take the wet towel away, and put it skin to skin against the mother's chest under blankets with a hat on its head, so mother and baby warm each other. Warm the birth room in advance if you can, keep the draughts out, and keep the two of them together and covered for the first days rather than laying the baby down alone ([infant feeding](page:infant-feeding); [hypothermia](card:hypothermia)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Feet or bottom first, heavy bleeding before the birth, a cord coming out first, or fits are emergencies: get help at once — [[call 999]] ([pregnancy emergencies](card:pregnancy-emergencies)).
**Warning:** Never pull on the cord to deliver the placenta.

## Stop or escalate

A birth like this always needs help coming — [[call 999]]. If the baby does not breathe after drying and rubbing for a minute, start [child CPR](card:cpr-child). If nobody can come: heavy bleeding after the birth is the killer. Put the baby to the breast, massage the belly until the womb feels hard and check it every few minutes, and if blood keeps coming press down hard with both hands just below the navel and keep pressing long after it stops; give her plenty to drink and lie her with the legs up if she is faint ([WTIND, p. 313](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=313); [WTIND, p. 314](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=314)). Chills, fever, belly pain or a foul discharge in the days after are childbirth fever, treated with the antibiotics you hold under the rules in Where There Is No Doctor ([WTIND, p. 325](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=325)).

{{#unless phones}}
No midwife and no ambulance, so two of you stay with her: one for the mother, one for the baby. Do not drive her anywhere once the pushing has started, and never leave her alone. After the birth, the watch is on bleeding: the womb should feel like a hard ball below the navel, so rub it up every fifteen minutes for the first two hours and check the pad; heavy bleeding is treated as above, with the baby at the breast, and it is the one thing that would make you carry her to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:grid-collapse}}
Assume nobody is coming for weeks and prepare the birth now rather than on the night: two helpers named, clean towels, soap, boiled and cooled water, clean scissors or a knife boiled ten minutes, string or tape for the cord, hats and blankets for the baby, and pads for the mother put by in one bag. Agree who runs where for help. Keep the room the warmest in the house and the light ready, and write down times as they happen, because nobody will remember them later.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:long-rebuild}}
With no midwife, no scan and no hospital, the risk moves to what you watch for rather than what you can do. Before the birth: a bleed, a fit, severe headache or swelling, or a baby that has stopped moving are the danger signs ([pregnancy emergencies](card:pregnancy-emergencies)). After it: fever, foul discharge or belly pain in the mother, and a baby who will not feed, is floppy, or goes cold or yellow. Feed the mother well and keep the baby at the breast; breast milk is the one thing that stays safe ([infant feeding](page:infant-feeding)).
{{/if}}
