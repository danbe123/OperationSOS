## When to use

Someone touching a live wire or appliance, or struck by lightning: collapse, no breathing, burns.

## Steps

1. Do not touch them. Switch off at the socket, fuse box or mains.
2. Cannot switch off? Push them clear with dry wood; stand on dry wood.
3. Not breathing: start CPR and get help — [[call 999]].
4. Lightning: they are safe to touch at once, they hold no charge. Start [CPR](card:cpr-adult) on anyone not breathing before treating the others; hearts stopped by lightning restart ([Lightning injury (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Lightning_injury)).
5. Look for an entry and an exit burn (hands, feet). Cool with running water and cover with a dry, non-fluffy dressing ([burns](card:burns); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 1, p. 13](doc:scmg-ch01#page=13)).
6. A shock throws people: check for a [spinal injury](card:spinal-injury) and [broken bones](card:broken-bones) ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 410](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=410)).
7. Everyone shocked by the mains or lightning should be checked at hospital even if they feel fine: heart-rhythm problems and deep muscle damage show later and the skin burn is smaller than the damage beneath ([Electrical injury (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Electrical_injury); [Emergency War Surgery, p. 409](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=409)).
8. Overhead lines, railway lines, substations, a fallen cable: stay well back, keep others back, and wait for the network operator ([[call 105]]) or fire service; high voltage jumps through the air and along wet ground.

{{#unless power}}
A power cut does not make wires safe. A generator, an inverter, a battery system or a solar array can be feeding the house wiring, and solar panels are live whenever there is daylight even with the grid down, so isolate at the changeover switch or the consumer unit and pull the generator's plug before touching anything ([solar and islanding](page:solar-islanding); [mains electricity](page:mains-electricity)). A fallen line stays live whether or not your lights work. Work by torch and never assume the supply is off because nothing is switched on.
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Water conducts. Do not touch anyone in a bath, puddle or flood until the power is off ([mains electricity](page:mains-electricity)).
**Warning:** Take cover from lightning at once: a building or a car, never a lone tree ([weather](page:fieldcraft-weather)).

## Stop or escalate

Any mains shock with burns, collapse, chest pain, confusion or numbness, any shock to a pregnant woman or a child, and every lightning strike — [[call 999]]. A brief tingle from a low-voltage appliance with no symptoms — [[call 111]] if unsure. If nobody can come: treat the burns as burns, keep them drinking, and watch the urine: dark red or brown urine means muscle breakdown, so push fluids hard until it clears ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 409](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=409); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 1, p. 13](doc:scmg-ch01#page=13)).

{{#unless phones}}
Neither 999 nor the power company's number will connect, so make the scene safe yourself and treat where you are. After any mains shock or lightning strike, rest them for the day with someone watching: chest pain, palpitations, fainting, confusion or dark urine are the reasons to carry them to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health). Keep everyone away from a fallen cable, mark it and post someone to warn people off, and send a runner to the fire station or the network operator's depot rather than leaving it unguarded ([getting help without phones](page:no-phones)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:storms-flooding}}
Water and electricity are the flood killer that catches rescuers. Do not enter a flooded room, cellar or garage until the supply is off at the consumer unit, and never touch a switch, socket or appliance with wet hands or standing in water ([mains electricity](page:mains-electricity)). Fallen lines lie hidden under floodwater and energise it for a distance around, so keep out of any water near a pole or a downed cable, and keep generators and their leads well clear of the wet.
{{/if}}
