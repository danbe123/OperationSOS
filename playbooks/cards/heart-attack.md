## When to use

Crushing or tight chest pain, maybe spreading to arms, jaw, neck or back, with sweating or breathlessness.

## Steps

1. Say "chest pain, possible heart attack" — [[call 999]].
2. Sit them on the floor, back against a wall, knees bent and supported.
3. Give one 300 mg aspirin to chew slowly, if not allergic.
4. If they have a GTN spray prescribed, let them use it as directed.
5. Loosen tight clothing. Keep them still and calm; no walking about.
6. Stay with them and watch their breathing.
7. If they collapse and stop breathing normally, start [CPR](card:cpr-adult) and send for a defibrillator.

{{#unless power}}
With the lift and the stairlift dead, nobody carries a person with chest pain up or down stairs: make up a bed downstairs, near a door and near a bucket, and bring everything to them. Keep one torch and the box's power bank for the night watch instead of lighting the whole room. Aspirin and a GTN spray keep without a fridge; what does and does not survive a warm house is on [Chronic conditions](page:chronic-conditions).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Women, older people and people with diabetes often have no chest pain: just breathlessness, sweating, nausea or "indigestion".
**Warning:** Do not drive them to hospital; the ambulance carries the treatment.

## Stop or escalate

This is an emergency from the first minute — [[call 999]]. Every minute of delay costs heart muscle. If nobody can come: complete rest in bed, propped up on pillows, or sitting forward if that eases the breathing, for at least a week and then very quiet for a month or more; the aspirin at once and then one a day; no smoking, no alcohol, no salt if the feet swell, and a pulse and breathing check every half hour in the first day ([WTIND, p. 374](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=374); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 7, p. 3](doc:scmg-ch07#page=3)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming, and the clot-busting drugs and the stents are in hospitals, not here. The warning against driving them assumes a crew is on its way, and none is: this is a judgement forced on you by there being no ambulance, not normal advice; the casualty never drives. If a hospital is a short drive away and someone else can drive, carry them to the car, sit them upright, and put someone in the back to watch their breathing ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)). If nothing is reachable, this becomes nursing: the aspirin chewed now, complete rest, and someone beside them ready to start [CPR](card:cpr-adult). Say honestly that most of what a hospital adds is not available here.
{{/unless}}
