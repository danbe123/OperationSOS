## When to use

A child is hot, flushed and miserable or measures 38 °C or more; with or without a fit.

## Steps

1. Undress to one layer. Keep the room comfortable, not cold.
2. Offer drinks often; breastfeed more often. Count wet nappies.
3. Medicine only if the child is distressed; one at a time, never both.
4. Paracetamol 120 mg/5 ml liquid: 3 to 5 months 2.5 ml; 6 to 23 months 5 ml; 2 to 3 years 7.5 ml; 4 to 5 years 10 ml; up to 4 times in 24 hours, at least 4 hours apart. 250 mg/5 ml liquid from 6 years: 6 to 7 years 5 ml; 8 to 9 years 7.5 ml; 10 to 11 years 10 ml; 12 to 15 years 10 to 15 ml ([NHS paracetamol for children](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/paracetamol-for-children/)).
5. Ibuprofen 100 mg/5 ml liquid, with food, from 3 months and 5 kg: 3 to 11 months 2.5 ml; 1 to 3 years 5 ml; 4 to 6 years 7.5 ml; 7 to 9 years 10 ml; 10 to 11 years 15 ml; 3 times in 24 hours, at least 6 hours apart; 12 to 17 years 200 to 400 mg tablets up to 3 times in 24 hours ([NHS ibuprofen for children](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/ibuprofen-for-children/how-and-when-to-give-ibuprofen-for-children/)). Not with chickenpox, dehydration or asthma unless a doctor says so ([NHS ibuprofen for children](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/ibuprofen-for-children/who-can-and-cannot-take-ibuprofen-for-children/)).
6. A fit with fever (febrile convulsion) mostly happens between 6 months and 5 years and usually stops within 5 minutes: note the time, lay the child on their side on the floor, cushion the head, put nothing in the mouth, and do not try to cool them during the fit ([Febrile seizure (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Febrile_seizure); [seizures](card:seizures)).
7. When it stops: [recovery position](card:recovery-position); the child will be sleepy. A first fit, or one lasting over 5 minutes — [[call 999]] ([Febrile seizure (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Febrile_seizure)).
8. Check the child through the night. Press a glass on any rash: spots that do not fade are meningitis or [sepsis](card:sepsis) — [[call 999]].

{{#unless power}}
The glass test needs light, so keep a torch by the bed and look at the whole child, undressed, every few hours through the night rather than feeling a forehead in the dark. A digital thermometer runs on its own battery; keep a spare. Medicine doses are measured with the syringe or spoon in the packet, by torchlight, never by eye. Keep the room comfortable rather than hot, and write down each dose and the time so the next person does not repeat it ([Power module](module:power)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
A feverish child needs more fluid than usual and it has to be safe: boiled and cooled or bottled water, small amounts often, breastfeeds more often, and oral rehydration drink if there is diarrhoea or vomiting ([dehydration](card:dehydration); [Water module](module:water)). Count wet nappies or trips to the toilet, because that is your only measure. Do not sponge a feverish child down at all: the NHS says it does not help ([Fever in children (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-children/)). Keep the little clean water you have aside for washing hands and the child's face.
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Never give aspirin to a child under 16 ([NHS ibuprofen for children](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/ibuprofen-for-children/)). Check that no other medicine (cold remedies) contains paracetamol. Too much: [poisoning](card:poisoning).
**Warning:** A baby under 3 months with a fever needs a doctor the same day ([Fever (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Fever)).

## Stop or escalate

Mottled, blue or very pale skin, a rash that does not fade under a glass, breathing hard or grunting with the ribs sucking in, floppy or hard to wake, a weak high-pitched cry, a stiff neck, a bulging soft spot, a first fit or one over 5 minutes ([Febrile seizure (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Febrile_seizure)) — [[call 999]]. A baby under 3 months, a child not drinking or with dry nappies, a fever that goes on for days, or a parent who is worried — [[call 111]], or in Northern Ireland the GP out-of-hours service ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)). If nobody can come: keep the fluids going ([dehydration](card:dehydration)), treat the child not the thermometer, and if the child looks ill with no obvious cause assume a serious bacterial infection and start any antibiotic you hold as Where There Is No Doctor directs ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 110](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=110); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 125](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=125)).

{{#unless phones}}
No 111 and no ambulance, so somebody watches the child and writes it down: temperature, drinks, wet nappies, breathing and how rousable they are, every hour or two, day and night. The list above is still the list, and any one of those signs, or a baby under three months with a fever, means carrying the child to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health) rather than waiting for morning. Take the notes and the medicines with you, keep the child warm and drinking on the way, and send someone ahead.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:pandemic}}
Treat every fever as catching until you know better: one named carer, a mask and gloves if you hold them, hands washed before and after, the child's cup, bowl and bedding kept and washed apart, and the rest of the household, especially older people, kept out of the room ([Sanitation module](module:sanitation)). Fluids matter more than the thermometer ([dehydration](card:dehydration)). Fast breathing, mottled or grey skin, a rash that does not fade, or a child too sleepy to rouse is [sepsis](card:sepsis) and does not wait for the outbreak to pass.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:heat-drought}}
In a heatwave, work out whether this is an illness or the heat: a child who has been hot, is flushed, sweaty or has stopped sweating, is dizzy, sick or cramping, needs cooling and fluids first, not paracetamol, and confusion or fits mean heat stroke ([heat stroke](card:heat-stroke)). Keep them in the coolest, shaded room, one light layer, damp cloths and a fan, and offer drinks every half hour. Never leave a child in a parked car, and never ration a sick child's water ([Water module](module:water)).
{{/if}}
