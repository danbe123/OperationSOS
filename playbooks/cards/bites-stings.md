## When to use

Bitten by a dog, cat, person, bat or other animal, or stung by a wasp, bee or hornet. Adders: see below.

## Steps

1. Wash the bite with soap under running water; keep going for minutes.
2. Press a clean pad on it until bleeding stops, then cover it.
3. Every bite that breaks the skin gets seen today — [[call 111]].
4. Do not close a bite with strips or stitches: leave it open under a dressing to heal from the bottom up ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 135](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=135); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 255](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=255)).
5. Cat and human bites, and any bite on the hand, face or over a joint, infect most often and usually need antibiotics (co-amoxiclav) and a tetanus check ([Cat bite (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Cat_bite); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 270](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=270); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 255](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=255)).
6. Bat bite or scratch: UK bats can carry a rabies-like virus. Wash with soap and water for 15 minutes and get the post-exposure vaccine urgently; it works only if started before symptoms ([Rabies (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Rabies)). Same for any bite abroad.
7. Sting: get the sting out at once by whatever is quickest, scraping or pulling, then wash and apply a cold compress; an antihistamine (cetirizine or chlorphenamine) and paracetamol ease the itch and pain ([Bee sting (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Bee_sting); [NHS cetirizine](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/cetirizine/); [NHS chlorphenamine](kiwix:nhs_medicines/www.nhs.uk/medicines/chlorphenamine-including-piriton/)).
8. Adder bites and tick removal are on the [ticks and adders](page:ticks-adders) page. A wound going red, hot or oozing: [wound cleaning](card:wound-cleaning).

{{#unless water}}
No tap does not mean no washing: what cleans a bite is the volume of water run over it. Pour stored, bottled or boiled and cooled water from a jug over the wound with soap for several minutes, catching the run-off in a bowl and emptying it outside; a bat bite or scratch still gets the full 15 minutes ([Rabies (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Rabies); [Water module](module:water)). Never clean a wound with flood, river or pond water, and wash your own hands before and after ([wound cleaning](card:wound-cleaning)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless power}}
With the freezer off, a cloth wrung out in cold water and changed often takes the heat and itch out of a sting. Look at the wound in daylight or under a head torch rather than guessing in the dark, and draw round the edge of any redness with a pen so that in the morning you can see whether it has spread, which is the sign that decides whether antibiotics start ([wound cleaning](card:wound-cleaning)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Swelling of the face, lips or tongue, wheeze, faintness or a widespread rash after a sting or bite is [anaphylaxis](card:anaphylaxis) — [[call 999]].
**Warning:** A sting inside the mouth or throat can swell the airway: suck ice and get help at once.

## Stop or escalate

Anaphylaxis, a sting in the throat, a bite to the neck or face of a child, heavy bleeding, or a mauling — [[call 999]]. Every other bite that breaks the skin, any bat contact, and a sting site that is spreading red or hot — [[call 111]] today, or in Northern Ireland the GP out-of-hours service ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)). If nobody can come: clean and cut away crushed tissue, leave the wound open, give a course of co-amoxiclav (doxycycline if penicillin-allergic) for cat, human and hand bites, watch for infection daily, and for a bite that could carry rabies wash thoroughly with soap and water and leave it open ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 255](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=255); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 256](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=256); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 230](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=230)). Spreading infection or fever: [sepsis](card:sepsis).

{{#unless phones}}
Nobody is coming, and nobody is giving a rabies vaccine or a tetanus booster today either. Clean the bite long and hard, leave it open under a dressing, start the antibiotic you hold for a cat, human or hand bite, and write down the time, the animal and what you gave. Carry a mauled child, a bite to the face or neck, or anyone bleeding heavily to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health), keeping the pressure on all the way. Look at the wound twice a day: spreading redness, swelling, pus or fever is the point to act ([sepsis](card:sepsis)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:long-rebuild}}
With no health service there is no rabies vaccine and no tetanus injection, so prevention is the whole of the plan: keep clear of strange and stray dogs, never pick up a bat with bare hands, keep dogs and livestock away from small children, and gloves on for handling animals or clearing rubble. Wash every bite that breaks the skin for minutes on end, leave it open, and treat an infection early with what you hold rather than waiting to see ([Where There Is No Doctor, p. 230](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=230)).
{{/if}}
