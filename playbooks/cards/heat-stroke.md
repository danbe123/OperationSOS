## When to use

Hot skin, confusion, fast breathing, fits, or no better 30 minutes after cooling; temperature over 40 °C.

## Steps

1. Heat stroke can kill within the hour — [[call 999]].
2. Move them to the coolest shaded place. Take off outer clothing.
3. Cool with cool water: sponge, spray or pour it over them and fan.
4. Put cold packs or wet cloths on the neck, armpits and groin.
5. If they are fully awake, give sips of water or oral rehydration solution ([dehydration card](card:dehydration)).
6. If they are drowsy or fitting, put them in the [recovery position](card:recovery-position) and give nothing by mouth.
7. {{#if water}}Keep cooling until they are alert and their skin feels normal, then keep them in the shade and watch them.{{else}}With the mains off, wet cloths from stored water and fan them rather than pouring it away; keep cooling until they are alert, then keep them in the shade and watch them.{{/if}}

{{#unless power}}
No fan, no fridge and no air conditioning, so work with evaporation instead. Wet cloths on bare skin plus moving air cools better than wet cloths alone: fan them by hand, with a piece of card, or with a sheet swung by two people. The coolest place in the house is the ground floor on the shaded side, or a cellar. Bottles thawing in a dead freezer are still cold for a while; put them at the neck, armpits and groin before they warm.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:heat-drought}}
With the water rationed, cooling still comes first, because heat stroke can kill within the hour: spend the water on the person. Bath water, butt water or grey water is fine on the skin, and only drinking water goes in the mouth. Look in twice a day on older neighbours, anyone living alone, and anyone taking diuretics or heart or mental-health medicines, who are the groups the Adverse Weather and Health Plan puts most at risk ([AWHP, p. 34](doc:awhp-2026#page=34)).
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Heat exhaustion (sweating, dizziness, cramps, nausea) is the warning stage: cool down and rehydrate within 30 minutes.
**Warning:** Children, older people and anyone on diuretics or some heart or mental-health medicines are at most risk.

## Stop or escalate

Confusion, fits, loss of consciousness, or no improvement after 30 minutes of cooling — [[call 999]]. If nobody can come: keep soaking and fanning until the temperature drops and they are alert, then a day of rest in the shade with a salt-and-sugar drink once they can swallow, and watch for confusion or fits returning; heat cramps and heat exhaustion answer to lying down in the cool and a teaspoon of salt in a litre of water ([WTIND, p. 130](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=130)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming, so the cooling is the whole treatment and it has to be done properly. Keep pouring, sponging and fanning until they are alert and their skin feels normal, and do not stop because they seem better after a few minutes; two people take turns. Do not put them in a hot car and drive across country in the middle of the day. Cool them where they are, in the deepest shade you have, and move them in the cool of the evening if they still need help ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)).
{{/unless}}
