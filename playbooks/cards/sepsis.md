## When to use

An infection (wound, chest, urine, tooth, after a birth) plus confusion, fast breathing or mottled skin.

## Steps

1. Ask out loud: could this be sepsis? Any infection can start it.
2. Look: confused or slurred, breathing fast, shivering, mottled skin.
3. Any of those — [[call 999]] and say "I think this is sepsis".
4. Adults: slurred speech or confusion, extreme shivering or muscle pain, no urine passed all day, severe breathlessness, skin mottled, bluish or very pale, or "I feel like I am dying" ([Sepsis (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Sepsis)).
5. Children: breathing very fast, a fit, mottled, bluish or pale skin, a rash that does not fade under a glass, very sleepy or hard to wake, cold to touch; babies not feeding, vomiting repeatedly or with dry nappies ([fever in a child](card:fever-child)).
6. While waiting: lie them down, keep them warm, sips of fluid if fully awake; write down when it started, any temperature readings and every medicine taken.
7. The temperature may be low or normal rather than high, especially in older people, babies and anyone with a weak immune system ([Sepsis (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Sepsis)).
8. Find the source: a wound that is red, hot, swollen or has a red line running from it ([wound cleaning](card:wound-cleaning)), a cough, burning urine, a tooth ([dental abscess](card:dental-abscess)), a bite ([bites and stings](card:bites-stings)), a recent birth or miscarriage ([pregnancy emergencies](card:pregnancy-emergencies)).

{{#unless power}}
No thermometer readout, no pulse oximeter and no good light. You are the monitor: count the breaths for a full minute and the pulse for a full minute by a watch, write both down with the time, and repeat every hour, because a rate that climbs over hours is the finding that matters. Look at the chest and the knees by torch light for mottling, and at the lips for blue. Check anything you hold that needs cool storage before the fridge warms ([medical module](module:medical)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
They will need far more fluid than you expect and it has to be safe: boiled and cooled, or bottled, made up as rehydration drink and given in constant sips ([dehydration](card:dehydration); [water disinfection](page:water-disinfection)). Wash your hands with soap and stored water before and after every contact with them or with the wound, and boil the cloths you use on it. Untreated water in a wound, or drunk by a septic person, adds a second infection to the one already killing them.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:pandemic}}
Sepsis is what kills in a pandemic, and the person nursing is the one who catches it. Wear a mask, wash your hands before and after every contact, and keep one person as the nurse rather than the whole household taking turns ([medical module](module:medical)). With the surgeries and hospitals overwhelmed, an infection that is getting worse will not be seen in time, so act on the warning signs yourself. Keep the sick person away from anyone frail, and count the breathing rate twice a day in anybody with a fever.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:famine}}
Hunger makes infection more likely and hides it: a starved person may not run a fever and a wound may not go red. Trust confusion, fast breathing, a fast pulse and passing no urine instead. Feed as well as treat, because nothing heals on no food, and give the rehydration drink even when they will not eat ([dehydration](card:dehydration)). Spend the antibiotics you hold on the people with real signs of infection rather than on every fever, because what you have cannot be replaced.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:long-rebuild}}
With no health service at all, antibiotics are finite and sepsis is the reason to spend them: full dose, full course, never a half course for a cough ([medical module](module:medical); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 212](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=212)). Prevention becomes the medicine, which means clean water, clean wounds, clean birth, hand washing and abscesses drained early. Teach more than one person in the household to recognise these signs, because the one who knows will not always be the one who is there.
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Every hour without antibiotics lowers the chance of survival: hospitals aim to start them within the first hour ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 105](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=105)).
**Warning:** "It is just flu" is how sepsis is missed. Trust a change in behaviour: a person who is not themselves, or a child who is unusually quiet.

## Stop or escalate

Any of the signs above — [[call 999]]. An infection that is getting worse without them — [[call 111]] the same day. If nobody can come: start the strongest broad-spectrum antibiotic you hold at once and give the full course, co-amoxiclav first choice and ciprofloxacin second ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 212](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=212)); open and drain any abscess, clean the wound, push oral fluids or rehydration drink ([dehydration](card:dehydration)) and treat for [shock](card:shock); a pulse over 110, confusion or a falling blood pressure means the infection is winning ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 110](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=110)). Antibiotic rules in a collapse: [medical module](module:medical).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance and no hospital means no antibiotics into a vein, no drip and no intensive care, and sepsis kills quickly without them. What can still be given: the strongest broad-spectrum antibiotic you hold, started now and the full course finished; the source found and drained or cleaned; rehydration drink in constant sips; warmth, and the legs raised ([shock](card:shock)). If any hospital is crewed and reachable, go, even if it takes hours, because that is where the treatment is ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)). Tell the family plainly what the chances are.
{{/unless}}
