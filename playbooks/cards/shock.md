## When to use

Pale clammy skin, fast weak pulse, fast breathing, dizziness, thirst or confusion after injury or bleeding.

## Steps

1. Send for help — [[call 999]]. Stop bleeding, cool burns.
2. Lie them down on a blanket. Raise the legs about 30 cm.
3. Keep the legs flat if a leg, pelvis or head is injured.
4. Loosen tight clothing at the neck, chest and waist.
5. Keep them warm with blankets, but do not overheat them.
6. Nothing to eat or drink; moisten the lips if they are thirsty.
7. Reassure, stay with them, and check breathing and pulse every few minutes; if they become unresponsive but breathe, use the [recovery position](card:recovery-position).

{{#unless power}}
No monitors, so you are the monitor. Pulse at the wrist for a full minute, breaths for a full minute, and are they awake, sleepy or confused; write each set down with the time, because the trend is what tells you. A wrist pulse you can no longer feel is a bad sign ([FM 4-25.11, p. 14](doc:fm-4-25-11-first-aid#page=14)). Look at the lips and the nail beds under a torch rather than in the gloom, because pale and blue are easy to miss in poor light.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
Cold makes shock worse, so warmth here is treatment and not comfort. Insulate underneath them first, then blankets over, then a hat: someone lying on a cold floor loses heat through the floor faster than through the air. Do not put a hot water bottle straight against the skin and do not try to warm them fast; steady, all-over warmth is what is wanted ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:terrorism}}
After a blast, shock can come from bleeding you cannot see: into the belly, into the pelvis from a fall, or from a crush injury. Anybody caught in an explosion who is pale, clammy and thirsty is treated as shocked even with no obvious wound. Get behind cover before you treat ([severe bleeding](card:severe-bleeding); [run, hide, tell](playbook:terrorism)). Do not let a walking casualty wander off: sit them down, keep them warm, and look at them again in 10 minutes, because shock develops.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:invasion}}
Expect more casualties than hands and expect long delays. Stop the bleeding in everybody before you treat anybody's shock properly, because bleeding is the cause ([severe bleeding](card:severe-bleeding)). Lay them flat with the legs raised, wrapped and out of the wet, somewhere you can defend and can carry them out of. Anyone who has been shocked and seems to have recovered still needs watching for hours, because it comes back.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:civil-unrest}}
Get them off the street. A person in shock cannot walk far and must not be made to; if you have to move them, carry them lying flat with the legs raised. Do not stand outside waiting for an ambulance that will not come into an area the crew judges unsafe. Keep the door shut, keep them warm, and send one person out on foot for help rather than moving the whole group ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)).
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Shock can develop slowly; a person who seems fine after a big bleed may collapse an hour later.
**Warning:** This is not emotional shock; it is failing circulation and it kills.

## Stop or escalate

Shock kills without treatment, so get help at once — [[call 999]]. If breathing stops, start [CPR](card:cpr-adult). If nobody can come: keep them lying with the feet a little higher than the head (half-sitting instead if the head is injured), warm, every bleed stopped and every wound dressed; once they are awake and can swallow, give sips of water or rehydration drink, and lots of it if they are dehydrated or burned ([dehydration card](card:dehydration)); give paracetamol rather than anything sedating for pain, and if you know how, fluid into a vein at a fast drip ([WTIND, p. 126](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=126)). Sepsis causes the same picture with fever ([sepsis card](card:sepsis)).

{{#unless phones}}
No ambulance is coming, and shock from serious blood loss is treated with blood and fluid into a vein, which is a hospital job. What can still be done: stop every bleed, lie them flat with the legs raised, keep them warm, and once they are awake, swallowing, and no operation is in prospect, give sips of water or rehydration drink ([dehydration](card:dehydration)). Take the pulse and count the breaths every 15 minutes and write them down: a pulse that climbs while they get sleepier means they are losing ground, and they must be carried to whatever hospital is crewed ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)).
{{/unless}}
