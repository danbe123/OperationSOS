## When to use

Something in the eye, a chemical splash, a blow to the eye, or an object stuck in or through the eyeball.

## Steps

1. Tell them not to rub. Wash your hands.
2. Grit: blink, flush with clean water, or lift the lid and wipe it out.
3. Chemical: hold the eye open under running water and keep rinsing.
4. Keep rinsing a chemical splash for 15 to 20 minutes, lifting both lids, with the head tilted so the water runs away from the good eye; take contact lenses out ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 182](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=182); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 1, p. 13](doc:scmg-ch01#page=13)). Then get it seen ([chemical exposure](card:chemical-exposure)).
5. Grit that will not come out, or anything from hammering, grinding or chipping metal: stop trying, cover the eye and get it seen the same day; a fragment can be inside the eye with little pain ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 12](doc:scmg-ch04#page=12); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 267](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=267)).
6. A blow (fist, ball, cork): cold compress on the closed lids without pressure, sit them up; blood visible inside the eye, blurred vision or a pupil of odd shape needs hospital ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 184](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=184)).
7. Anything stuck in or through the eyeball, or the eye leaking fluid or jelly: do not remove it, do not press. Tape a rigid shield (a cut-down cup) over the eye with no pad beneath it, cover the other eye to stop movement, nothing by mouth — [[call 999]] ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 223](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=223); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 12](doc:scmg-ch04#page=12)).
8. Arc eye or snow blindness: gritty red eyes hours after welding or a day on snow; cold compresses, dark glasses, painkillers, settles in 24 to 48 hours ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 13](doc:scmg-ch04#page=13); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 193](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=193)).

## Warnings

**Warning:** Never pull out anything embedded in the eye and never press on an injured eyeball ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 223](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=223)).
**Warning:** A sudden painful red eye in one eye only, or worsening pain days after a blow, can be pressure in the eye and threatens sight ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 183](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=183)).

## Stop or escalate

A penetrating injury, a chemical splash (after rinsing), loss of vision, blood inside the eye, or an eye that will not open after a blow — [[call 999]] or get to the eye casualty department at once. Grit that will not come out, a scratched eye, or arc eye — [[call 111]] or an optician the same day. If nobody can come: keep the eye covered and the person resting sitting up, use antibiotic eye ointment if you hold it, give painkillers, and know that a penetrating eye injury without surgery usually costs the eye ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 184](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=184); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 267](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=267)).
