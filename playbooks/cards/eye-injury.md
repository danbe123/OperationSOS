## When to use

Something in the eye, a chemical splash, a blow to the eye, or an object stuck in or through the eyeball.

## Steps

1. Tell them not to rub. Wash your hands.
2. Grit: blink, flush with clean water, or lift the lid and wipe it out.
3. Chemical: hold the eye open under running water and keep rinsing.
4. Keep rinsing a chemical splash for 15 to 20 minutes, lifting both lids, with the head tilted so the water runs away from the good eye; take contact lenses out ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 182](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=182); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 1, p. 13](doc:scmg-ch01#page=13)). Then get it seen ([chemical exposure](card:chemical-exposure)).
5. Grit that will not come out, or anything from hammering, grinding or chipping metal: stop trying, cover the eye and get it seen the same day; a fragment can be inside the eye with little pain ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 12](doc:scmg-ch04#page=12); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 267](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=267)).
6. A blow (fist, ball, cork): cold compress on the closed lids without pressure, sit them up; blood visible inside the eye, blurred vision or a pupil of odd shape needs hospital ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 184](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=184)).
7. Anything stuck in or through the eyeball, or the eye leaking fluid or jelly: do not remove it, do not press. Tape a rigid shield (a cut-down cup) over the eye with no pad beneath it, cover the other eye to stop movement, nothing by mouth — [[call 999]] ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 223](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=223); [Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 12](doc:scmg-ch04#page=12)).
8. Arc eye or snow blindness: gritty red eyes hours after welding or a day on snow; cold compresses, dark glasses, painkillers, settles in 24 to 48 hours ([Ship Captain's Medical Guide ch. 4, p. 13](doc:scmg-ch04#page=13); [Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 193](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=193)).

{{#unless water}}
The chemical rinse takes far more water than a tap-less house expects, so start it at once with what is nearest: bottled, stored or boiled and cooled water poured slowly from a jug while someone else holds the lids open, refilling as you go for the full 15 to 20 minutes ([Water module](module:water)). Tilt the head so the run-off leaves by the outer corner and never crosses the good eye. Never rinse an eye with flood, river, butt or pond water.
{{/unless}}

{{#unless power}}
Examine the eye with a torch held to the side rather than shone straight into it, in the shade or a dim room, because an injured eye hates light. Take the light away as soon as you have looked, and let them keep both eyes shut, or shaded with dark glasses, in the darkest room in the house. A cloth wrung out in cold water replaces the cold pack from the freezer for a blow to the eye.
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Never pull out anything embedded in the eye and never press on an injured eyeball ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 223](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=223)).
**Warning:** A sudden painful red eye in one eye only, or worsening pain days after a blow, can be pressure in the eye and threatens sight ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 183](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=183)).

## Stop or escalate

A penetrating injury, a chemical splash (after rinsing), loss of vision, blood inside the eye, or an eye that will not open after a blow — [[call 999]] or get to the eye casualty department at once. Grit that will not come out, a scratched eye, or arc eye — [[call 111]], or in Northern Ireland the GP out-of-hours service, or an optician the same day ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)). If nobody can come: keep the eye covered and the person resting sitting up, use antibiotic eye ointment if you hold it, give painkillers, and know that a penetrating eye injury without surgery usually costs the eye ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 184](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=184); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 267](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=267)).

{{#unless phones}}
No eye casualty department to ring, so rinse first and move afterwards. For a chemical splash or a penetrating injury, rinse or shield, taping a cup or folded pad over the injured eye without ever pressing on it and asking them not to look about rather than padding both eyes, and lead them, or carry them, to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health), sitting up, with a written note of what went in and when the rinsing started. Sight is worth the journey. If nothing is reachable, keep the eye shielded and rested, give painkillers and antibiotic ointment if you hold it, and check the other eye daily.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:volcanic}}
Ash is sharp and it scratches eyes: this is the commonest injury in an ash fall after breathing trouble. Wear tight goggles or wrap-around glasses outdoors, wear glasses rather than contact lenses while the ash is about, and tell everyone not to rub. Flush a gritty eye with clean water rather than wiping it, do not sweep ash dry indoors, and take dusty outer clothing off outside so it is not shaken out over the room ([volcanic eruption](playbook:volcanic)).
{{/if}}
