## When to use

Any cut, graze, puncture or open wound once the bleeding is under control.

## Steps

1. Wash your hands; wear gloves if you have them.
2. Rinse the wound under clean running water for at least five minutes.
3. Pick out grit and dirt with clean tweezers. Do not scrub.
4. {{#if water}}Boiled and cooled water, or bottled water, is fine; do not use untreated stream water on a wound.{{else}}With the mains off, rinse from a bottle or with boiled and cooled water, a jug at a time, and never with untreated stream water ([Water module](module:water)).{{/if}}
5. Pat dry with a clean cloth, apply an antiseptic such as chlorhexidine if you have it, and cover with a non-stick dressing.
6. Change the dressing daily or when wet or dirty; leave a deep or dirty wound open under a dressing rather than closing it tightly. Only a clean, fresh cut is closed ([wound closure](card:wound-closure)).
7. Check every day for spreading redness, heat, swelling, pus, a red line up the limb, or fever.
8. Puncture wounds, bites and anything from soil carry tetanus; if the person's vaccinations are not complete or not known, follow the tetanus rules in the [medical module](module:medical). Bites have their own card ([bites and stings](card:bites-stings)).

{{#unless power}}
Wound work needs light and both hands: a head torch, and a second person holding a spare torch on the wound if you can spare one. Lay out gloves, gauze, tweezers, antiseptic and the dressing before you start. Boil water on gas or on a stove used outdoors and let it cool covered. Check what you hold and its dates by daylight rather than by torch, and keep the kit somewhere you can find it in the dark.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:pandemic}}
Whoever dresses the wound wears a mask and washes their hands before and after, for their own sake as much as the patient's ([medical module](module:medical)). Keep one person as the dresser instead of everybody helping. With the surgeries and urgent treatment centres overwhelmed, a wound infection will not be seen early, so look at it every day and act on the first spreading redness rather than waiting for advice. Burn or bag the old dressings; do not leave them where children or animals get at them.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:famine}}
Wounds do not heal on no food. A hungry person's cut stays open for weeks and turns septic more easily, and the redness and swelling that would warn you may be muted, so trust fever, a fast pulse and confusion instead ([sepsis](card:sepsis)). Feed them as part of the treatment, protein and whatever else you have, and give the rehydration drink ([dehydration](card:dehydration)). Keep the wound covered and the dressings clean, because flies and dirt do far more harm where people are already weak.
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:long-rebuild}}
With no health service and antibiotics that cannot be replaced, cleaning is the medicine. Wash every wound out properly the first time, leave dirty and old wounds open under a dressing rather than closing them ([wound closure](card:wound-closure)), and re-dress on alternate days. Save the antibiotics for spreading or systemic infection, and give a full course when you do ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 212](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=212)). Teach two people in the household to dress a wound properly, because the one who knows will not always be there.
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Fever, fast breathing, confusion, mottled or very pale skin, or not passing urine can be sepsis. Sepsis kills within hours ([sepsis card](card:sepsis)).
**Warning:** Do not use hydrogen peroxide or neat bleach in a wound.

## Stop or escalate

Signs of sepsis are an emergency — [[call 999]]. For spreading infection, a wound that will not close, an animal or human bite, or a wound from a dirty or rusty object, see a pharmacist or [[call 111]], or in Northern Ireland the GP out-of-hours service ([UK numbers](page:uk-numbers)). If nobody can come: an infected wound gets hot compresses for 20 minutes four times a day, rest, and the limb raised above the heart; a deep, dirty or bite wound is washed out with boiled water and soap, never stitched, and the antibiotics you hold are started at once if the person is not vaccinated against tetanus or the wound is a bite; a bad smell, brown or grey ooze, or black skin with bubbles is gangrene and needs help by any route ([WTIND, p. 137](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=137); [WTIND, p. 138](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=138)).

{{#unless phones}}
No 111 and no hospital, so you are the one watching for infection. Draw round the edge of any redness with a pen and write the date on the skin, then look again 12 hours later: redness spreading past the mark, a red line up the limb, pus, or a fever means the infection is winning, and the antibiotics you hold are started at once and the full course finished ([sepsis](card:sepsis); [medical module](module:medical)). Carry anyone with a spreading infection, gangrene, or a deep wound over a joint or on the face to whatever hospital is crewed ([the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health)).
{{/unless}}
