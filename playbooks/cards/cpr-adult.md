## When to use

Someone has collapsed, does not respond when you shout and shake them, and is not breathing normally.

## Steps

1. Shout for help, a phone on speaker beside you — [[call 999]].
2. Send someone for a defibrillator (AED) if one is nearby.
3. Kneel beside them. Heel of one hand on the centre of the chest.
4. Other hand on top, arms straight. Press down 5 to 6 cm, 100 to 120 times a minute (two a second), letting the chest come back up fully each time.
5. After 30 presses, tilt the head back, lift the chin, pinch the nose and give 2 breaths, each one second, watching the chest rise. If you cannot or will not give breaths, keep pressing without stopping.
6. Carry on 30 presses then 2 breaths. Swap with someone every two minutes if you can.
7. When the AED arrives, turn it on and follow its voice instructions; keep pressing while the pads go on.
8. Do not stop until they breathe normally, help takes over, or you are exhausted.

{{#unless power}}
None of this needs mains power: a defibrillator runs on its own battery and works in a blackout, so if there is one nearby, send someone for it anyway. Clear the floor round the person, put a torch on the floor pointing at the chest rather than holding it, and keep the box's power bank for the torch and the phone. Do not stop compressions to fetch, fix or light anything: someone else does that while you keep pressing ([Power module](module:power)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if dark}}
Working in the dark, put a light down before you start rather than holding one: a head torch on the person doing the breaths, or a torch stood on end beside the chest. Feel for the centre of the chest between the nipples with the heel of your hand instead of looking for it, and count out loud so whoever takes over from you keeps the same rhythm.
{{/if}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Gasping, snoring or occasional gulps are not normal breathing. Start CPR.
**Warning:** Broken ribs are common and do not mean stop.

## Stop or escalate

Someone else raises the alarm while you keep pressing — [[call 999]]. Stop only when the person breathes normally on their own (then use the [recovery position](card:recovery-position)) or a paramedic takes over. If nobody can come: CPR only buys time until a defibrillator or the heart's own recovery, so without a defibrillator it is reasonable to stop after 20 minutes with no response; keep going much longer for drowning, hanging, suffocation or anaphylaxis, where the heart can restart once oxygen or adrenaline gets through ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 96](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=96)).

{{#unless phones}}
Nobody is coming and no paramedic will take over, so decide the shape of it now: keep pressing, swap with someone every two minutes, and send a third person for the nearest defibrillator, remembering that many public cabinets are locked with a code the 999 call handler gives out, so an unlocked cabinet or one in a staffed building is the one to run to ([getting help without phones](page:no-phones)). Do not carry an adult who is in cardiac arrest anywhere. With no defibrillator and no response at all, stopping at 20 minutes is reasonable, and the exceptions above are the ones worth an hour.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:severe-winter}}
A very cold person can look dead: the pulse and breathing become slow and hard to find, so check breathing for a full minute before you decide, and if you start, keep going far longer than usual while you get them out of the wind and wet and insulated from the ground ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)). Handle them gently, because rough movement can stop a cold heart. Warm them as you go; nobody is declared dead on a cold day until they have been warmed.
{{/if}}
