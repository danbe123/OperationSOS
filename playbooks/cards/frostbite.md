## When to use

Skin numb, waxy white, grey or hard after cold: fingers, toes, ears, nose, cheeks. Often with hypothermia.

## Steps

1. Get out of the cold. Treat [hypothermia](card:hypothermia) first.
2. Take off wet gloves, socks, rings and watches. Do not rub.
3. Warm fingers in an armpit or groin, cheeks and ears with a warm palm.
4. Thaw properly only when there is no chance of it refreezing: a part that thaws and freezes again is far worse than one left frozen for a few hours ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 418](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=418); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 458](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=458)).
5. Rewarm in water at 37 to 39 °C, which feels warm not hot to your own elbow, until the part is soft and pink; take the hand or foot out while you top up with warmer water ([Frostbite (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Frostbite); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 458](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=458)).
6. It hurts as it thaws: give paracetamol or ibuprofen ([sprains and strains](card:sprains-strains) doses) ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 193](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=193)).
7. Leave blisters alone. Dry sterile dressings, gauze between fingers and toes, raise the limb and keep it warm ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 418](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=418)).
8. Never warm by a fire, radiator, engine or hot-water bottle: numb skin burns without feeling it ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 418](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=418)).

{{#unless power}}
No electric heater and no thermometer for the water, so heat it on the gas hob or a camping stove used in a ventilated place, mix it in a bowl and test it on your own elbow: warm, never hot, and topped up with more warm water while the hand or foot is lifted out ([Water module](module:water)). Work by head torch, and keep everyone well away from the stove with the numb part, which cannot feel a burn ([Power module](module:power)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless heating}}
Do not thaw at all until you have somewhere that will stay warm: a part that thaws and freezes again is far worse than one left frozen for a few hours ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 418](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=418)). Get the whole person warm first, in dry clothes, a sleeping bag and the warmest room, with the cold treated as [hypothermia](card:hypothermia). Afterwards the thawed part must be kept warm, raised and dry for days, so choose the room you can keep warm and stay in it ([Shelter and heat](module:shelter-heat)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless water}}
The rewarming bath is the one job worth the water: a bowl deep enough to cover the part, from stored water or melted snow and ice, warmed and topped up as it cools. Melt snow before heating it rather than packing it in the pan, and never rub the part with snow. Keep the drinking water separate, and give warm sweet drinks throughout ([Water module](module:water)).
{{/unless}}

## Warnings

**Warning:** Do not rub with snow or hands and do not burst blisters; both destroy tissue ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 418](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=418)).
**Warning:** If you must walk to safety, walk on frozen feet before thawing; a thawed foot cannot be walked on ([Emergency War Surgery, p. 418](doc:emergency-war-surgery-2018#page=418)).

## Stop or escalate

Deep frostbite (hard, white, no feeling after rewarming, blisters, blue-grey or black skin), frostbite of a whole hand or foot, or any frostbite with hypothermia — [[call 999]]. Mild frostbite that has rewarmed but stays numb or blistered — [[call 111]]. If nobody can come: rewarm as above, then keep the part clean, dry, raised and dressed like a burn ([burns](card:burns)); do not cut away dead-looking tissue, because the line between dead and living takes weeks to months to show and amputation is only for spreading infection ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 193](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=193); [Where There Is No Doctor, p. 458](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=458)). Trench foot from wet cold is different: [hygiene in the field](page:fieldcraft-hygiene).

{{#unless phones}}
Nobody is coming, and this is an injury you will be nursing for weeks rather than hours. Rewarm once, properly, and only when you can keep them warm; then dressings, gauze between the fingers and toes, the limb raised, painkillers, and no walking on a thawed foot. Do not cut anything away. Carry them to the nearest crewed help on [the map's health layer](map:?overlay=health) if a whole hand or foot is frozen, if the skin turns black with spreading redness, swelling or fever, or if they are also hypothermic ([hypothermia](card:hypothermia)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:impact-winter}}
In a winter that does not end, frostbite is a daily risk rather than an accident, so the work is prevention: loose dry layers with mittens over gloves, nothing tight, boots that are not laced tight, faces covered, and no bare skin on metal. Check each other's faces, ears and noses every half hour outdoors, because you cannot feel your own, and change wet socks the moment they are wet. If you must keep walking to shelter, walk on frozen feet and thaw only when you arrive ([Shelter and heat](module:shelter-heat); [impact winter](playbook:impact-winter)).
{{/if}}
