## The six knots

Practise these until your hands know them wet, cold and in the dark. Each does a job the others do not:

1. **Bowline**: a fixed loop that will not slip or jam; for tying a rope round a person, a post or a tree.
2. **Clove hitch**: a quick tie to a pole or post, easily adjusted; starts a lashing.
3. **Round turn and two half hitches**: the tie-off that holds under load and unties after rain; the tarp corner to a peg or tree.
4. **Taut-line (midshipman's) hitch**: an adjustable loop that slides to tighten and grips under load; every guy line.
5. **Trucker's hitch**: a three-to-one pulley in a rope; for a tight ridge line or a load on a barrow.
6. **Prusik**: a loop that grips a rope under load and slides when slack; for climbing a rope, a safety line, a tarp ridge tensioner.

Add the **sheet bend** to join two ropes of different sizes, the **figure of eight** for a stopper, and the **square lashing** to make an A-frame or a stretcher of poles. The knots library in the box shows every one with drawings ([Knots library (zimgit)](kiwix:zimgit-knots_en/home)).

## Rope

Paracord (550 cord) for guy lines and lashings; 6 to 8 mm static rope for hauling and safety lines; never trust climbing loads to anything you cannot name. Rope loses strength wet, in the sun, over an edge and at every knot; a rope that has held a fall is retired. For real loads, the rigging manual gives the arithmetic of blocks, tackle and anchors ([Rigging Techniques, FM 5-125](doc:fm-5-125-rigging)).

## Cordage from the hedgerow

When the cord runs out, Britain grows it. **Nettle** stems, stripped of leaves, split, dried a day and then twisted, make strong fine cord and were spun into cloth in the Bronze Age. **Bramble** runners with the thorns scraped off, split in two, bind a shelter frame. **Willow** and **lime** inner bark, soaked for a week and peeled into strips, twist into rope. **Honeysuckle** and **ivy** stems bind as they are. **Wheat straw** and **rushes** plait into rough cord. The two-strand twist (twist each strand away from you, cross them towards you) turns any fibre into cord; it is slow and it works.

## The knife

A fixed blade of 10 to 12 cm with a full tang, or a stout folding knife, does everything from feather sticks to butchery. Cut away from the body and away from the hand holding the work, with the elbows on the knees for a stable cut; never cut towards the thigh's femoral artery, and never catch a falling knife. Use the spine on the ferro rod and keep the edge for wood. The law: a folding non-locking blade under three inches (7.62 cm) may be carried without a reason; any fixed or locking blade needs a good reason, and "in case" is not one, though an actual camping trip is ([Knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law)).

## Saw and axe

A folding saw cuts more wood per hour, more safely, than any axe and is the tool to carry. An axe splits, and splitting is where the axe earns its place: on a chopping block, the wood on the far side of the block from your legs, a short controlled swing, the axe stopping in the block, never in the air behind you. Carry an axe masked, and never chop with anyone within two axe lengths. A hatchet used one-handed above the knee sends people to hospital every winter.

## Keeping an edge

A blunt tool is the dangerous one. A small stone or a diamond card, a little water, the blade at the angle the maker set (a matchbook thickness at the spine for a knife), a dozen strokes each side, then a strop on the back of a leather belt. Axes are sharpened with a round puck in circles; saws are replaced, not sharpened. The repair guides in the library cover sharpening step by step ([iFixit: how to sharpen knives](kiwix:ifixit_en_all/Guide/How+to+Sharpen+Knives/6098)).

## Improvised tools

A stretcher of two poles and two jackets buttoned inside out; an A-frame of three poles lashed at the top to lift a load or hang a pot; a wedge and a maul of hardwood to split logs without an axe; a digging stick fire-hardened; a bow saw from a bent green pole and a spare blade; a water carrier from a bin liner in a rucksack. The tools and repair module carries the household side ([Tools and repair module](module:tools-repair)).
