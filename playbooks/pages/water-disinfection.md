## The table

This is the home page for making water safe; the water module and the field-craft pages only summarise it.

| Method | Dose per litre | Wait | Kills | Does not remove |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boil | Rolling boil, 1 minute | — | Bacteria, viruses, parasites including Cryptosporidium and Giardia ([Water Treatment Library](kiwix:zimgit-water_en/home)) | Chemicals, radioactive particles |
| Thin, unscented bleach (4.5 to 5%) | 2 drops (0.1 ml) | 30 minutes | Bacteria, most viruses | Cryptosporidium ([Cryptosporidium](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Cryptosporidium)), chemicals; double the dose if cloudy |
| NaDCC tablets | 8.5 mg per litre, 17 mg per 2 litres, 67 mg per 20 litres | 30 minutes | Bacteria, most viruses ([Sodium dichloroisocyanurate](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Sodium_dichloroisocyanurate)) | Cryptosporidium; double the dose if cloudy |
| 2% tincture of iodine | 5 drops (clear), 10 drops (cloudy) | 30 minutes | Bacteria, most viruses | Cryptosporidium; no longer sold for water in the UK; not for pregnant women, thyroid patients, or use beyond a few weeks |
| SODIS (solar) | Clear PET bottle, full sun | 6 hours full sun, 2 days if cloudy ([Solar water disinfection](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Solar_water_disinfection)) | Bacteria, viruses, parasites | Chemicals |
| Hollow-fibre or ceramic filter (0.1 to 0.2 micron) | — | — | Bacteria, parasites (Cryptosporidium, Giardia) | Viruses unless rated for them, chemicals |

**The rule that follows from the table**: a filter removes mud and parasites but not viruses; chlorine, NaDCC and iodine kill bacteria and viruses but not Cryptosporidium, whose spores resist chlorine ([Cryptosporidium](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Cryptosporidium)); boiling kills everything. So filter and then boil, or filter and then chlorinate, and boil whenever you can ([Water purification](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Water_purification)).

{{#if power}}Boiling is the surest of these, on the hob or in the kettle.{{else}}Boiling is still the surest of these, but with the power off it means a gas hob lit with a match or a stove used outdoors, so the tablets and the bleach doses may be the practical answer ([Power module](module:power)).{{/if}}

## UK bleach

Ordinary UK household bleach comes in two strengths: thin bleach around 4.5 to 5% sodium hypochlorite, used for the doses above, and thick, gelled bleach which is far stronger and unsuitable for dosing this way. Read the label for the percentage of sodium hypochlorite and check it says "no perfume, no thickener, no added cleaning agents". If the label does not state a plain sodium hypochlorite percentage, or you are in any doubt, do not use it to treat drinking water.

## Cloudy, coloured or smelly water

Let cloudy water settle, pour off the clear water on top, then filter it through a clean cloth before treating it: sediment protects microbes from chemical disinfection and can hide the taste of over- or under-dosing. Never try to make water safe to drink if it is contaminated with fuel, chemicals or sewage; treatment removes microbes, not those hazards.

## Storing water

Store water in clean, sealed containers, kept dark and cool, and rotate the stock every six months. Sanitise a container before use with a teaspoon of thin bleach in a litre of water, swirl it round every surface, then rinse thoroughly ([Water Treatment Library](kiwix:zimgit-water_en/home)).
