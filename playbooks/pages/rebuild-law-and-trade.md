## The law does not switch off

Nothing here replaces the law of England, Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland; it describes how a community governs itself while no court sits and no constable comes. Theft is still theft and assault still assault, prosecuted years later if a court reopens ([Theft Act 1968 s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/60/section/1); [Security and the law module](module:security-law)). Emergency regulations under the Civil Contingencies Act 2004 are one lawful route to restricting movement or requisitioning property, and public health law is another: the 2020 lockdowns were made under section 45C of the Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984, not under the Civil Contingencies Act ([Civil Contingencies Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/contents); [Public Health (Control of Disease) Act 1984](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Public_Health_(Control_of_Disease)_Act_1984)). What they have in common is that both need a functioning government and a sitting Parliament. Emergency regulations must be approved by resolution of **both Houses within 7 days** of being laid or they lapse, and they lapse anyway 30 days after they are made — there is no power to renew them, only to make fresh ones ([Civil Contingencies Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/contents); [Security and the law module](module:security-law)). No village committee has any of those powers.

What survives collapse in Britain is not a new legal system but an old habit: a parish that meets, keeps minutes, and writes things down ([Parish council (England)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Parish_council_(England)); [Common law](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Common_law)).

## Rules people will keep

Write few rules, write them plainly, and put them where everyone passes them ([The first year](page:rebuild-first-year)). Rules that hold have four properties: everyone can recite them, they apply to the people who wrote them, they say who decides and how that person is chosen, and they say how they can be changed. Rules that fail are invented mid-crisis by whoever is angriest.

Practical shape: a meeting on a fixed day whether or not there is business; minutes read out and kept; decisions dated and numbered; anyone may see the ledger and the minutes; and a rule about rules, saying a change needs notice and a second meeting. The parish council, the community council in Scotland and Wales, and the hall noticeboard are the machinery Britain already has ([Community module](module:community); [Ready Scotland community planning](kiwix:govuk_resilience/ready.campaign.gov.scot/community-emergency-planning)).

## Making a rule stick without a police force

Enforcement rests on consent, and consent is spent quickly. Use the mildest sanction that works, in order: a word in private; making it good in goods or work; loss of a turn at something shared; suspension from a shared meal or tool; and, only for repeated or serious harm, exclusion. Understand what the last means in a British winter: it can be a death sentence, so it is decided by a meeting, recorded, and reserved for danger to others.

Three things a community must never do, whatever the provocation: imprison people, use violence as punishment, or take a life. Those are false imprisonment, assault and homicide in every part of the UK, no emergency creates an exception, and communities that start doing them do not stop ([Security and the law module](module:security-law); [Criminal Law Act 1967 s3](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1967/58/section/3)). Detaining someone briefly to stop immediate harm, and handing them over the moment any authority exists, is the outer limit. In law that brief restraint rests on the common law power to prevent a breach of the peace, which anyone may use with reasonable force ([Breach of the peace](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Breach_of_the_peace)). A citizen's arrest proper is narrower still: it applies only to an indictable offence that has actually been committed — an honest but mistaken belief is no defence, and there is no such power at all for summary offences such as common assault — and only where it is not reasonably practicable for a constable to make the arrest instead, with the person handed over at once ([Citizen's arrest](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Citizen's_arrest); [Security and the law module](module:security-law)).

Record every decision and sanction with the date, the reasons and who was present. When the courts come back, that record is the difference between a community that governed itself and a group of people facing hard questions.

## Settling disputes without courts

Most disputes are about food, tools, animals, boundaries, work and debts, and most settle if heard early.

1. **Talk, with a neutral third person.** Mediation is a neutral party helping two sides reach their own agreement; the agreement is written down and signed ([Mediation](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Mediation)).
2. **If that fails, a panel of three.** Both sides agree in advance to accept the decision — that is arbitration, and it is an ordinary part of English law, not an improvisation ([Arbitration](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Arbitration)). Choose people not related to either side, hear both in public, decide the same day, and write the reasons.
3. **Repair, not punishment.** What the wronged person needs is the animal replaced, the fence rebuilt, the work done. Bringing both sides together to agree that repair is restorative justice, and it holds better in a small community than any penalty ([Restorative justice](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Restorative_justice)).

England did this for centuries in the manorial court and the court leet, local courts of neighbours dealing with boundaries, straying animals, nuisances and petty debts ([Manorial court](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Manorial_court); [Court leet](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Court_leet)). Serious crime is not for a village panel: record it, preserve what evidence you can, and hold it for a real court ([Death and grief](page:death-and-grief)).

## Land and property

Land is what people fight over, and English land law is well documented, so use it rather than invent.

- **Occupation is not ownership.** Titles, deeds and Land Registry entries survive the loss of the office that holds them. Copy your title number, plan and boundaries onto paper now, and keep the deeds dry ([Land registration](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Land_registration)).
- **Squatting.** Trespass in England and Wales is normally a civil matter, but the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012 made squatting in a residential building a criminal offence carrying up to 6 months in prison, an unlimited fine, or both — the fine was capped at £5,000 until 12 March 2015, and has had no ceiling since ([Squatting in England and Wales](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Squatting_in_England_and_Wales)). Occupying an empty house is therefore not a legal grey area; where people must be sheltered, get the owner's agreement if they can be found, and record the arrangement and the condition of the property.
- **Adverse possession.** Long occupation can eventually become title, but not quickly and not quietly: for registered land in England and Wales the squatter needs ten years' adverse possession and must then apply, the Land Registry must notify the registered owner, and the owner has 65 working days to object or serve a counter-notice, which usually but not always defeats the application: the squatter still wins on any of three grounds, including a reasonable mistaken belief about a boundary, and may reapply after a further two years. Unregistered land is different, and more dangerous for an absent owner: twelve years' adverse possession bars the owner's claim outright, with no notice and no counter-notice, under the Limitation Act 1980 s.15 — and much rural land in Britain is still unregistered ([Adverse possession](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Adverse_possession); [Land registration](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Land_registration)).
- **Requisition** is a government power exercised under emergency regulations, with a record and compensation, not something a committee may do ([Requisition](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Requisition); [Civil Contingencies Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/contents)). Where a village must use somebody's field, barn or generator, ask, agree a rent or a share, write it down, and expect to pay for it later. Record anything taken by anyone in uniform, with the date and the name.

## Records, and why paper

Paper needs no power, no drive and no password, is readable by anyone, and lasts centuries kept dry; a village that keeps no records loses its land, its debts and its dead within a generation ([Keeping the box alive](page:rebuild-keeping-the-box)).

England has kept these records for a long time. Parish registers of baptisms, marriages and burials were ordered in every parish on 5 September 1538, and civil registration of births, deaths and marriages began on 1 July 1837 ([Parish register](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Parish_register); [General Register Office](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/General_Register_Office_for_England_and_Wales)). The Domesday Book is what a written record of who holds what looks like when it outlives everyone in it ([Domesday Book](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Domesday_Book)).

Keep five books, bound, with numbered pages, entries in ink, dated, and signed by two people:

1. **People** — births, deaths, marriages, arrivals and departures, with enough detail for a registrar later ([Death and grief](page:death-and-grief)).
2. **Land and buildings** — who holds what, boundaries sketched, tenancies and agreed uses ([Cadastre](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Cadastre)).
3. **The ledger** — stores held, rations issued, work done, debts and credits ([Ledger](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Ledger); [Double-entry bookkeeping](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Double-entry_bookkeeping)).
4. **Decisions** — minutes, rules, disputes and their outcomes.
5. **The day count and the weather** — the calendar and the sowing dates ([The essentials, printed](page:rebuild-essentials-printed)).

No erasures: strike a mistake through so it stays readable and initial it. Copy the whole set once a year and keep the copy in another building. A tally stick, split lengthwise with each side holding half of the same notched record, is the pre-literate version of the same idea and neither party can alter it ([Tally stick](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Tally_stick)).

## Money, barter and the guarantee

Barter works badly at scale, because it needs both sides to want what the other has at the same moment ([Barter](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Barter)). What communities actually use, in this order:

- **Credit in the ledger.** Small settlements run on remembered obligation; writing it down is what stops the remembering going wrong.
- **Commodity money.** A good everyone wants and that keeps: grain, salt, fuel, tools, spirits, silver ([Commodity money](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Commodity_money)). Expect people to hoard the good money and spend the poor, which is Gresham's law ([Gresham's law](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Gresham's_law)).
- **A local scrip**, issued by the council against real stores and redeemable in them, with the issue written in the ledger; local currencies are ordinary and workable, and the danger is issuing more of it than the stores behind it ([Local currency](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Local_currency); [Hyperinflation](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Hyperinflation)).
- **Never pay in obligations to work for the issuer alone.** Paying wages in tokens that only one store accepts is the truck system, and Britain legislated against it for good reasons ([Truck system](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Truck_system)).

While banks and the state still exist, sterling deposits are protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme up to its published limit per eligible person per banking group — per person and per group, not per account — funded by the industry and paid within about seven working days for most claims; a two-name joint account is covered to twice the limit, and temporary high balances take longer to settle. The limit itself changes, so check it in the library rather than acting on a rumour, spread savings across banking groups rather than brands, and remember that a run is a queue that causes the failure it fears ([Financial Services Compensation Scheme](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Financial_Services_Compensation_Scheme); [Economic collapse](playbook:economic-collapse); [Deposit insurance](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Deposit_insurance)).

## Dealing with other groups

Trade is safer than raiding for both sides, and it is how a valley gets salt, iron and news. Make it boring and predictable:

- Meet in the open, on neutral ground, in daylight, few people each side, unarmed and visibly so.
- Send the same two people every time, one to talk and one to write.
- Agree what is traded and in what quantities, and write both copies before anything moves.
- Agree the next meeting before you part, and keep to it.
- Do not disclose your numbers, stores or defences, and do not display plenty ([Security and the law module](module:security-law)).
- Record every meeting, including anything promised and anything taken.

Treat rumours about other groups the way this box treats every rumour: check the source before acting on it ([Household emergency plan](page:household-plan)).

## Self-defence and the limits of force

The law of self-defence does not lapse because the police cannot come, but it is narrower than people think. Reasonable force to prevent crime and to make a lawful arrest comes from the Criminal Law Act 1967 s3 ([Criminal Law Act 1967 s3](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1967/58/section/3)); defending yourself or another person comes from the common law; and defending **property** is narrower still — you cannot use force on a trespasser who is committing no crime ([Self-defence in English law](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Self-defence_in_English_law)).

For a householder facing an intruder in the dwelling in England and Wales, grossly disproportionate force is never lawful, and force short of that must still be **reasonable** in the circumstances as you honestly believed them to be: the Court of Appeal in *R v Ray* [2017] was explicit that disproportionate force falling short of grossly disproportionate is not, by itself, reasonable force ([Criminal Justice and Immigration Act 2008 s76](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2008/4/section/76)). The householder rule gives latitude, not a licence. It covers defending **people** inside the home — not property, and not the garden. Force used after the danger has passed is not self-defence at all, it is assault.

Weapons law is unchanged: a folding non-locking knife with a cutting edge of 3 inches (7.62 cm) or less is the only blade that may be carried in public without good reason, and self-defence is not a good reason; pepper spray and stun guns are prohibited weapons; firearms need certificates ([Criminal Justice Act 1988 s139](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/33/section/139); [Knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law)). A village that arms itself against itself is the outcome to avoid: locks, lighting, a watch rota, agreements with neighbours and no displays of plenty do far more for safety than weapons ([Security and the law module](module:security-law)).

Write down every incident: what happened, when, who was there, what was done and why. That record protects the people who acted reasonably.
