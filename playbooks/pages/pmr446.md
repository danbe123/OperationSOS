## The rules

PMR446 radios are licence-free across the UK and most of Europe: 0.5 W effective radiated power, a fixed antenna, 16 analogue channels 12.5 kHz apart between 446.00625 and 446.19375 MHz ([PMR446](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/PMR446); [Ofcom IR 2030, table 3.18](doc:ofcom-pmr446-ir2009#p120)). Digital PMR446 (dPMR446 and DMR446) shares the same 446.0 to 446.2 MHz band. Range is one to three kilometres in a town and ten or more from a hilltop to a valley. No repeaters, no external antennas, no power boosting: a modified set is an unlicensed transmitter.

## Using them in an emergency

- Agree a channel and tone with your street or village and write it on the household plan ([Household plan](page:household-plan)); keep a second channel as a fallback.
- Keep messages short: who you are, where you are, what you need. Say "over" when you finish and "out" when you are done.
- Listen before you transmit; a call every 15 minutes at an agreed time saves batteries.
- Rechargeable sets die in a long outage; sets that take AA cells keep going with a torch's batteries. Keep radios warm; cold kills batteries.
- PMR446 is for local coordination. For reaching further, the amateur bands need a licence ([Amateur bands](page:amateur-bands)) and CB radio on 27 MHz is licence-free with 4 W ([Citizens band radio](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Citizens_band_radio)).
