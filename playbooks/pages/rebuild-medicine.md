## The lever that moves the most: sanitation

If you can do only one thing for the health of a village, keep human waste out of its water and off its hands. That is not a slogan. The World Health Organization's accounting is that in 2019 safe water, sanitation and hygiene could have prevented at least 1.4 million deaths, about 2.5% of all deaths in the world, over a million of them from diarrhoeal disease and 564,000 tied to unsafe sanitation in particular ([Sanitation](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Sanitation)). The Victorian record is starker: before Liverpool's sewers were built, life expectancy in the city was 19 years, and by the time the engineer who built them retired it had more than doubled ([History of water supply and sanitation](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/History_of_water_supply_and_sanitation)). No drug and no operation has ever matched that.

The same lesson runs through childbirth. In 1847, in a Vienna clinic where the doctors' wards had three times the mortality of the midwives', Ignaz Semmelweis made the staff wash their hands in chlorinated lime and the maternal death rate fell from 18% to under 2% ([Ignaz Semmelweis](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Ignaz_Semmelweis)). Soap, latrines dug well away from and downhill of the water, and a village that does not drink what it excretes are the whole of preventive medicine in a rebuild ([Sanitation module](module:sanitation); [Hand washing](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Hand_washing)).

## Clean water, and the drink that treats the diarrhoea anyway

Boiling kills everything, including the Cryptosporidium that chlorine does not; a filter takes out mud and parasites but not viruses. So filter and then boil, or filter and then chlorinate ([Water disinfection](page:water-disinfection); [Water Treatment Library](kiwix:zimgit-water_en/home)). At village scale that means a settling tank, a slow sand filter and a covered well with a hand pump ([Slow sand filter](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Slow_sand_filter); [Water module](module:water)). When diarrhoea comes anyway, the treatment is oral rehydration solution in small sips, clean water with salt and sugar to the recipe on the [dehydration card](card:dehydration) ([Oral rehydration therapy](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Oral_rehydration_therapy)).

## Childbirth and midwifery

Most births need a calm attendant and nothing else, and Where There Is No Doctor says so plainly: in a normal birth, the less the birth attendant does, the more likely all goes well ([WTIND, p. 305](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=305)). What kills is the bleeding afterwards. Postpartum haemorrhage is the leading cause of death in pregnancy worldwide, about 8.7 million cases a year and roughly a 3% risk of death where care is poor; it is prevented by oxytocin (or misoprostol where there is no cold chain) as soon as the baby is born, and treated with fluids, rubbing up the uterus and compressing it by hand ([Postpartum bleeding](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Postpartum_bleeding); [Childbirth card](card:childbirth)).

Prepare in the seventh month, not on the night: a new razor blade still wrapped, or boiled rust-free scissors, for the cord, plus soap, a scrub brush and clean cloth ([WTIND, p. 303](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=303)). Wash the mother's belly, genitals and legs, work in good light on clean sheets, and do not push on the belly or tell her to bear down in the first stage ([WTIND, p. 309](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=309)). Anyone with a fever, a cough or a skin infection should not deliver a baby ([WTIND, p. 305](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=305)). Train two or three midwives while there is still someone to learn from ([Midwifery](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Midwifery)).

## Teeth

Toothache disables a working adult as thoroughly as a broken arm, and dental infection can kill. Where There Is No Dentist is the whole trade in one book, and it is clear about limits: take a tooth out only when it hurts all the time, when it is loose and hurts to move, or when the root or crown is broken with the nerve exposed — and learn the technique from a person, not only from the page ([Where There Is No Dentist, p. 156](doc:where-there-is-no-dentist#page=156)). Root canal work needs a trained dental worker; extraction is what a village can realistically do.

For pain the book asks for lidocaine (lignocaine) 2% and a dental syringe with long 27-gauge needles, injected at the line where lip or cheek meets gum, aimed at the root, about 1 ml per root ([Where There Is No Dentist, p. 139](doc:where-there-is-no-dentist#page=139); [p. 142](doc:where-there-is-no-dentist#page=142)). An abscess is drained; spreading facial swelling with fever is an emergency in any era ([Dental abscess card](card:dental-abscess)). Brushing, with a brush or a frayed twig, and keeping sugar rare prevent far more than extraction cures.

## Anaesthesia: what is honestly possible

Modern anaesthesia is an industry, and none of it can be made in a village.

**Local infiltration, while lidocaine lasts, is the realistic answer.** It covers wound closure, an abscess, a tooth: injected into the wound margins through the wound for clean wounds, or as a field block about a centimetre out from the edge for dirty ones. The limit is toxicity — roughly 4.5 mg per kg of body weight, 7 mg per kg with adrenaline — and the first sign of overdose is tingling around the mouth, followed by a fit if you keep injecting, so stop at the tingling ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 218](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=218); [p. 220](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=220)). Hoard ampoules now: synthetic local anaesthetics cannot be manufactured in a low-technology setting at all ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 219](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=219)).

**Alcohol is not an anaesthetic.** Making a patient drunk rendered most of them insensible only until the first incision, and intravenous ethanol, genuinely used in the 1930s and 1940s, gives surgical anaesthesia at close to the dose that drops the blood pressure and stops the breathing ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 227](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=227)).

**Ether is the one general anaesthetic a rebuilt community might make, and it is dangerous.** It carried surgery from the 1840s to the 1950s and can be given by open drop onto a gauze-covered mask ([Diethyl ether](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Diethyl_ether); [History of general anesthesia](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/History_of_general_anesthesia)). The hazards are not small print. Ether is highly flammable: use it only in a very well ventilated space with no flame and no spark anywhere near ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 224](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=224)). Judging depth is an art, and cutting during the "false plane" of the first minute or two, before true anaesthesia at five to ten minutes, can stop the heart; one stage further on is respiratory arrest and death ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 225](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=225); [p. 226](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=226)). It needs a fasted patient, a way to hold the airway open, and two people — one giving, one watching and timing. Chloroform is easier to give and kills more people; it is not the answer ([Chloroform](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Chloroform)).

## Wounds, and the fight against infection

With no antibiotics, surgery goes back to what it was before them, and what saved lives then was cleanliness and drainage; Lister's carbolic antisepsis of the 1860s is the turning point worth reading ([Antiseptic](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Antiseptic); [Joseph Lister](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Joseph_Lister)).

The practice: stop the bleeding, irrigate with plenty of clean boiled water, take out dirt and dead tissue, and leave dirty, deep, bite and puncture wounds open rather than stitching them shut ([Wound cleaning card](card:wound-cleaning); [Wound closure card](card:wound-closure); [Debridement](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Debridement)). A closed dirty wound is how you make tetanus and gas gangrene. Boil instruments; a pressure cooker is an autoclave ([Autoclave](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Autoclave)). Watch for spreading redness, fever and confusion, because sepsis kills fast and the only treatments left are drainage and nursing ([Sepsis card](card:sepsis)). Tetanus is the disease to fear most: a deep dirty wound in someone whose vaccination is incomplete or unknown gets thorough washing and is never stitched, because half of those who develop tetanus die even in hospital ([WTIND, p. 138](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=138); [Tetanus](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Tetanus)). Dry antibiotics kept cool, dark and dry hold much of their potency for years past the printed date, tetracyclines being the class to discard ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 46](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=46); [WTIND, p. 381](doc:where-there-is-no-doctor#page=381)). Spend them on the infections that kill, and finish courses.

## What penicillin actually takes

Growing the mould is the easy part, and it is not the point. Fleming saw the effect in 1928 and the work stopped for a decade; it took Florey's Oxford team from 1939 — Chain, Heatley, Abraham, Jennings and the rest — to learn how to grow, extract, purify and assay the drug ([History of penicillin](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/History_of_penicillin)). Their first patient, the policeman Albert Alexander, began to recover in February 1941; the team ran out, recovered more penicillin from his urine, and it was still not enough. He relapsed and died on 15 March. Industrial quantities came only with a better strain, corn steep liquor and deep-tank fermentation, on a wartime national effort ([History of penicillin](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/History_of_penicillin)).

The arithmetic for a village: a single culture yields perhaps 100,000 to 200,000 units, about a third of one dose, so many cultures are needed for a single course, and low-technology production is possible but "probably not viable for most communities" ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 467](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=467)). Not all bread and fruit moulds are Penicillium, and of those that are, not all are the right strain ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 543](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=543); [Penicillium chrysogenum](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Penicillium_chrysogenum)). **Never put mouldy bread on a wound or eat it as medicine**: it delivers no useful dose, and many moulds make toxins. If a community does chase antibiotics, the sulfa drugs are the easier target, sulphanilamide being close to a college chemistry exercise ([Survival and Austere Medicine, p. 467](doc:survival-austere-medicine-2017#page=467)). Read the microbiology first and write down everything you try ([OpenStax Microbiology](doc:openstax-microbiology)).

## Herbal medicine, with the evidence attached

- **Willow bark** contains salicin, the compound behind aspirin, which Bayer synthesised in 1899; a bitter bark tea is a genuine painkiller ([Salicin](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Salicin); [Salix alba](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Salix_alba)). The cautions are aspirin's: not for children under 16, not in asthma, stomach ulcers or bleeding disorders, and an overdose damages the kidneys and gut ([Salicin](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Salicin)).
- **Honey on wounds** has the best evidence of any folk dressing: preliminary evidence that partial-thickness burns heal four to five days faster than with other dressings, and moderate evidence that post-operative infections treated with honey heal faster than with antiseptic and gauze. Evidence for other wounds is low quality, and it does not work for venous leg ulcers ([Honey](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Honey)).
- **Foxglove** is the warning. Digitalis genuinely acts on the heart and is also very toxic, causing slow heart rate, heart block, seizures and death ([Digitalis](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Digitalis)). With no way to measure the dose, do not use it.
- **What has no evidence:** homeopathy, which the scientific and medical community regards as pseudoscience and quackery, with no plausible mechanism and no sound evidence of effect ([Homeopathy](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Homeopathy)); and colloidal silver taken internally, which has no established benefit and permanently discolours the skin ([Medical uses of silver](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Medical_uses_of_silver)). Neither is worth a rebuild's scarce labour.

Write down every remedy tried, the dose and what happened: a village that keeps records is doing the thing that turned herbalism into medicine ([Herbal medicine](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Herbal_medicine)).

## Keeping the library, and teaching the next one

The medical library on this box outlasts the medicine chest: keep it powered, keep a second copy, and print the sheets you would need by candlelight ([Keeping the box alive](page:rebuild-keeping-the-box); [The essentials, printed](page:rebuild-essentials-printed)). The order to read in is on [The library map](page:rebuild-library-map). Teach from it while there is still a working screen: anatomy and physiology so people know what is inside them ([OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology](doc:openstax-anatomy-physiology-2e)), microbiology so germ theory survives as an idea rather than a rumour ([OpenStax Microbiology](doc:openstax-microbiology); [Germ theory of disease](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Germ_theory_of_disease)), then the handbooks. Apprentice two people to every skill — midwife, wound care, teeth, the pharmacy — so that no trade in the village is one death away from being lost.
