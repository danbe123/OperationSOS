## The one rule

Eat nothing you cannot name with certainty, and do not learn to name things from a book in a crisis. Wild food is a skill to build in good years, one plant at a time, with someone who knows. In the first days of an emergency it is not worth the risk; over months and years it is the difference between a hungry winter and a fed one. There is no test, no rule of thumb and no animal behaviour that tells you a plant or a fungus is safe. Universal edibility tests from survival manuals are written for other continents and will not protect you from what grows here.

## Plants that are common, easy and safe

- **Nettle** (spring to early summer, then the new growth after cutting): leaves cooked as greens or soup, rich in protein and iron; wear gloves, cook for a minute and the sting is gone ([Nettle](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Urtica_dioica)).
- **Wild garlic, ramsons** (March to May, damp woods, the smell is the identifier): leaves, flowers and bulbs. Its lookalikes, lily of the valley and lords-and-ladies, are poisonous and do not smell of garlic; crush a leaf every time ([Wild garlic](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Allium_ursinum)).
- **Dandelion**: leaves bitter but fine, roots roasted. **Chickweed, sorrel, hairy bittercress, fat hen, ground elder** are the everyday greens of waste ground and gardens.
- **Blackberry** (August to October), **elderflower and elderberry** (berries cooked, never raw, stems and leaves never), **rosehip** (deseeded, vitamin C), **hawthorn** berries and young leaves, **sloe**, **crab apple**, **rowan** cooked, **bilberry** on the moors, **wild strawberry and raspberry** ([Blackberry](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rubus_fruticosus); [Hawthorn](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Crataegus_monogyna)).
- **Hazelnut** (September, race the squirrels), **sweet chestnut** (spiny cases, pointed nuts, roast) but not horse chestnut (conkers, smooth cases, poisonous), **beech mast** in small amounts, **acorns** only after leaching in many changes of water ([Hazel](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Corylus_avellana)).
- **Coast**: **sea beet**, the wild ancestor of chard, on shingle and sea walls all year; **rock samphire** and **marsh samphire**; **sea kale**; **sea purslane**. Every seaweed on British shores is edible once washed and cooked, though not all are pleasant: dulse, laver, sugar kelp, sea lettuce ([Sea beet](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Sea_beet)).
- **Roots**: burdock, wild parsnip and wild carrot exist, but the parsnip and carrot families contain the killers below; leave roots to the experienced.

## The plants that kill

- **Hemlock water dropwort**: the most poisonous plant in Britain, growing in ditches, streams and wet meadows everywhere, with celery-like leaves, white umbrella flowers and roots like a bunch of small parsnips. It has killed foragers who took it for wild celery or parsnip. Anything in the carrot family growing in water is off limits ([Hemlock water dropwort](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Oenanthe_crocata)).
- **Hemlock**: purple-blotched hollow stems, mousy smell, waste ground and riverbanks ([Hemlock](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Conium_maculatum)).
- **Foxglove**: the leaves before flowering look like comfrey and have been eaten by mistake; it stops the heart ([Foxglove](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Digitalis_purpurea)).
- **Yew**: every part except the red flesh of the berry, and the seed inside that flesh is deadly ([Yew](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Taxus_baccata)).
- **Deadly nightshade**, **lords-and-ladies** (bright orange berries in woods), **laburnum**, **daffodil bulbs** (mistaken for onions), **bryony**, **monkshood**, **giant hogweed** (sap blisters skin in sunlight; do not touch) ([Deadly nightshade](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Atropa_belladonna); [Giant hogweed](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Heracleum_mantegazzianum)).

If someone has eaten something and you are not sure: call 999 or 111, keep a sample of the plant, and do not induce vomiting ([Medical module](module:medical)).

## Fungi

Britain has around thirty fungi that can kill and several thousand that will not, and the deadly ones (death cap, destroying angel, deadly webcap, fool's funnel) look ordinary. The death cap, responsible for most fungus deaths in Europe, grows under oak and beech across the country and tastes pleasant; the poison starts a day later and destroys the liver ([Death cap](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Amanita_phalloides)). Unless you have years of practice, the only fungi worth learning are the few with no dangerous lookalike in Britain: chanterelle, hedgehog fungus, giant puffball, cauliflower fungus, beefsteak fungus, chicken of the woods (which upsets some people). Learn each from a person, not a page, and never eat any fungus with white gills and a ring or a cup at the base ([Chanterelle](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Chanterelle)).

## The shore

Mussels, cockles, limpets, winkles and razor clams are the most reliable wild protein in Britain, on every rocky or sandy shore, and they need the most care. Take them from clean water away from harbours, outfalls and estuaries below towns; take only closed, live ones; cook them thoroughly; and never eat shellfish that stay shut after cooking. In warm months algal blooms make shellfish poisonous with toxins cooking does not destroy; local warnings and a "red tide" mean leave them alone ([Paralytic shellfish poisoning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Paralytic_shellfish_poisoning)). Crabs and prawns in rock pools, and fish from the rocks, follow the same water rules. Know the tide before you go out on any shore ([Moving across country](page:fieldcraft-moving)).

## Animals, and the law about them

Rabbit, wood pigeon, grey squirrel, deer and (in the south) wild boar are the meat on offer; pheasant, partridge and duck have seasons; badgers, most birds, bats, otters and wild birds' eggs are protected outright ([Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Wildlife_and_Countryside_Act_1981); [Hunting Act 2004](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Hunting_Act_2004)). Taking any of them on land that is not yours is poaching, and firearms, air rifles, snares and traps all carry rules: see [Knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law) and [Foraging and the law](page:foraging-law). Fishing in England and Wales needs a rod licence for freshwater and the owner's permission for the water; sea fishing from the shore needs neither ([Fishing licence](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Fishing_license)).

When the law no longer runs, the practical rules remain: kill cleanly, bleed and gut at once, cook thoroughly, and never eat an animal found dead or one that was sick. Roadkill is legal to take in Britain if you did not hit it, and pheasants and deer on the verge are a real food source in the countryside.

## Storing and making it last

Wild food is seasonal and gluts. Dry (mushrooms, apples, herbs, meat in strips over a fire), salt, pickle in vinegar, make jam with sugar while there is sugar, bury root crops in sand in a cool place, and smoke fish and meat. The [food module](module:food) and the [growing food module](module:growing-food) cover the household side; the wild side is a supplement to a garden, not a substitute for one.
