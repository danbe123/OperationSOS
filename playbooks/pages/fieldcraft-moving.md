## Where you may go

This is the home page for access law; the other pages only point here.

- **England and Wales**: public footpaths, bridleways, restricted byways and byways (the green dashes on the map), and open access land (the yellow-shaded mountain, moor, heath, down and registered common) under the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000 ([CRoW s2](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/37/section/2); [CROW Act](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Countryside_and_Rights_of_Way_Act_2000)). Access is on foot; it is not a right to camp, light fires or take anything. Trespass off those routes is a civil wrong, not a crime, unless you are on railways or military land, cause damage, or the trespass is aggravated ([Trespass in English law](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Trespass_in_English_law)).
- **Wild camping in England and Wales** needs the landowner's permission, with one exception: the Dartmoor commons, where the right to camp under the Dartmoor Commons Act 1985 was confirmed by the Court of Appeal in 2023 and upheld by the Supreme Court in May 2025 ([Dartmoor Commons Act 1985](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Dartmoor_Commons_Act_1985); [Wild camping](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Wild_camping)). Elsewhere a discreet late pitch and an early start is tolerated in many hill areas but is not a right. In a real emergency nobody will prosecute someone sheltering for a night.
- **Scotland**: a right of responsible access to almost all land and inland water under the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003, which includes wild camping, and small fires with care under the Scottish Outdoor Access Code ([Land Reform (Scotland) Act s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2003/2/section/1); [Scottish Outdoor Access Code](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Scottish_Outdoor_Access_Code)).
- **Northern Ireland and the Republic**: very few public rights of way; walk on waymarked trails, and on hill land by custom and with permission ([Navigation module](module:navigation)).

The offline map shows footpaths and access land as overlays ([Footpaths and access land on the map](map:?overlay=footpaths&overlay=access-land)). In an evacuation nobody will care where you walked, but farmers with livestock and crops will: leave gates and property as you find them or follow the instructions on the sign, because closing a gate a farmer has propped open can cut stock off from water, and shutting one he left shut is just as important. Stay on the marked path across a field, even if it is muddy; walking round the edge instead of along the line of the right of way is trespass. Keep dogs on a lead near stock, and on open access land a short lead near livestock and at all times from 1 March to 31 July, when ground-nesting birds are on the eggs ([The Countryside Code](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/The_Countryside_Code); [CRoW s2](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/37/section/2)). Fires are on [Fire](page:fieldcraft-fire) and taking things on [Foraging and the law](page:foraging-law).

## How fast you actually move

Naismith's rule: five kilometres an hour on the flat plus one minute for every ten metres of ascent, which is an extra hour for every 600 m of climb ([Naismith's rule](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Naismith's_rule)). The rule assumes a fit walker in ordinary conditions; a tired party with packs is slower, a family with small children and a pram far slower, and bog, tussock, bracken, heather and darkness each cut the speed again. Set out with a target you can reach in daylight with an hour to spare, and know where the next shelter is before you leave the last. Pace counting, timing a leg and walking on a bearing are on [Map, compass and tides](page:fieldcraft-navigation).

## Rivers

British rivers are small until it rains, and then they are not. A beck you stepped over in the morning can be waist-deep and killing by the evening; upland rivers rise within an hour of heavy rain and fall again a day later. The first rule is to wait or go round: a bridge, a ford marked on the map, or the headwaters. If you must cross:

- Choose a wide, shallow, slow stretch with a clean run-out below, never above a weir, rapid or fallen tree.
- Undo the waist belt and chest strap so you can shed the pack; keep boots on.
- Face upstream, lean on a pole, shuffle sideways, feet apart, one foot moving at a time.
- Groups: link arms in a line across the current, or in a huddle, the strongest upstream; or use a rope, but never tie it to anyone.
- Never cross fast water above the knee, cold water above the thigh, or anything you cannot see the bottom of.

If you are swept away: feet up and downstream, on your back, steer to the bank, and get out of wet clothes at once ([Cold shock](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Cold_shock_response); [Military Mountaineering, TC 3-97.61, the river-crossing chapter](doc:tc-3-97-61-military-mountaineering)).

## Bog, moor and mountain

Bright green sphagnum, cotton grass and pools mean deep bog; heather and bilberry mean firm ground. Test with a pole, walk on the tussocks, and if you sink to the thigh, spread your weight backwards and crawl out ([Bog](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Bog)). Peat hags, the eroded trenches of the Pennines and the Cairngorms, hide you from the wind and swallow hours. On the tops, the danger is the weather and the cliff in the mist: in poor visibility, walk on a bearing, count paces, and treat every edge as a drop ([Map, compass and tides](page:fieldcraft-navigation); [Reading the weather and exposure](page:fieldcraft-weather)).

## The coast

Britain has some of the largest tides in the world. Sands and estuaries such as Morecambe Bay, the Wash, the Solway and the Severn flood faster than a person can run, through channels that fill behind you first ([Morecambe Bay](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Morecambe_Bay); [Tide](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Tide)). Never walk out on open sands without a tide time you trust and a route; never round a headland on the beach without knowing when the tide turns; keep off cliff edges and cliff bases, which fall without warning. Working out the tide from the moon and the strand line is on [Map, compass and tides](page:fieldcraft-navigation). In the sea, rip currents run out through gaps in the surf: swim across them, not against ([Rip current](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rip_current)). The coastguard, not the police, is the 999 service on the shore ([Getting found](page:fieldcraft-rescue)).

## Livestock

Cows with calves are the danger, and nearly always because of a dog. Keep dogs on a lead near stock, but let the dog off the lead the moment livestock or horses threaten you and do not risk getting hurt protecting it, walk quietly, never between a cow and her calf, never turn your back and run ([The Countryside Code](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/The_Countryside_Code)). Treat any bull as dangerous. Horses are curious and heavy; sheep are harmless; pigs and boar are not to be cornered ([Wild boar](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Wild_boar)).

## At night

Move at night only with a reason: heat, pursuit, or a road you dare not use by day. Red light keeps night vision; a head torch on full ruins it for a while. Follow linear features, walls, tracks, rivers and field edges, count paces (your night pace is shorter, so measure it separately, [FM 3-25.26, p. 141](doc:fm-3-25-26-map-reading#page=141)), and stop every hour to listen. A British night in summer never gets fully dark in the north; in winter the dark lasts most of the day, and the box's Sun and moon tool gives the times. Fog on low ground and moor is the real hazard: in fog, stop.

## Carrying

A load you can carry all day is smaller than the one you can lift, and the pack that fits your back matters more than its size ([Camping and Woodcraft, the pack chapter](doc:kephart-camping-and-woodcraft)). Weight high and close to the back, hip belt taking most of it, water and waterproofs on top, the heaviest things nearest the spine. A wheelbarrow, a pram, a bicycle pushed with panniers, or a sledge on snow moves far more than a back does, and the roads and canal towpaths of Britain are made for wheels ([Evacuation module](module:evacuation); [Vehicles and fuel module](module:vehicles-fuel)).
