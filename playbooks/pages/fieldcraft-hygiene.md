## Why this page matters more than the fire

A group living outside a working sewer for a week will meet norovirus, campylobacter, giardia and cryptosporidium unless it keeps its water, its hands and its latrine apart ([Norovirus](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Norovirus); [Giardiasis](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Giardiasis); [Sanitation module](module:sanitation)). The rules are simple and boring, and they only work if everyone follows them every time.

## The latrine

- **At least 30 metres** from any well, spring, stream or other water, downhill of it and downwind of the camp, and never where surface water will run through it ([FM 4-25.12, p. 35](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=35)). The same 30 m rule is used for the household pit latrine in the [sanitation module](module:sanitation).
- A **cat hole** for one person for one halt: about 30 cm deep, filled and packed down after use ([FM 4-25.12, p. 34](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=34)). A **trench latrine** for a group staying a few days: about 30 cm wide, 75 cm deep and 120 cm long, allowing 60 cm of length per person, with the earth kept beside it and a shovelful thrown in after each use, and a board or lid over it ([FM 4-25.12, p. 36](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=36)). Fill it in before it is full and dig the next one.
- Toilet paper burned or buried; sanitary products and wipes carried out or burned, never buried, because animals dig them up.
- In a town without working sewers, a bucket with a bin liner and sawdust or soil, emptied into a deep pit well away from wells, is the standard; the [sanitation module](module:sanitation) covers the household version.
- On sensitive ground (peat, sand dunes, caves, above the treeline) everything is carried out.

{{#unless sewage}}
With the sewers out, the toilet indoors is a lined bucket rather than a hole dug fresh each day: sawdust, soil or ash after every use, a separate one for urine if you can manage it, a lid, and the contents emptied into a deep pit at least 30 metres from any well, spring or watercourse and downhill of it ([FM 4-25.12, p. 35](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=35); [Sanitation module](module:sanitation)). Do not flush into a blocked sewer. Keep the emptying to one person, with gloves and their own washing point.
{{/unless}}

## Hands

Wash hands with soap after the latrine and before touching food, every time; a hand-washing point stands outside every latrine ([FM 4-25.12, p. 35](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=35)). Where water is short, a "tippy tap": a bottle hung from a stick with a string to tilt it, so the group washes from a litre. Alcohol gel is unreliable against norovirus ([Norovirus](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Norovirus)) and does nothing to cryptosporidium, whose spores resist most disinfectants ([Cryptosporidiosis](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Cryptosporidiosis)); soap and water do the job. Nails short; one person handles the food.

{{#unless water}}
No tap means hand washing is rationed, not abandoned. Soap and half a litre poured from a jug or a tippy tap, over a bowl that catches the water for flushing the latrine, still does the job that alcohol gel does not do against norovirus and cryptosporidium ([Norovirus](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Norovirus); [Cryptosporidiosis](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Cryptosporidiosis)). Wash after the latrine and before food, every time. Give one person the job of keeping the washing point filled from treated water, and treat what you collect before it goes anywhere near hands or food ([Water disinfection](page:water-disinfection)).
{{/unless}}

## Food and animals

Cook thoroughly, eat straight away, and do not keep cooked food overnight without a fridge. Rats, mice, foxes and crows find any camp within a night; hang food from a branch in a bag or box, keep the cooking area away from the sleeping area, and bury or burn scraps far from camp and never within 30 m of water ([FM 4-25.12, p. 38](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=38)). Rat urine on stored food and in still water carries leptospirosis: covered containers and washed tin tops are the defence ([Water outdoors](page:fieldcraft-water)).

## Skin and feet

Trench foot, the wet-cold injury, ends more British expeditions than frostbite. It happens above freezing, can begin within about ten hours of wet cold feet, and is made likelier by tight boots and not moving; the first signs are tingling, itching and numbness, then swollen, white and later red, painful feet ([Trench foot](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Trench_foot)). Prevention is dry socks every night, feet out of boots and dried when resting, boots loose enough to wriggle toes, and feet checked daily; treatment is dry, warm (not hot), raised and rested, and it takes days. Wash the body, especially groin, armpits and feet, once a day if there is any water at all; a cloth and half a litre is enough ([FM 4-25.12, p. 97](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=97)). Treat blisters early with a plaster over clean skin, and small cuts by washing them; in field conditions an infected cut becomes a real problem within days ([Wound cleaning card](card:wound-cleaning)).

## Things that bite

- **Ticks**: check the whole body each evening in bracken, heather and long grass; removal, Lyme disease and adders are on [Ticks and adders](page:ticks-adders).
- **Midges**: the Highland midge swarms from late spring to late summer in the north-west of Scotland and down the west coast to north Wales, worst near dawn and dusk in still, damp, cloudy weather; it is grounded by wind over about 10 km/h and by dry sun, and it is kept off by covered skin, a head net and repellents such as DEET ([Highland midge](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Highland_midge)). Camp on a breezy knoll, not in the sheltered hollow by the loch.
- **Horseflies (clegs)**: the females bite hard through thin clothing on warm summer days and the bite swells; cover up and treat the bite as a wound ([Horsefly](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Tabanidae)).
- **Wasps**: most stings come in late summer and early autumn, when colonies stop raising workers and the adults hunt sugar; keep food and drink covered and look before you sip from a can ([Wasp](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Wasp)). Stings, and the swelling that is an emergency, are on the [bites and stings card](card:bites-stings) and the [anaphylaxis card](card:anaphylaxis).
- **DEET**: the standard repellent for ticks, midges and mosquitoes. Concentrations above 50% add nothing to the protection time; 50% is the NHS advice for malaria areas and 10% for children; it dissolves some synthetic fabrics and plastics, so keep it off waterproofs, watch faces and compass housings ([DEET](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/DEET)). Wash hands after applying it and keep it out of eyes; one application lasts up to about twelve hours in normal conditions, less in heat and sweat ([FM 4-25.12, p. 143](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=143)). Icaridin is the newer alternative ([Insect repellent](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Insect_repellent)).

## Washing kit and clothes

Wet kit dries in wind, not in a tent; a line under the tarp edge and a fire at a distance. Sleeping bags aired every dry morning. Boots stuffed with dry grass or paper overnight. Clothes washed in a bucket with soap, rinsed well away from streams, because even biodegradable soap poisons the small life in cold water.

## When someone is ill

Diarrhoea and vomiting: isolate them with their own latrine and bowl, the rest of the group washes hands more, and the patient drinks oral rehydration solution made up with treated water, in the amounts on the [dehydration card](card:dehydration). Most bouts pass in a few days ([Norovirus](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Norovirus)); blood in the stool, a high fever, or a child or old person who cannot keep fluids down needs medical help ([Medical module](module:medical)).

{{#if scenario:pandemic}}
**Everything here counts twice over.** One person handles the food and a different person nurses the sick; the ill person has their own bowl, cup, cloth and latrine or bucket; hands are washed with soap after every contact, and the mask and apron kept for nursing come off and stay outside the sleeping area ([FM 4-25.12, p. 35](doc:fm-4-25-12-field-sanitation#page=35); [Sanitation module](module:sanitation)). Keep the group spread out and outdoors as much as the weather allows, and let anyone with diarrhoea and vomiting recover apart, on oral rehydration solution ([dehydration card](card:dehydration)).
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:storms-flooding}}
Floodwater is sewage. Keep children and pets out of it, wash hands and any skin it touched, throw away food and tins whose seals were under it, and cover and watch every cut, because a fever with muscle pain about a week later can be leptospirosis ([UKHSA flooding and health](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/flooding-and-health-public-advice/flooding-and-health-advice-for-the-public); [Leptospirosis (WikiMed)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Leptospirosis)). Move the latrine and the camp above the water and off the flooded ground, and treat every local water source as contaminated until it has been boiled ([Water disinfection](page:water-disinfection)).
{{/if}}

## Leaving

Fill the latrine, scatter the fire site cold, pick up every scrap of plastic and foil, and leave the place so that the next family, or you next year, can use it. The Countryside Code is good manners in normal times and good sense in bad ones ([The Countryside Code](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/The_Countryside_Code)).
