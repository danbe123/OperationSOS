## Ticks

This is the home page for ticks; the field-craft pages only point here. Remove a tick with a tick tool or a pair of fine-pointed tweezers, gripping as close to the skin as possible and pulling straight out with steady pressure. Never squeeze the body, and never try to burn it off or smother it with oil or petroleum jelly, which can make it inject more saliva. Clean the bite site afterwards with soap and water or an antiseptic. Prevention: long trousers tucked into socks in bracken, heather and long grass, a DEET repellent on skin and clothing, and a check of the whole body every evening between spring and autumn, when ticks are most active ([Lyme disease](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Lyme_disease)). Midges, horseflies, wasps and how to use DEET are on the [hygiene page](page:fieldcraft-hygiene).

## Lyme disease

Watch for a rash that spreads outwards from the bite, often but not always ring-shaped (erythema migrans), which usually appears about a week after the bite but can take longer, or a flu-like illness with fever, headache and tiredness. Either sign means antibiotics: see a GP, or [[call 111]]. Doxycycline is the usual treatment ([Lyme disease](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Lyme_disease); [Erythema migrans](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Erythema_migrans)). Lyme disease is the main tick-borne illness in the UK ([Lyme disease](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Lyme_disease)).

## Adders

The adder is the only venomous snake native to Britain, and there are no snakes at all in Ireland ([Adder](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Adder); [Fauna of Ireland](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Fauna_of_Ireland)). It is not aggressive and bites only when trodden on, picked up or cornered; bites are painful but rarely fatal, and dogs are bitten far more often than people ([Adder](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Adder)). If someone is bitten: keep them still and calm, immobilise the bitten limb, remove any rings or watches near the bite before swelling starts, wash the bite with soap and water, and note the time of the bite. Do not apply a tourniquet, do not cut the wound, do not try to suck out venom, and do not apply ice ([Snakebite](kiwix:wikipedia_en_medicine_maxi/Snakebite)). Antivenom is held at hospital, not carried in the field, so get them to A&E — [[call 999]].
