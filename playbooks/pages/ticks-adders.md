## Ticks

Remove a tick with a tick tool or a pair of fine-pointed tweezers, gripping as close to the skin as possible and pulling straight out with steady pressure. Never squeeze the body, and never try to burn it off or smother it with oil or petroleum jelly — this can make it inject more saliva. Clean the bite site afterwards with soap and water or an antiseptic. Prevention: wear long trousers tucked into socks in bracken and long grass, and check the whole body daily between spring and autumn, when ticks are most active.

## Lyme disease

Watch for a rash that spreads outwards from the bite over three days to a month, often but not always ring-shaped (erythema migrans), or a flu-like illness with fever and fatigue. Either sign means antibiotics: see a GP, or [[call 111]]. Doxycycline is the usual treatment, typically for around three weeks ([Lyme disease (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/lyme-disease/); [Lyme disease](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Lyme_disease)).

## Adders

The adder is the only venomous snake native to Britain; there are no snakes at all in Ireland. About 100 people are bitten a year, and deaths are extremely rare — dogs are bitten far more often than people. If someone is bitten: keep them still and calm, immobilise the bitten limb below the level of the heart, remove any rings or watches near the bite before swelling starts, and note the time of the bite. Do not apply a tourniquet, do not cut the wound, do not try to suck out venom, and do not apply ice. Antivenom is held at hospital, not carried in the field, so get them to A&E — [[call 999]] ([Snake bites (NHS)](kiwix:nhs_uk/www.nhs.uk/conditions/snake-bites/); [Vipera berus](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Vipera_berus)).
