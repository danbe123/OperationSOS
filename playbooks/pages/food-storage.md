## The five rules for dry staples

1. **Dry.** Grain keeps only if it is dry: farmers store it at 12 to 13% moisture, and a kernel that cracks hard between the teeth rather than squashing is dry enough ([Storing grain (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/GRNSTOR3.HTM); [Grain preparation (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/GRAINPRP.HTM)). Bought rice, flour, oats and pulses arrive dry; the job is to keep them that way, so never decant into a damp container or store against an outside wall that sweats.
2. **Sealed and rigid.** Flour, rice, oats and pulses go in rigid, sealed containers, which keep out moisture, insects and rodents; glass jars for the kitchen, food-grade plastic buckets with lids, or metal drums and bins for bulk ([Food storage](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Food_storage); [Storing grain (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/GRNSTOR3.HTM)). Paper sacks are for the shop, not the store.
3. **Cool and dark.** Oxygen, light and heat turn oils and fats rancid, and the higher the polyunsaturated fat the faster it goes: keep oil in dark glass or metal, and once opened use it within a few weeks ([Food storage](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Food_storage); [Rancidification](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rancidification)). Whole grains outlast the flour milled from them because milling exposes the oil in the germ to the air, and the bran of oats carries an enzyme that turns them rancid unless they were steamed ([Rolled oats](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rolled_oats)).
4. **No air.** An oxygen absorber is a sachet of iron powder and salt that rusts inside a sealed container and takes the oxygen down below 0.01%, which stops insects and slows rancidity; it works only in a container that is genuinely airtight and needs some humidity to start ([Oxygen scavenger](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Oxygen_scavenger)). Sealed buckets or heat-sealed foil bags of white rice, wheat, oats or pulses with an absorber inside are the long-term store; the working store is smaller jars you open.
5. **Rotate.** Date every container, put new stock at the back and eat from the front, and open the long-term store one container at a time.

{{#unless power}}
The dry store is untouched by a power cut, but the cold store is now on a clock: a closed fridge holds about four hours and a freezer about 48 hours full, or 24 hours half full ([CMO food advice](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/public-health-advice-from-uk-cmos-during-a-national-power-outage/food-and-nutrition-scripts-for-broadcast-media); [What still works](page:what-still-works)). Keep both shut, eat the fridge down first and the freezer in the order it thaws, cook what has thawed thoroughly and do not refreeze it. Wheat becomes flour by hand now, so know where the mill or the quern is ([Grain mill (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Grain_mill)).
{{/unless}}

{{#unless shops}}
The store is now the whole supply, so count it before you eat into it: work out how many days it covers from the 2,000 kcal table below, write that number on the cupboard door, and ration to it rather than to appetite ([Food module](module:food)). Open one sealed container at a time and keep the rest sealed against damp and weevils. Remember that dried staples give nothing until you have the water and the fuel to cook them, so count those too ([Water disinfection](page:water-disinfection)).
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:heat-drought}}
Heat is the enemy of a store: oils and fats turn rancid faster the warmer they are, and tins keep indefinitely only if they are never frozen and never kept above 32 °C ([Rancidification](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rancidification); [Canning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Canning)). Move the store to the coolest place you have, a cellar or a north-facing cupboard at floor level rather than a loft or a sunny outbuilding, keep oil in dark glass or metal, check more often for insects, and freeze or heat any new stock before it joins the long-term store ([Rice weevil](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rice_weevil)).
{{/if}}

## Weevils, moths and rats

- Grain and rice weevils lay eggs inside kernels, so a clean-looking bag can already be infested. Every stage of the rice weevil dies below −18 °C for three days or at 60 °C for 15 minutes ([Rice weevil](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rice_weevil)); the Indian-meal moth, whose webbing you find in flour and oats, dies after a week of freezing or brief heating in an oven ([Indian-meal moth](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Indianmeal_moth)). Freeze or heat new stock before it goes into the long-term store, sieve anything that shows insects, and use it: weevils are a nuisance, not a poison.
- Rats and mice get in through any hole; fill and seal every crack, patch wood with sheet metal or flattened tins, keep the store swept of spilt grain, and keep stock off the floor ([Storing grain (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/GRNSTOR3.HTM)). A cat earns its keep here.
- Mould means the grain got damp: newly harvested grain brought under cover carries excess moisture that encourages mould unless it is dried ([Granary](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Granary)). Throw mouldy grain away rather than pick through it.

## How long each keeps

| Food | Keeps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Whole wheat, barley, dried peas and beans | Longest of all, kept dry and sealed | Whole grain outlasts the flour milled from it ([Rancidification](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rancidification)); mill or soak as you need it |
| White rice | Years, dry and sealed | Freeze or heat against weevils first ([Rice weevil](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rice_weevil)) |
| Flour, especially wholemeal | Months rather than years | The germ oil goes rancid; smell it before use ([Rancidification](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rancidification)) |
| Rolled oats | Months to a year or two | Steamed at the mill for shelf life; unsteamed oat groats go rancid sooner ([Rolled oats](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rolled_oats)) |
| Pasta | Years, dry and sealed | Dry pasta is flour with the water taken out; treat as flour that keeps |
| Tins | Safe indefinitely if never frozen or kept above 32 °C; best quality 12 to 18 months for fruit and tomatoes, 2 to 5 years for meat and vegetables | Discard any tin that is dented on a seam, rusted or swollen ([Canning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Canning); [Shelf life](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Shelf_life)) |
| Honey | Indefinitely, as long as its water stays below about 18% | Keeps the lid on and the jar dry ([Honey](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Honey)) |
| Sugar and salt | Indefinitely, kept dry | They cake when damp but do not spoil |
| Powdered milk | Far longer than liquid milk, because of its low moisture | Seal after opening ([Powdered milk](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Powdered_milk)) |
| Cooking oil | Unopened, a long time in the dark; opened, a few weeks | Rancid oil often does not smell rancid until well after it has spoiled ([Food storage](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Food_storage)) |
| Hard biscuit (hardtack) | Years, if kept dry | Twice-baked flour and water, the navy's answer to the same problem ([Hardtack](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Hardtack)) |

"Best before" dates on dry goods are about quality, not safety; the date that matters on a tin is the state of the tin.

## Milling wheat by hand

Whole wheat stores; flour does not. A hand grain mill that clamps to a table, the Corona pattern and its many copies, grinds with steel burrs for coarse meal or stone burrs for flour, and can be turned by hand, bicycle or motor ([Grain mill (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Grain_mill)). Without a mill, a pestle and mortar or a saddle quern works but is slow: pounding grain gives roughly 4 kg an hour, and a household grinding 4 to 6 kg a day spends over an hour a day at it ([Traditional grinding methods (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Analysis_of_the_various_types_of_mills/Traditional_methods_of_grinding_grain); [Quern-stone](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Quern-stone)). Grind only what you will use in a week, sieve for finer flour, and use coarse meal for porridge and flatbread rather than fighting for a white loaf. Wheat can also be soaked and boiled whole, or sprouted, which needs no mill at all.

## What 2,000 kcal looks like

Adults need roughly 2,000 kcal a day for a woman and 2,500 for a man, more for heavy work outdoors ([Food module](module:food)); the United States puts the range at 2,000 to 2,600 ([Food energy](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Food_energy)). Carbohydrate and protein give about 4 kcal a gram and fat 9, so fat and oil are the densest thing in the store ([Food energy](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Food_energy)). The table gives the weight of each food that supplies 2,000 kcal, from the figures in the library:

| Rolled oats, dry | 379 | about 530 g | [Rolled oats](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rolled_oats) |
| Wholemeal wheat flour | 339 | about 590 g | [Wheat flour](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Wheat_flour) |

So two weeks for one adult is on the order of 8 kg of dry rice, oats or flour, plus pulses, oil, sugar and tins for the protein, fat and taste; potatoes and beans alone mean eating two kilos a day. Nobody eats one food: use the table to check that the store as a whole adds up, and remember that dried staples give nothing until you have the water and the fuel to cook them.

## Water to cook them

Every dry staple needs drinking-quality water on top of the 3 litres a person a day for drinking ([Water module](module:water); [Water disinfection](page:water-disinfection)). Rice absorbs water and grows in volume and mass as it cooks, and newer or softer rice needs less ([Cooked rice](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Cooked_rice)); oats cook in water, milk or a mix ([Porridge](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Porridge)); lentils boil in 10 to 40 minutes depending on the kind, and small skinned red lentils need no soaking ([Lentil](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Lentil)); dried red kidney beans must be soaked and then boiled hard for at least 10 minutes (the US FDA says 30) to destroy their toxin, and a slow cooker at 80 °C does not do it ([Kidney bean](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Kidney_bean)). Pasta and pulses that must be boiled in a pan of water are the thirstiest and the most fuel-hungry choices; rice, oats and flatbread from flour use least. Cook once a day in bulk, keep the lid on, and use a haybox or blankets round the pan to finish cooking pulses off the flame.

## The right to an allotment

A traditional ten-rod plot is about 250 square metres ([Allotment](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Allotment_(gardening))). Under the Small Holdings and Allotments Act 1908, as amended up to 1950, a council has a duty to provide sufficient allotments to meet demand, and section 23 of the 1908 Act is triggered by a written request from six registered electors or council-tax payers of the parish or borough ([Allotment](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Allotment_(gardening)); [Allotments (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Allotments)). In a long food shortage that letter is the first thing a street should send. By 1943, under Dig for Victory, Britain had about 1.4 million allotments ([Dig for Victory](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Victory_garden)); what to grow on one is in the [growing food module](module:growing-food).
