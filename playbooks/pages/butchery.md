## The law

In England the Welfare of Animals at the Time of Killing Regulations 2015 govern how any animal is killed: they list the permitted stunning and killing methods (captive bolt, percussive blow to the head, electrical stunning, bleeding) and make separate provision for poultry, rabbits and hares killed and bled by their owner for private domestic consumption, which is the case this page is about ([WATOK 2015](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1782/contents)). Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own equivalent regulations. The Animal Welfare Act 2006 duty to avoid unnecessary suffering applies right up to the moment of death ([Animal Welfare Act 2006](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/45/contents)). Meat you kill at home is for your own household, not for sale; a captive-bolt gun or a rifle brings in [knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law); and wild animals bring in [foraging, fishing and hunting law](page:foraging-law). The [livestock module](module:livestock) covers keeping the animals.

The practical rule that sits under all the law: the animal must be unconscious before it feels anything, and dead before you cut. Decide the method, have everything ready, work fast and quietly, and never let the other animals watch.

## Before you start

Sharp knife, a second knife, a rope or hook to hang the carcass, two buckets, clean water, a table you can scrub, and gloves: rabbits and hares can carry tularaemia, which passes through cuts in the skin when skinning and through undercooked meat, and gloves and thorough cooking are the protection ([Tularemia](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Tularemia)). Never eat an animal that was sick or found dead ([Wild food in Britain and Ireland](page:fieldcraft-food)). Rabbits fed on grass taste better if given a week on grain or pellets before killing ([Rabbit production (FAO)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/CD3WD/AGRIC/R0041E/B181_18.HTM)).

## Rabbits

1. **Kill.** Hold the rabbit by the hind legs in one hand; put the thumb of the other hand on the neck just behind the ears with the fingers round the neck; press down hard with the thumb while pulling the rabbit sharply upwards, which dislocates the neck. The alternative is a hard chopping blow with a heavy stick to the back of the neck just behind the ears ([Raising rabbits (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/RABBITS.HTM)). Done fast and firmly, dislocation separates the spinal cord from the brain at once and is the accepted humane method for small animals ([Cervical dislocation](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Cervical_dislocation)). If in doubt that it is dead, strike again immediately.
2. **Bleed and hang.** Hang it at once by one hind leg with twine or a nail through the hock, and cut off the head so it bleeds out into a bucket ([Raising rabbits (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/RABBITS.HTM)).
3. **Skin.** Cut off the front feet and the free hind foot; cut the skin round the hanging hock and down the inside of each hind leg to the vent; then take hold of the skin and pull it down off the body like a sock, inside out, over the front legs and off; the knife is only needed to free it where it sticks ([Raising rabbits (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/RABBITS.HTM); [Simplified tanning (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Back_Yard_and_Commercial_Rabbit_Production/Simplified_tanning)). On any small mammal, cutting the hide round the body and pulling the two halves apart with two fingers under the skin does the same job ([FM 21-76, p. 96](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=96)).
4. **Draw.** Slit the belly from vent to breastbone without cutting the gut, starting by freeing the bladder so no urine touches the meat; remove everything except the kidneys, liver and heart, which are good to eat ([Raising rabbits (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/RABBITS.HTM); [Simplified tanning (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Back_Yard_and_Commercial_Rabbit_Production/Simplified_tanning)). Look at the liver: it should be smooth, wet and deep red or purple; a spotty or pale liver is discarded, though the muscle meat is still fine ([FM 21-76, p. 96](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=96)).
5. Wash, and cook the same day or joint into legs, saddle and ribs for the pot.

## Poultry

1. **Kill.** Hang the bird by its feet, or put it head down in a cone of sheet metal or an old traffic cone with the tip cut off so it hangs calm, and cut across the veins in the throat just below the jaw with a sharp knife; it bleeds out in the bucket beneath ([Chickens and ducks (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/CHICDUCK.HTM); [Chickens (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Chickens)). Neck dislocation, a sharp pull and twist down and away with the bird held by the legs, is the other household method and must be done in one movement ([Animal slaughter](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Animal_slaughter)). Kill birds the day the meat is to be used unless you can chill it ([Chickens and ducks (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/CHICDUCK.HTM)).
2. **Pluck.** Dip the bird for a minute in water at 54 to 57 °C, below the boil but too hot to hold a hand in, and pluck at once while the feathers are wet; a drop of soap in the water helps, ducks especially; singe the small feathers over a flame ([Chickens and ducks (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/CHICDUCK.HTM)). Skinning instead of plucking is quicker but loses the fat under the skin ([FM 21-76, p. 96](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=96)).
3. **Draw.** Cut a small circle round the vent, open towards the breast enough to get a hand in, and pull out the intestines; go in at the neck for the crop and windpipe; keep the heart, liver and the gizzard, which is slit and emptied ([Chickens and ducks (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/CHICDUCK.HTM)). Cut off the feet, and boil any bird that scavenges (gulls, crows) for at least 20 minutes for parasites before cooking it further ([FM 21-76, p. 96](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=96)).

## Sheep and goats, briefly

A sheep or goat is a two-person job and a legal one: it must be stunned first, by captive bolt or another method the regulations permit, and then bled at once by cutting the throat vessels ([WATOK 2015](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1782/contents)). The cut goes in behind the angle of the jaw and forward away from the spine to sever both jugulars, both carotids and the windpipe; with the vessels open, death follows in seconds ([Exsanguination](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Exsanguination)). Hoist the animal by the hind legs immediately after stunning so it bleeds properly, and keep it hanging for skinning and gutting so the meat never touches the ground ([Slaughter facilities (FAO)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/INPHO/VLIBRARY/S1250E/S1250E18.HTM)). Then as for any large animal: split the hide from throat to tail with the blade turned up so only the hide is cut, cut round the sexual organs and the anus, cut the gullet free of the diaphragm and roll the entrails out, pinch off and remove the bladder, and save the heart and liver after inspecting them ([FM 21-76, p. 96](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=96)). Sheep livers often carry liver fluke: discard a liver with white tracks or lumps ([Liver fluke](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Liver_fluke)).

**Jointing.** The front legs are held on by muscle alone and cut off with no joint to find; the hindquarters come off at the hip by cutting round the big bone to the ball and socket and bending it back; the loins are the long muscles either side of the spine; then the ribs, which are easier broken first and cut at the breaks ([FM 21-76, p. 97](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=97)). Big pieces roast or boil; the scraps on the bone go in the stock pot.

## Hanging

Meat stiffens after death and then softens as its own enzymes work; hanging develops flavour and tenderness, but only in the cold. Beef is hung at 1 to 3 °C and about 85% humidity for a minimum of eleven days and rarely more than 20 to 30; warmer and it spoils, colder and it freezes and the process stops ([Meat hanging](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Meat_hanging)). Game was traditionally hung until "high" ([Meat hanging](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Meat_hanging)). Without a fridge, that means: hang in the coldest airy place you have, out of sun, in a muslin bag against flies, for a day or two in winter and not at all in summer, when rabbits and poultry are cooked the day they are killed and larger carcasses are salted, smoked or dried at once ([Chickens and ducks (VITA)](kiwix:cd3wdproject.org_en_all/www.cd3wdproject.org/VITAHTML/SUBLEV/EN1/CHICDUCK.HTM)). Whatever the animal, cook meat to 74 °C right through; this kills the parasites that pork and wild game can carry and that freezing does not ([Trichinosis](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Trichinosis)).

## Smoking, salting and drying

- **Smoking.** Build a small enclosure round a low fire; the aim is smoke, not heat. Use hardwood, somewhat green or soaked, never resinous softwood, which taints the meat. Cut the meat in thin slices, hang them so none touch, and keep the smoke round them: a night's smoking keeps meat about a week, two days' continuous smoking two to four weeks, and properly smoked meat is dark, curled and brittle and can be eaten without cooking ([FM 21-76, p. 97](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=97)). Cold smoking at 20 to 30 °C does not cook, so meat must be salted or cured first; smoke on its own flavours the surface but does not preserve ([Smoking (cooking)](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Smoking_(cooking))).
- **Salting.** Salt preserves by drawing the water out of microbes, which takes a salt concentration of nearly 20% ([Curing](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Curing_(food_preservation))). Pack pieces in dry salt, or cover them completely in a brine strong enough to float an egg, in a crock in a cool place; meat in a good brine keeps for weeks, and is soaked in fresh water before cooking ([Smoking meat (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Smoking_Meat); [FM 21-76, p. 98](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=98)).
- **Drying.** Cut meat into strips about 6 mm thick along the grain, hang on a rack in sun and moving air, out of reach of animals and covered against blowflies; it is done when dry, crisp and no longer cool to the touch ([FM 21-76, p. 98](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=98)). British air is rarely dry enough for this without a fire or a stove; combine drying with smoke or salt.
- **Fat-sealing.** Cooked sausage or pieces of meat packed in jars and covered with hot rendered lard keep far longer than raw ([Smoking meat (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Smoking_Meat)). Freezing keeps meat indefinitely but is not cooking: it must still be cooked ([FM 21-76, p. 98](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=98)).

Home-canning meat needs a pressure canner; water-bath canning it risks botulism ([Food module](module:food); [USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning](kiwix:usda-2015_en/home)).

## Offal, fat, bones and hide

- Heart, liver, kidneys, spleen, pancreas, tongue (skinned and boiled) and brain are all eaten and cooked like muscle meat; they spoil first, so eat them on the day ([FM 21-76, p. 97](doc:fm-21-76-survival#page=97)). Intestines, cleaned, are sausage casings, and the blood makes black pudding ([Offal](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Offal)).
- Fat is rendered by gentle heat with a little water into lard (pig) or tallow (sheep, goat, cattle), which cook, fry, seal jars and make candles and soap ([Lard](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Lard); [Tallow](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Tallow); [Rendering](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rendering_(animal_products))). Bones go in the stock pot, then the fire.
- The hide: scrape off every scrap of flesh and fat, then either salt it heavily on the flesh side (about 1 kg of salt to a rabbit pelt after a night in strong brine) and tan with alum and salt, wash, oil and work it soft as it dries ([Simplified tanning (Appropedia)](kiwix:appropedia_en_all/Back_Yard_and_Commercial_Rabbit_Production/Simplified_tanning)); or stretch it on a frame and dry it into rawhide for lashings, soles and drum skins ([Rawhide](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Rawhide_(material))); or bark-tan it with oak bark for leather ([Tanning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Tanning_(leather))). Feathers stuff pillows.
- What is left, the gut contents and unusable parts, is buried well away from any well or watercourse or composted hot, never left for rats and dogs ([Sanitation module](module:sanitation)).
