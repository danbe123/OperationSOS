## Plants and fungi

This is the home page for foraging, fishing and hunting law; the field-craft pages only point here. The law differs between the nations, so check which heading applies.

- **England and Wales**: picking wild fruit, foliage, fungi and flowers for your own use is not theft under the Theft Act 1968, which applies only in England and Wales; picking to sell is a different matter and can be ([Theft Act s4](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/60/section/4); [Theft Act 1968](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Theft_Act_1968)).
- **Scotland**: Scots law has no equivalent to the Theft Act, but the right of responsible access does not extend to gathering for commercial gain, while the customary picking of wild fungi and berries for your own eating is not prohibited under the Scottish Outdoor Access Code ([Scottish Outdoor Access Code](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Scottish_Outdoor_Access_Code); [Land Reform (Scotland) Act s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2003/2/section/1)).
- **Everywhere in Great Britain**: uprooting any wild plant without the landowner's permission is an offence, and a short list of especially rare plants under Schedule 8 of the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 may not be picked or disturbed at all; the Act extends to England, Wales and Scotland ([WCA s13](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69/section/13); [Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Wildlife_and_Countryside_Act_1981)). Northern Ireland has its own wildlife order with the same shape: assume the same rules apply.
- Byelaws on many nature reserves, and in places such as the New Forest and Epping Forest, forbid picking fungi entirely, whatever the general law allows.

Before eating anything foraged, check it is correctly identified, cooked if it needs to be, and not from a contaminated spot: a roadside verge, a dog-walking area, or a field that has recently been sprayed ([FSA safe foraging](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-for-safe-foraging/guidance-for-safe-foraging)). What is safe and what kills is on [Wild food in Britain and Ireland](page:fieldcraft-food).

{{#unless shops}}
The law does not lapse because the shops are shut. Picking wild fruit, foliage, fungi and flowers for your own use is still not theft in England and Wales, uprooting any wild plant without the landowner's permission is still an offence across Great Britain, and the rod licence and the close season still apply ([Theft Act s4](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/60/section/4); [WCA s13](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69/section/13)). Ask the landowner rather than assume: a hedge you have permission to pick is worth far more over a season than one you are chased off.
{{/unless}}

{{#if scenario:famine}}
Hunger is not a defence, and the rules worth keeping are the ones that leave the hedge producing next year: take a share and leave the rest, never uproot, leave the rare Schedule 8 plants alone, and keep off the reserves and forests whose byelaws forbid picking entirely ([WCA s13](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69/section/13)). Wild birds, their nests and their eggs are protected throughout the UK unless a close-season quarry species, a general licence or an individual licence applies, and Northern Ireland runs on its own 1985 Order; a snare is banned outright in Wales and Scotland and lawful only in limited form in England, so check the current law where you are ([WCA s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69/section/1); [WCA Part I](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69/part/I)).
{{/if}}

{{#if scenario:long-rebuild}}
Over years the question stops being what the law allows and becomes what the land can stand and what the neighbours have agreed, so settle it in the open: who gathers where, which woods are left to recover, what is taken in which season, and who holds the fishing ([Community module](module:community); [Long rebuild](playbook:long-rebuild)). Deer, game and fish seasons exist so that breeding stock survives the winter, so keep them even when nobody is enforcing them, and expect the licences and the wardens to come back.
{{/if}}

## The coast

More than half of the UK's foreshore, the strip between high and low water, belongs to the Crown Estate, and gathering seaweed or shellfish there in quantity needs permission ([Crown Estate](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Crown_Estate)). Shellfish taken from water of unknown quality carry a real risk of norovirus, E. coli and shellfish poisoning, and should not be relied on without knowing the water is clean ([Paralytic shellfish poisoning](kiwix:wikipedia_en_all_maxi/Paralytic_shellfish_poisoning)).

## Fishing

In England and Wales, anyone aged 13 or over needs a rod fishing licence from the Environment Agency to fish with a rod and line in fresh water, on top of the water owner's permission; there is a coarse-fish close season on rivers from 15 March to 15 June ([rod fishing rules](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/freshwater-rod-fishing-rules); [buy a licence](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/fishing-licences/buy-a-fishing-licence)). No licence is needed for sea fishing from the shore. Scotland requires no rod licence, only the landowner's or fishery's permission. Northern Ireland requires a DAERA rod licence and a permit for the specific water.

## Hunting and shooting

The Hunting Act 2004 bans hunting wild mammals with dogs in England and Wales, though its Schedule 1 exempts hunting rabbits and rats ([Hunting Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/37/contents)). Deer may only be taken in season, with a certificated rifle of the legal calibre, under the Deer Act 1991. Game has its own seasons: pheasant from 1 October to 1 February, grouse from 12 August to 10 December. Rabbits and pigeons may be taken with the landowner's permission, subject to general wildlife protections ([Hunting Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/37/contents); [Deer Act 1991](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/contents); [GOV.UK hunting](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/hunting)). Snares, traps and poisons are tightly restricted under Part I of the Wildlife and Countryside Act, and the devolved nations have gone further, so assume a snare is unlawful unless you have checked the current law where you are ([WCA Part I](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69/part/I)). Eggs and nesting birds are protected everywhere in the UK, whatever the species ([WCA s1](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69/section/1)). Firearms, air rifles and crossbows are on [Knife and firearms law](page:knife-firearms-law).
