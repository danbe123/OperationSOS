## Plants and fungi

Picking wild fruit, foliage, fungi and flowers for your own use is not theft under the Theft Act 1968; selling what you pick is a different matter and can be ([Theft Act s4](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/60/section/4)). Uprooting any wild plant at all without the landowner's permission is an offence, and a short list of especially rare plants under Schedule 8 of the Wildlife and Countryside Act may not be picked or disturbed at all ([WCA s13](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69/section/13)). Byelaws on many nature reserves, and in places such as the New Forest and Epping Forest, forbid picking fungi entirely, whatever the general law allows. Before eating anything foraged, check it is correctly identified, cooked if it needs to be, and not from a contaminated spot — a roadside verge, a dog-walking area, or a field that has recently been sprayed ([FSA safe foraging](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/government/publications/guidance-for-safe-foraging/guidance-for-safe-foraging)).

## The coast

Most of the foreshore below the high-water mark belongs to the Crown Estate, and gathering seaweed or shellfish there in quantity needs permission. Shellfish taken from water of unknown quality carry a real risk of norovirus, E. coli and shellfish poisoning, and should not be relied on without knowing the water is clean.

## Fishing

In England and Wales, anyone aged 13 or over needs a rod fishing licence from the Environment Agency to fish with a rod and line in fresh water, on top of the water owner's permission; there is a coarse-fish close season on rivers from 15 March to 15 June ([rod fishing rules](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/freshwater-rod-fishing-rules); [buy a licence](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/fishing-licences/buy-a-fishing-licence)). No licence is needed for sea fishing from the shore. Scotland requires no rod licence, only the landowner's or fishery's permission. Northern Ireland requires a DAERA rod licence and a permit for the specific water.

## Hunting and shooting

The Hunting Act 2004 bans hunting wild mammals with dogs. Deer may only be taken in season, with a certificated rifle of the legal calibre, under the Deer Act 1991. Game has its own seasons: pheasant from 1 October to 1 February, grouse from 12 August to 10 December. Rabbits and pigeons may be taken with the landowner's permission, subject to general wildlife protections ([Hunting Act 2004](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/37/contents); [Deer Act 1991](kiwix:legislation_uk/www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/54/contents); [GOV.UK hunting](kiwix:govuk_resilience/www.gov.uk/hunting)). Snares are now banned outright in Wales (2023) and Scotland (2024). Eggs and nesting birds are protected everywhere in the UK, whatever the species.
