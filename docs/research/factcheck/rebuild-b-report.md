## Counts

**Medicine (30 claims):** VERIFIED 16 · PARTLY 8 · UNSUPPORTED 1 (honey/infant omission) · WRONG 2 · UNCHECKED (book-cited, PDFs absent) 7 — some claims carry two labels.

**Farming (30 claims):** VERIFIED 15 · PARTLY 10 · UNSUPPORTED 2 · WRONG 4.

Both pages are, overall, unusually careful — the sourcing is real, the safety warnings
are in the right places, and the two "do not do this" boxes (mouldy bread, foxglove) are
correct. The failures are concentrated in invented or mis-sourced numbers.

## The five corrections that matter most

1. **Farming, calorie base.** "2,000 kcal a day for an adult doing hard work" is a resting
   figure (NHS: 2,500 for an average man, 2,000 for an average woman, at average activity).
   Farm labour needs ~3,000. Every land figure on the page is built on this and under-plans
   food by a third — the most dangerous kind of error in a page about not starving.
2. **Farming, potato vs wheat.** The 95 GJ/ha and 31 GJ/ha figures are not in the cited
   Wikipedia *Potato* article (checked article text, raw wikitext, and a site-wide search).
   Recomputed from FAO production and area: potato ≈ 73 GJ/ha, wheat ≈ 50 GJ/ha. The
   "twelve people vs four people per acre" conclusion is wrong; potatoes lead by about
   half again, not threefold.
3. **Farming, night soil temperature.** "Held at 40 to 60 °C" sanctions a heap that will
   not kill helminth eggs. The cited source says 55 °C for two weeks or 60 °C for one week,
   or 1–2 years' storage. This is the page's one genuine pathogen hazard.
4. **Medicine, tetanus.** "Half of those who develop tetanus die even in hospital" is
   roughly five times the real figure (CDC and WHO-aligned sources: about 1 in 10 with
   treatment). Overstating it does not make the wound advice safer and costs the page
   credibility on the claims that are exactly right.
5. **Medicine, honey.** Two errors in one bullet: the burns evidence is Cochrane
   *high*-quality, not "preliminary"; and venous leg ulcers are "unclear (low quality
   evidence)", not "does not work". Add the missing hard safety line — never honey by
   mouth for an infant under twelve months.

Also worth fixing at the same time: "urine is close to sterile" (it is not, even in the
bladder), the missing ether-peroxide hazard, and the absolute lidocaine ceilings
(300 mg plain / 500 mg with adrenaline) alongside the mg/kg figures.
