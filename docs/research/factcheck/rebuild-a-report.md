## The five corrections that matter most

1. **Quench-oil fire is unaddressed** (`iron-and-tools`, hardening). The page puts "a can of oil for quenching" beside a live forge and gives no rule. Add the deep steel container with a smothering lid, enough oil that it cannot boil, no water in it, no plastic near it. This is the likeliest injury across all four pages.
2. **"Bury it in dry ash or lime" to anneal** (`iron-and-tools`). On a page whose sister guide teaches the reader to burn quicklime, red-hot steel into quicklime is a real hazard. Specify dry wood ash or vermiculite, and exclude quicklime by name.
3. **The distilling law citation is out of date, though the rule stands** (`making-things`). ALDA 1979 s.12 was repealed on 1 August 2023. Cite Finance (No. 2) Act 2023 s.82 (approval required to produce alcoholic products) and s.84, whose home-use exemption covers beer, cider and wine but expressly "not spirits" — which makes the page's point more strongly than its current wording does.
4. **Grid-parallel connection is missing entirely** (`power`). The back-feed warning covers islanded generation well, but ESQCR 2002 reg. 22 and the G98 (≤16 A/phase, notify) / G99 (>16 A/phase, apply and get approval) route are the rules that actually apply if the mains still exists. Add the bullet; correct "notifiable work under Part P" to England and Wales, new-circuit/consumer-unit scope.
5. **Forge-welding temperature reads as hotter than it should for common steel** (`iron-and-tools`). 1,400–1,500 °C is the pure-iron figure; 0.2–0.8% carbon steel welds at a bright yellow, nearer 1,300 °C. As written a reader will burn good spring steel. Split the two figures.
