## Summary

61 claims checked: **33 VERIFIED, 24 PARTLY, 4 WRONG, 0 UNSUPPORTED.**

Per kind — flood-zone: 12 V / 8 P / 0 W. footpath: 12 V / 8 P / 1 W. access-land: 9 V / 8 P / 3 W.

### The five most important corrections

1. **"Shut every gate" is wrong and is repeated three times** (`access-land.expect[s4]`,
   `access-land.approach[3]`, `footpath.approach[2]`). The Countryside Code says "leave gates
   and property as you find them or follow instructions on signs". Closing a gate a farmer has
   propped open can cut livestock off from water.
2. **The dog-and-cattle advice omits the one instruction that saves lives**
   (`footpath.avoid[1]`, `footpath.approach[2]`, `access-land.approach[3]`). The Countryside Code:
   "Let your dog off the lead if you feel threatened by livestock or horses. Do not risk getting
   hurt protecting your dog." People are killed holding on to a lead in a cattle field.
3. **`access-land.useful[1]` calls upland streams "the best raw water there is" with no treatment
   instruction.** UK hill water routinely carries Cryptosporidium and Giardia from grazing stock.
   It must say boil or filter, and never draw from below livestock or a carcass.
4. **`footpath.approach[2]` tells people to "keep to the field edge".** Where a right of way runs
   across a field, walking round the edge is trespass; the Code says stay on the marked path,
   even if it is muddy. Reverse it to "keep to the line of the path".
5. **The flood decision claims (`expect[s3]`, `avoid[1]`, `useful[0]`) do not match the warning
   levels.** Official action is level-driven: at a *flood warning* move upstairs or to higher
   ground; at a *severe flood warning* stay in a safe place, be ready to evacuate, and do as the
   emergency services say. "Moving late is what kills people" can push a household out into
   moving water — the documented killer — when staying upstairs is the safer option.

Also worth fixing, though lower risk: the zone definitions are England/Wales only (Scotland,
NI and Ireland use different schemes); flood warning areas are not the same shapes as flood
zones, so "properties inside it can usually get flood warnings" overpromises; Scotland's access
right covers "most" land, not "almost all", and camping byelaws apply around Loch Lomond;
Dartmoor wild camping is lawful only within the National Park Authority's mapped areas; and a
footpath is for feet only — bikes need a bridleway or byway.
