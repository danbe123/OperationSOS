## The five corrections that matter most
1. **cpr-adult — unless phones: "stopping at 20 minutes is reasonable" is wrong and unsafe.** No UK or wilderness authority endorses 20 minutes. The WMS/CWS remote protocol sets 30 minutes of sustained CPR, and explicitly exempts hypothermia, cold-water drowning, lightning and children. Twenty minutes will cause households to stop on people who could still be brought back. Change the figure to 30 minutes and name the exceptions in the same sentence.
2. **poisoning — unless water: do not tell people to give water or milk after a swallowed corrosive.** NHS poisoning guidance is unambiguous — "do not give the person anything to eat or drink". Dilution can provoke vomiting and a second passage of corrosive over the oesophagus. Rinse and spit only.
3. **seizures — unless phones: the "second dose after 15 minutes" of rescue medicine is a drug instruction the card cannot give.** NICE NG217 and Epilepsy Action are clear that a second dose of buccal midazolam is given only where the individual's own care plan specifies one, and then usually at 10 minutes. As written this tells a household to double a benzodiazepine dose in an unmonitored, no-ambulance setting — a respiratory-depression risk with nobody to bag the patient.
4. **drowning — unless phones: "wade with a line held from the bank" contradicts RNLI.** RNLI's instruction is "Do not go in the water yourself, even the shallows" — floodwater in particular hides drains and undercuts. Reach or throw only.
5. **Nation difference: `[[call 111]]` appears in `dehydration — if phones` and `radiation-sickness — if phones`, and 111 does not operate in Northern Ireland.** Every 111 reference needs the NI alternative (GP out-of-hours) or the box gives Northern Irish households a dead number in the one branch that assumes phones still work.

Also worth fixing on the next pass, in descending order: the hot-water-bottle wording in `hypothermia — unless phones` (NHS says never; austere practice allows wrapped heat to the trunk only); `nosebleed — unless phones` leaving a nasal pack a full 48 hours; `spinal-injury — unless phones` passing over catheterisation in one clause; `asthma-attack — unless phones` repeating the ten puffs at 15 minutes rather than Asthma + Lung UK's 10; `fever-child — unless water` implying it is fine to sponge a feverish child; and `anaphylaxis — unless phones` stating 24 hours of observation where NHS says 2–12.
