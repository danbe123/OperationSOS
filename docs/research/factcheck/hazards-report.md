### The five most important corrections

1. **`chemical.approach[1]` and `chemical.expect[5]` — decontamination method and timing are wrong and could cost lives.** UK guidance is dry decontamination first: get outer clothing off (avoid pulling it over the head), then **blot and rub** exposed skin with any dry absorbent material — face, hands, other skin, hair — then wash with plenty of water. There is no "15 to 20 minute" wash; 15 minutes is the window in which decontamination gives maximum benefit. Telling people to blot "not rub", and to spend 15–20 minutes washing before anything else, both delay and weaken the one action that matters most. (gov.uk chemical emergency guidance; Home Office/UKHSA Initial Operational Response.)
2. **`military.have[3]` and `military.useful[2]` — troops are not "under police control".** Under MACA the armed forces stay under military command and act **in support of** the civil authority at its request; service personnel have only a citizen's powers of arrest. Telling readers soldiers answer to the police invites them to treat a soldier as a police officer. (MoD MACA Factsheet 14.)
3. **`nuclear.have[1]` — sirens are not the warning at most UK nuclear sites.** Hartlepool and Sizewell DEPZ residents are warned by automated telephone/SMS and told to tune to local radio; Sellafield does have a siren. A reader waiting for a siren that does not exist will miss the warning. Say "a siren at some sites, an automated call or text at others".
4. **`military.expect[1]`/`avoid[1]` and `airport.expect[0]`/`have[1]` — the legal claims are over-broad.** Criminal trespass on military land comes from MoD byelaws (Military Lands Act 1892 s.17) or designation under s.128 SOCPA 2005, not from the land being MoD-owned. At airports the offence (Aviation Security Act 1982 ss.21C/21D) attaches to the airside **security restricted zone**, not to the landside terminal, which is normally open. Both should be stated as they are, or the advice will read as wrong to anyone who checks it.
5. **`nuclear.approach[3]` and `nuclear.useful[2]` — do not pre-decide an iodine dose, and do not send people to a cellar.** Stable iodine dosing is age-banded, printed on the pack, and confirmed by the official instruction at the time; tablets protect the thyroid only, work from 24 hours before to 2 hours after exposure and can be harmful more than 24 hours after. And a cellar is the wrong shelter room where a heavier-than-air chemical release is possible — an inner room upstairs is the safe default. (gov.uk radiation emergency guidance; HSE INDG258.)

### Also flagged

- `rail-station.useful[1]` (stations sit on embankments above floods) and `airport.useful[2]` (airfields are where aid is flown in first) are **UNSUPPORTED** and could send people towards a hazard for help that is not there. Both should be dropped or rewritten.
- `chemical.approach[4]` names an England-only hotline as if it were UK-wide.
- `rail-station.have[0]`/`have[3]` overstate what survives a power cut and how widespread defibrillators and first-aid rooms are.
- Nothing in the batch advises anything illegal; the risk is the opposite — over-stating the law (military, airport) and under-stating it is not present.
